# Fund Economics Validate Year-1 Pay, Upend Ten-Year Rows

The leader-pay-and-residual-pool test can be run on filed numbers at only three of the nine firms (Pershing Square's 2026 S-1, TCI's Companies House accounts as reported by the press, and Fundsmith LLP's accounts), partly at Akre (Form ADV ownership bands), and only by top-down modelling at the five others, but the pattern that emerges is consistent: **every Year-1 row in the original table is plausible and none is refuted**, while **the years-1–10 rows are too low at Pershing, TCI, Akre and Fundsmith for anyone who reaches partner** (Pershing's non-Ackman investment professionals implied at **≈$14–20M a year in 2023–25**, TCI's 16 equity partners at a filed **$10.17M average**, Fundsmith's non-Smith members at **≈£2.7M**, Akre's two newest partners holding **10–25% stakes** in a firm with a ~$115M partner pool) and simultaneously too high for the majority of analysts who leave before partnership, so those rows are survivorship-biased rather than simply wrong. The tenure column errs in both directions: Pershing's "4–6 years" is contradicted by an observed **≈11-year average** and a vesting schedule that back-loads half of equity into years 8–10, Fundsmith's "6–10 years" sits below the **13–16 years** of every named core analyst, while Coatue's "2–4 years" is the best-supported tenure claim in the table. The Rich List figures the user asked us to subtract from fees cannot be subtracted: Institutional Investor counts gains on the founder's own capital, which is why **Chris Hohn was credited with $4.9B for 2025 while his UK entity paid him an $81.6M dividend**, and why **roughly half of Bill Ackman's $1.4B for 2020 was the mark-to-market on his own PSH shares**. The arithmetic shows that at D1, Coatue, Valley Forge and Himalaya the management fee alone funds the claimed pay ladder two to eight times over, so what actually constrains realised analyst pay at these firms is the high-water-mark cycle (no incentive fees at D1 from 2022 to mid-2024, at Coatue in 2018 and 2022–23, at Pershing in 2016–18 and 2022, at Lone Pine on a half-fee basis from 2023) and the founder's structural profit share, not the size of the pool.

## Rich List dollars are not fee dollars, and researchers worked from excerpts

Two caveats govern everything below. First, Institutional Investor's Rich List "counts an individual's gains on their own capital invested in their funds plus their share of the fees," and managers below their high-water mark still get credit for personal-capital gains because the list is a one-year snapshot ([II 25th Rich List](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/hedge-funds/rich-list-institutional-investors-25th-annual-ranking-highest-earning-hedge)). II itself said of Hohn's 2023 figure that "most of [his] earnings stemmed from gains generated by his own capital invested in the fund. He received a small amount of fees as TCI had to hit its high-water mark before it could charge a performance fee" ([II 23rd Rich List](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2d0quhk4ghsahyyia26m8/corner-office/the-23nd-annual-ranking-of-the-highest-earning-hedge-fund-managers)). Forbes' older annual-earnings lists deducted firm operating costs and profit splits but still included "the personal gain or loss of each manager's interest in their funds," and Forbes has not clearly published an annual hedge-fund earnings list since 2019 ([Forbes 2019](https://www.forbes.com/sites/nathanvardi/2019/03/20/the-highest-earning-hedge-fund-managers-and-traders/)). Bloomberg's and II's numbers for the same year disagree (Laffont $1.7B vs $1.6B for 2020; Ackman $1.3B vs $1.4B) ([Bloomberg list summary](https://www.business-powerhouse.com/best-paid-hedge-fund-managers-2020); [II 20th Rich List](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/b1qmsgpxhz0lpt/The-20th-Annual-Rich-List-the-Definitive-Ranking-of-What-Hedge-Fund-Managers-Earned-in-2020)). A founder can therefore "earn" billions in a year when the firm's incentive-fee pool, and hence the staff bonus pool, is near zero, and the Rich List figure must be treated as an upper bound on fee take, never as money diverted from staff. Where a filed number exists (TCI dividend, Fundsmith largest-member share, Pershing LTIP distribution) it is a floor on the leader's firm-derived take, because profits retained inside founder-owned entities still belong to the founder.

Second, the research environment blocked direct access to Companies House, the SEC's EDGAR and IAPD systems, and nearly every news domain (Bloomberg, FT, Institutional Investor, Hedgeweek, Citywire, Forbes, Wikipedia), and the web-search budget was exhausted before every gap could be closed. Every figure in this report therefore comes from search-engine excerpts of press coverage of the filings, not from the filed PDFs; where excerpts conflict (TCI's prior-year operating profit of "$456M" per Hedgeweek versus "≈$200M" PBT per Bloomberg for the same year; Fundsmith's £185.7M Mauritius charge attributed to either FY2023 or FY2024) the conflict is flagged rather than resolved. Numbers are labelled REPORTED (traceable to a cited source) or ESTIMATED (the researchers' arithmetic with stated assumptions) throughout. The compensation benchmarks used for comparison are thin: the only recruiter survey that segmented by fund size put the average entry-level analyst at a >$4B fund at $335K (2013 pay), $372K (2014) and $360K (2015) ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2013/11/01/this-industry-has-an-entry-level-salary-of-335000.html); [HFR/Glocap release](https://hfr-wp-s3.s3.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/18172813/pr_20151104-1.pdf)), SumZero's 2012–16 self-reported medians were $220K for 3–5 years' experience and $270K for 5–7 ([SumZero 2017 report](https://sumzero.s3.amazonaws.com/public/uploads/specialpublications/SumZero2017CompensationReport_2ed40648db.pdf)), Odyssey's all-level hedge-fund average was $450K–$520K in 2017–18 ([Odyssey](https://www.odysseysearchpartners.com/news/hedge-fund-employees-expect-fatter-paychecks-in-beleaguered-year/)), eFinancialCareers' 2023 average was $487K ([eFC 2024 PDF](https://emails.efinancialcareers.com/assets/responsysimages/content/dhigroup/COMPENSATIONREPORT2024-prf4.pdf)), and anything above $1M for an analyst appears only in anonymous forum posts about Tiger Cubs.

## TCI: partners average $10M in a year Hohn's dividend fell to $82M

TCI is the firm with the widest gap between the leader's reported earnings and any filed payment to him, and the only one with a filed partner-pool number. Hohn's UK-filed dividends, drawn from press coverage of Companies House accounts for TCI Fund Management (UK) Ltd, run: FY to Feb 2016 nil (the directors "did not receive a dividend payout the previous year"); FY Feb 2017 **>$360M** to two directors, "almost all" to Hohn ([Bloomberg, Dec 2017](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2017-12-07/hedge-fund-tci-is-said-to-pay-360-million-dividend-to-founder)); FY Feb 2018 $274M and FY Feb 2019 £200M/$278M ([CEO Magazine](https://www.theceomagazine.com/business/management-leadership/hedge-fund-chris-hohn/)); FY Feb 2020 **$479M**, then "the UK's biggest annual pay packet" ([Wikipedia: Chris Hohn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Hohn)); FY Feb 2021 ≈$152M (low confidence, inferred from a City AM comparison); FY Feb 2022 **£575M/$690M**, a record that City AM headlined "won't go in his pocket" because it was earmarked for philanthropy ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2022/12/01/chris-hohn-rishi-sunak-old-boss-record-payday-dividend/); [City AM](https://www.cityam.com/boss-at-mayfair-hedgefund-tci-gets-575m-dividend-but-it-wont-go-in-his-pocket/)); FY Feb 2023 £276M/$346M against profit before tax of only ≈$200M, so partly paid from reserves ([Bloomberg, Dec 2023](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-12-06/hedge-fund-payout-to-hohn-halves-to-346-million-as-profits-dip)); FY Mar 2024 **£42M/$53M** as operating profit fell to $229M ([Hedgeweek](https://www.hedgeweek.com/tci-fund-founder-takes-233m-pay-cut/)); and FY Mar 2025 **£60M/$81.6M** "as TCI revenue and charitable giving surge" ([Hedgeweek](https://www.hedgeweek.com/hohn-takes-81m-dividend-as-tci-revenue-and-charitable-giving-surge/); [City AM](https://www.cityam.com/billionaire-hohn-handed-60m-dividend-and-ramps-up-philanthropy/)). The ten-year dividend sum is roughly $2.7B. Institutional Investor's series tells a different story: **$365M (2016), $775M (2017), rank 21 with no figure retrieved (2018), $1.8B tied first (2019), on the list but figure not retrieved (2020), $2.2B (2021), off the list after an 18% loss (2022), $2.9B first (2023), $1.3B tied eighth (2024) and a record $4.9B first (2025)** ([II "The Rich List"](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/b18dwjvpw2n9v9/the-rich-list); [II/GlobeNewswire 2020](https://www.globenewswire.com/en/news-release/2020/03/26/2006868/0/en/Christopher-Hohn-of-TCI-Fund-Management-and-James-Simons-of-Renaissance-Technologies-Top-Institutional-Investor-s-Rich-List.html); [II 22nd](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bstpd4t08pc8xwj4we80/corner-office/the-rich-list-the-22nd-annual-ranking-of-the-highest-earning-hedge-fund-managers); [II 23rd](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2d0quhk4ghsahyyia26m8/corner-office/the-23nd-annual-ranking-of-the-highest-earning-hedge-fund-managers); [II 24th](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2ekchq8rll8mbbn2glq80/hedge-funds/the-rich-list-the-24th-annual-ranking-of-the-highest-earning-hedge-fund-managers); [II 25th](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/hedge-funds/rich-list-institutional-investors-25th-annual-ranking-highest-earning-hedge)). In 2016 the two series were nearly identical ($365M vs ≈$364M), by 2023–24 the UK dividend was 2–6% of the II figure. The divergence is explained by own-capital gains on a personal fund stake already put at "no less than $1.15 billion" in the 2014 divorce judgment ([Judiciary.uk](https://www.judiciary.uk/wp-content/uploads/2014/12/hohn-judgment.pdf)) and by profit retention: an aggregator shows the UK company's net worth rising from ≈£1.44B (2020) to ≈£3.66B (2024) with only ≈£8.8M of cash, consistent with profits being reinvested in the fund rather than distributed ([North Data](https://www.northdata.com/TCI%20Fund%20Management%20(UK)%20Ltd%C2%B7,%20London/Companies%20House%2004706248)).

The fund side is well documented. TCI's net returns were **+13.5% (2016), +29% (2017), +0.9% (2018), +41% (2019), +14% (2020), +23.3% (2021), −18% (2022), +33% (2023), +15% (2024) and +27–27.8% (2025)** ([Forbes 2017](https://www.forbes.com/pictures/mdg45ekihf/16-christopher-hohn/); [Yahoo/Insider Monkey](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/tci-fund-18-annual-returns-194511527.html); [Bloomberg, Jan 2024](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-01-05/chris-hohn-s-hedge-fund-bounces-back-with-33-gain-in-2023); [Forbes, Jan 2026](https://www.forbes.com/sites/hanktucker/2026/01/18/hedge-fund-billionaire-chris-hohns-tci-profited-by-a-record-189-billion-in-2025/)), and LCH net investor gains were $8.4B (2019), $4.2B (2020), $9.5B (2021), roughly −$8B (2022), $12.9B (2023), ≈$8.2B derived (2024) and a record **$18.9B (2025)**, with AUM at $30B in January 2020, $35B in March 2021 and **$77.1B at end-2025** ([Forbes, Jan 2020](https://www.forbes.com/sites/nathanvardi/2020/01/20/billionaire-chris-hohn-becomes-a-hedge-fund-giant/); [II](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/chris-hohns-tci-tops-hedge-fund-gains-2025)). Fee terms are the weak link: at launch TCI charged a 1.5% management fee, a third of which went to the children's charity, and incentive fees of 13–17% depending on three- to five-year lock-ups, but it stopped contractual charity donations after 2008–09 and added lower-fee, more liquid classes in 2012 ([II, "Hedge Fund Manager Like No Other"](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bstnug68kijmzi8f50cg/culture/chris-hohn-is-a-hedge-fund-manager-like-no-other); [II 2012](https://institutionalinvestor.com/article/b18bkdwzzr6qqd/chris-hohns-tci-opens-to-new-investment-adds-more-liquid-share-classes)); current rates are private.

The single filed anchor is that Companies House accounts for TCI Holdings Limited for the year to March 2025 show **16 key management personnel, "equity partners of the limited liability partnership subsidiaries," paid an average $10.17M (£7.6M)**, that "only around half of the partners are actual portfolio managers," that headcount is about 50 but obscured by multiple entities, and that the accounts capture only three months of calendar 2025, so full-year 2025 averages "could be higher" ([eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.fi/news/tci-partner-pay-2025)). That ≈$163M pool is only ≈7% of the estimated 2024 gross fees and ≈16% of the notional residual after subtracting Hohn's $1.3B II figure and ≈$20–45M of estimated non-partner overhead, which confirms that the gross-fee estimate is too high, that profits are being retained, or both; the disclosed pool, not the notional residual, is the number to use. Scaling that pool to other years is unsourced, but if it tracks fees the 2025 pool would be ≈$300–400M (≈$20–25M per partner) and a 2022-type loss year perhaps $30–60M (≈$2–4M per partner, funded from management fees). Non-partner pay is known only from one anonymous forum claim of $400–500K for London associates ([Wall Street Oasis](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/hedge-fund/tci-process-and-work-experience)). Tenure is bimodal: current partners Ben Walker (joined January 2011) and Jonathan Amouyal (March 2012) have 14–15 years, and TCI Fund Services LLP's eight partners have "mostly" been there almost 20 years ([MarketScreener](https://ca.marketscreener.com/insider/BEN-WALKER-A04YRP/); [Cellnex](https://www.cellnex.com/investor-relations/corporate-governance/jonathan-amouyal/); [eFC](https://www.efinancialcareers.ch/en/news/tci-partner-pay-2025)), while documented leavers stayed three to six years (Sunak 2006–09, Amin 2004–09, Degorce 2003–09, Allen 2009–15) ([Hedgeweek](https://hedgeweek.com/news/ex-tci-partner-edgar-allens-hedge-fund-hits-30-returns); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2022/12/01/chris-hohn-rishi-sunak-old-boss-record-payday-dividend/)).

**Verdict on the TCI row (6–8 yrs; $0.5–1M; $1–2M; $2–5M).** Year 1 at $0.5–1M is plausible, consistent with the unverified $400–500K associate figure and with a firm where non-partners are a small minority of comp, but no filed source supports it. Years 1–5 at $1–2M is plausible and unverifiable. Years 1–10 at $2–5M is **too low** for anyone who reaches the partnership, where the filed average was $10.2M in a middling +15% year, and **too high** for staff who leave at the three-to-six-year mark that every documented departure shows. Tenure of 6–8 years is plausible as a blend of a 3–6-year leaver mode and a 14–20-year partner mode, but no published statistic supports it. The structural point is that TCI is the most top-heavy firm in the set: Hohn's II earnings exceeded the entire 16-partner pool by about eight times in 2024, and "rewarding them as a team," as II described the post-2008 approach ([II Alpha 2015 PDF](https://www.citadel.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/II-Alpha-Hedge-Fund-Report-Card-2015-Citadel.pdf)), means the per-head figure is a pool average, not an individual formula.

## Pershing Square: an audited S-1 shows partners split $339–459M a year

Pershing Square is the one firm where the whole waterfall is audited, because the management company filed an S-1 and listed on April 29, 2026 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/29/bill-ackmans-5-billion-pershing-square-ipo-to-start-trading-testing-berkshire-style-vision.html)). Ackman's reported earnings series is sparse in the loss years: no Rich List entry was found for 2016–2018 (the S-1 describes "a period of poor investment performance" from August 2015 to December 2017 and no performance fee accrued for 2018) ([S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2026053/000114036126008560/ny20040230x14_s1.htm); [PSH 2019 Interim](https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2019/08/15175158/PSH-2019-Interim-Financial-Statements.pdf)); 2019 not retrieved; **2020 $1.4B (II, #10) or $1.3B (Bloomberg, #9)**, of which II said $720M was the gain on his 23% of PSH's shares alone ([II 20th Rich List](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/b1qmsgpxhz0lpt/The-20th-Annual-Rich-List-the-Definitive-Ranking-of-What-Hedge-Fund-Managers-Earned-in-2020); [II, "billion-dollar year"](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/b1pzgx69sxhstk/bill-ackman-s-billion-dollar-year); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2021-02-10/chase-coleman-leads-23-billion-payday-for-15-hedge-fund-earners)); 2021 not in the top ten and not found; 2022 "failed to crack the top 15" ([FA-Mag](https://www.fa-mag.com/news/bill-ackman-rockets-up-best-paid-hedge-fund-list-by-doing-very-little-77007.html)); **2023 $610M (Bloomberg, #7)**, "roughly a third" from PSH share-price gains ([Bloomberg, Feb 2024](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-02-20/richest-hedge-fund-managers-led-by-izzy-englander-ken-griffin)); **2024 $550M (II, #15)**; **2025 $1.1B (II, #10)** ([II 24th](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2ekchq8rll8mbbn2glq80/hedge-funds/the-rich-list-the-24th-annual-ranking-of-the-highest-earning-hedge-fund-managers); [II 25th](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/hedge-funds/rich-list-institutional-investors-25th-annual-ranking-highest-earning-hedge)). The filed counterpart for 2025 is that **"distributions from CompCo under the LTIP in respect of 2025 were $139,698,760 for Ackman, $28,261,234 for Israel and $11,401,856 for Hakim"** ([S-1/A](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002026053/000114036126016419/ny20040230x28_s1a.htm)), so even in a $1.1B Rich List year his cash from the firm's incentive plan was ≈13% of the headline.

PSH's terms are a 1.5% management fee on fee-paying NAV and a 16% performance fee above a high-water mark, less an "additional reduction" of 20% of fees earned on other Pershing funds, which until February 2024 was absorbed by a $120M IPO-cost offset, so PSH effectively paid the full 16% through 2023 ([PSH June 2020 Interim](https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2020/08/28125504/Pershing-Square-Holdings-Ltd.-June-2020-Interim.pdf); [BusinessWire, Feb 2024](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240207306652/en/Pershing-Square-Holdings-Ltd.-%E2%80%9CPSH%E2%80%9D-Announces-Amendments-to-its-Investment-Management-Agreement-Which-Will-Reduce-Fees-Paid-by-PSH)). PSH NAV returns were **+58.1% (2019), +70.2% (2020), +26.9% (2021), +26.7% (2023), +10.2% (2024) and +20.9% (2025)**, with 2016–18 and 2022 unretrieved from a citable source but known to be loss years ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/bill-ackman-pershing-square-2019-performance-130053673.html); [PSH 2021 Annual Report](https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2022/03/29140526/Pershing-Square-Holdings-Ltd.-2021-Annual-Report.pdf); [Reuters/Investing.com](https://investing.com/news/stock-market-news/ackmans-hedge-fund-pershing-square-gains-267-in-2023--investor-update-3265809)). PSH year-end AUM went $7.1B (2019), $11.2B (2020), $14.4B (2021), $12.2B (2022), $14.4B (2023), $15.3B (2024), **$18.7B (2025)**, with firm-wide AUM of $30.7B (fee-paying $20.7B) at end-2025 after the private funds shrank to ~$1.6B following the 2018 redemption wave ([PS Inc. 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002026053/000119312526257657/ps-ex99_1.htm); [Bloomberg, Apr 2018](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2018-04-05/large-investors-said-to-have-pulled-from-pershing-square-funds)). Two features stand out. Partners were paid $339M in 2024 while the firm ran an operating loss, so the pool is effectively a revenue share rather than a profit share, and Israel's $28.3M LTIP distribution against Hakim's $11.4M shows a steep skew (the CIO plausibly earns two to three times the average, the newest member, Manning Feng who joined in 2021, may not yet hold a profits interest). Extending backwards with the half-year PSH fees that were retrievable ($26.8M for H1 2018, $27.1M for H1 2019, $39.5M for H1 2020, with $290M of performance fee accrued by June 2020) ([PSH June 2020 Interim](https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2020/08/28125504/Pershing-Square-Holdings-Ltd.-June-2020-Interim.pdf)), the researchers' low-confidence model puts the non-Ackman investment-professional pool at ≈$11–27M in the zero-incentive years 2016–2018 (≈$1.5–3.6M per head), ≈$150–190M in 2020–21 (≈$20–25M per head) and ≈$36M in 2022, for a ten-year average of ≈$7–12M per head depending on whether Ackman's pre-2024 share was 55% or 75%. Tenure is the strongest fact: Israel joined in 2009, Hakim 2012, Massaro 2013, Korn 2014, Alamanda and Qayyum 2017, Feng 2021, an average of ≈11 years, Ackman says the firm has had no "undesired departure," and M Units vest 6.25% a year in years 1–4, 8.33% in years 5–7 and 16.67% in years 8–10 ([SPARC S-1/A](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1895582/000119312523234569/d305814ds1a.htm); [Fortune, Aug 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/08/08/bill-ackmans-35-billion-hedge-fund-has-under-50-employees-and-all-of-them-from-janitors-to-receptionists-own-millions-in-company-stock/); [S-1/A](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002026053/000114036126016419/ny20040230x28_s1a.htm)).

**Verdict on the Pershing row (4–6 yrs; $0.4–0.8M; $0.8–2M; $2–5M).** Year 1 at $0.4–0.8M is **plausible**: a first-year analyst has no profits interest and is paid from a line that averages ≈$0.4–0.6M per non-partner head across all functions. Years 1–5 at $0.8–2M is **too low for Pershing specifically** in most cohorts, because Korn, Alamanda and Qayyum carried partner titles within six to nine years and even a 2–3% slice of a $160–225M non-Ackman pool is $3–7M in a good year, though a cohort whose first five years spanned 2016–18 or 2022 would land near the claim. Years 1–10 at $2–5M is **too low** against ≈$14–20M per non-Ackman investment professional in 2023–25 and ≈$7–12M averaged over the decade, with the caveat that CFO, CLO and Vice Chairman also draw on the pool and the skew toward Israel and Hakim is steep. Tenure of 4–6 years is **contradicted**: the observed average is ≈11 years and the equity design makes ten-year tenure the norm.

## Lone Pine: a half-fee high-water mark compresses a pool built for 30 analysts

## Valley Forge and Himalaya: founder-owned GPs make later-year pay a choice, not a constraint

Neither Dev Kantesaria nor Li Lu has a credible published earnings or net-worth figure; sites quoting ">$4B" for Kantesaria are relabelling the fund's 13F value as personal wealth, and Li Lu's "net worth" pages range from $300M to $5B with no method ([TipRanks](https://www.tipranks.com/experts/hedge-funds/dev-kantesaria); [Fiscal.ai](https://fiscal.ai/blog/li-lu-the-chinese-warren-buffett/)). Both firms are founder-controlled: Kantesaria "through direct and indirect ownership, is Valley Forge's principal owner" and Li Lu holds more than 75% of Himalaya ([HedgeFundDB Valley Forge](https://hedgefunddb.com/Home/FundDetails/801-110956/VALLEY-FORGE-CAPITAL-MANAGEMENT--LP); [PrivateFundData Himalaya](https://privatefunddata.com/fund-companies/himalaya-capital-management-llc/)), so the founder's take is simply gross fees minus overhead minus whatever the founder chooses to pay a handful of colleagues.

Valley Forge's fee terms are filed: "a monthly management fee, generally set at 1.00% annualized" plus "a performance allocation equal to 20% of the annual increase" in each capital account with a loss-carryforward high-water mark, on a main fund plus a sub-advised vehicle run by a large institution ([SEC IAPD brochure](https://files.adviserinfo.sec.gov/IAPD/Content/Common/crd_iapd_Brochure.aspx?BRCHR_VRSN_ID=900040)). Assets went from ≈$450–500M in 2019 to $2.1B by Q1 2021, $2.6B at end-2021, $2.1B in Q3 2022, $3.5B at end-2023, **$4.54B at end-2024** and ≈$4.4–4.45B of regulatory AUM with **nine employees** in the 2025 filing ([Hedgeweek, Sep 2019](https://www.hedgeweek.com/2019/09/19/278823/valley-forge-capital-management-surpasses-usd500m-aum); [13f.info](https://13f.info/manager/0001697868-valley-forge-capital-management-lp); [24/7 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/investing/2025/03/17/hedge-fund-founder-puts-his-faith-in-these-3-stocks-to-deliver-market-beating-returns/); [PrivateFundData](https://privatefunddata.com/fund-companies/valley-forge-capital-management-lp/)). Returns are not public beyond a since-inception ≈14.7–15% net annualised versus ≈10% for the S&P 500 from 2007 to May 2025, and 13F-derived year points of +34.3% (2017), −5.6% (2018) and +51.2% (2019) ([Yahoo/Insider Monkey](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/only-finance-stocks-matter-valley-002115423.html)). In an interview recorded in late 2022 Kantesaria said the investment team was two people, himself and Principal Benjamin Silverman, that they hoped "to add an analyst or two over the following couple of years," and that he has "always preferred a small investment team" ([Good Investing Talks](https://www.good-investing.net/2023/01/26/dev-kantesaria-what-is-your-formula-for-quality-investing/)). The researchers' estimated gross fees (reconstructed year returns, fee-paying AUM equal to 13F value) are ≈$3M (2016), $12M (2017), $3M (2018), $40M (2019), $35–50M (2020), $45–50M (2021), $22M (2022, management fee only), **$130–160M (2023), $190–240M (2024)** and $45–55M (2025), with non-investment overhead of ≈$3–6M; the implied founder take is ≈$15M in 2022 and ≈$180–230M in 2024. A $0.3–0.6M analyst is 1–3% of the 2022 residual and under 0.5% of the 2024 residual, and even a 10% share of residual revenue to one to three people would be $5–15M a year, so the gap between what could be paid and what benchmarks suggest is paid ($0.3–1.5M) is the whole uncertainty.

Himalaya's structure is the Buffett partnership: no management fee and a 25% incentive allocation "after recovery of certain prior losses and a 6% per annum (compounded annually) preferred return" ([HedgeFundDB Himalaya](https://hedgefunddb.com/Home/FundDetails/801-72763/HIMALAYA-CAPITAL-MANAGEMENT-LLC); [Market Folly](https://www.marketfolly.com/2018/09/li-lu-himalaya-capital-interview-chinas.html)). Regulatory AUM was **$4.36B (May 2017), ≈$14.2B (2023), $17.2B (December 2024) and $21.4B (March 2026)**, with 19–21 employees of whom aggregators class ≈15 as investment staff ([WhaleWisdom ADV 2017](https://whalewisdom.com/form_adv/view/507417); [Granitefirm](https://www.granitefirm.com/blog/us/2025/09/04/li-lu-himalaya-capital/); [SmartAdvisorMatch](https://smartadvisormatch.com/advisor-firm-network/washington/himalaya-capital-157594); [aum13f](https://aum13f.com/firm/himalaya-capital-management-llc)). Master-fund Form D cumulative sales rose only from $7.5B (September 2021) to $9.2B (September 2026), so most AUM growth since 2021 was performance ([CapEdge](https://capedge.com/company/1576745/himalaya-capital-investors-lp)). The widely repeated 30% CAGR is explicitly "a rumor" ([Fiscal.ai](https://fiscal.ai/blog/li-lu-the-chinese-warren-buffett/)), and Munger's statement that the $88M he gave Li in 2004 had become "four or five times that" by 2023 implies roughly 8% net compounding on that capital unless he withdrew along the way, which is the strongest single reason to doubt the lore ([Yahoo/Business Insider](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/charlie-munger-invested-nearly-90-191130997.html)). Because the 6% hurdle compounds while underwater, one bad year suppresses the next one or two years' incentive entirely, so the firm's revenue is lumpy: the researchers' scenarios put incentive revenue at 1.0–4.0% of AUM in positive years (≈$100–760M on 2023–25 assets) and zero in 2018- and 2022-type years, with a ten-year total more likely in the ≈$1–2B range than the $3.5B high case. Non-investment overhead of ≈$5–10M and a 15-person investment pool of $6–28M (at $0.4–2.0M a head) would be 6–28% of revenue in a normal year, but in a zero-incentive year the founder must carry $15–40M of costs from retained capital, which is a structural reason to keep base pay modest and bonuses discretionary. Himalaya's only visible opening (2024–26) is for a generalist with "at least five years of equity research experience" willing to work in Asia, so its "Year 1" hire is mid-career ([AAAIM job PDF](https://aaaim.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/job_Research_Analyst.pdf)); the firm grew from one person in 1997 to ≈21 by 2026, under one hire a year ([Wikipedia: Himalaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalaya_Capital)).

**Verdict on the Valley Forge/Himalaya row ("long but rarely hire"; $0.3–0.6M; highly uncertain; highly uncertain).** Year 1 at $0.3–0.6M is **plausible**, matching forum ranges of $300–400K starting pay at top long-only firms and $500–700K post-MBA at $1–20B boutiques with "just a handful of analysts" ([WSO long-only comp](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/hedge-fund/long-only-analyst-compensation)) and trivially affordable at both firms. The "highly uncertain" entries for later years are **confirmed as the honest answer**: revenue is 60–100% performance-based and accrues to founder-owned GPs, no source indicates either founder shares carry, and career guides stress that at single-manager shops "there is no formula, and the founder decides bonuses each year out of fee income" ([Quantt](https://www.quantt.co.uk/resources/hedge-fund-manager-salary-guide)). "Rarely hire" is **confirmed** by a two-person investment team at a $2B+ firm and fewer than one hire a year over 27 years.

The fund side needs no estimation: Fundsmith Equity's T Acc class returned **+28.2%, +22.0%, +2.2%, +25.6%, +18.3%, +22.1%, −13.8%, +12.4%, +8.9% and +0.8%** for 2016–2025, trailing MSCI World's +12.8% in 2025 ([Fundsmith factsheet](https://www.fundsmith.co.uk/factsheet/); [2025 annual letter](https://www.fundsmith.co.uk/media/4hcfd1pg/2025-fef-annual-letter-web.pdf)); the fund grew from £9.1B (end-2016) to £18.8B (end-2019), £28.1B (November 2021) and a peak of £26–29B in 2022, then shrank to £22.5–23.3B (end-2024) and **£15.3B (end-2025)** after £7.9B of outflows in a year ([IT Investor](https://www.itinvestor.co.uk/2020/10/10-years-of-fundsmith-equity/); [Trustnet](https://www.trustnet.com/news/13280517/fundsmith-grows-by-5bn-in-2021--which-other-funds-have-jumped-in-size-this-year); [Fidelity](https://www.fidelity.co.uk/markets-insights/investing-ideas/funds/important-changes-at-fundsmith-equity/)); and it charges a flat 0.9% (I class) or 1.0% (T class) with no performance fee ([Fundsmith Oct 2021](https://www.fundsmith.co.uk/media/upmnrw22/october.pdf)). The core investment team is five people, Smith, Robins (co-founder, 2010), Daniel Washburn (2011), Tom Boles (2013) and Yiktat Fung (2020), all generalists, per a March 2024 Morningstar report ([Morningstar via Fundsmith](https://www.fundsmith.eu/media/mgjh0rkx/morningstar-report-fundsmith-equity-fund-sicav-march-2024.pdf)); firm-wide investment professionals including the Smithson and (pre-2022) FEET teams were roughly 9–12.

## Four forces, not pool size, set what an analyst actually takes home

Across the nine firms the arithmetic keeps producing the same result: the pool is never the binding constraint. D1's management fee alone covered its ladder six times over in its worst years, Coatue's two to eight times, Valley Forge and Himalaya's by a wider margin, and even Pershing paid partners $339M in a year the manager ran an operating loss. What moves realised pay is, first, **the high-water-mark cycle**. Incentive fees were zero or near zero at Pershing in 2016–18 and 2022, at Coatue in 2018 and 2022–23, at D1 from 2022 to mid-2024, at Lone Pine in 2021–22 and then halved from 2023, at TCI in 2022 with a haircut into 2023, and at Himalaya in any year the fund sat below its loss carryforward plus compounding 6% hurdle. Glocap's Anthony Keizner described the mechanism a decade ago: "Even without incentive fees, the GPs of firms this year will likely have to dip into management fees in order to compensate their teams," which is why only 53% of funds being at their high-water mark in 2015 coincided with deferred compensation and mandatory bonus reinvestment spreading ([HFR/Glocap release](https://hfr-wp-s3.s3.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/18172813/pr_20151104-1.pdf)). Lone Pine made the link explicit when it adopted a half-fee-below-HWM structure "to allow a fund to retain talent by being able to pay top employees" ([II 2024](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2dxayili734jtzbb86ark/hedge-funds/lone-pine-makes-back-earlier-losses)). A cohort that joined any of the Tiger Cubs in 2021 therefore lived through two to three bonus cycles funded from management fees, and its five-year average sits at the bottom of every range in the table; a 2019 or 2025 cohort sits at or above the top. Multi-year averages without a cohort label are not meaningful.

Second, **the founder's profit share is structural, not discretionary**. Pershing retains the performance fee on the first five points of net return before anything reaches CompCo, and Ackman then owns 51.5% of CompCo ([S-1/A](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002026053/000114036126016419/ny20040230x28_s1a.htm)); Fundsmith's founding members take "a pre-determined, fixed share of the firm's profit" and the Mauritius charge takes two-thirds of revenue off the table before the LLP pool is even formed ([Fundsmith Remuneration Policy](https://www.fundsmith.com/remuneration-policy/)); Akre's ownership bands put 30–60% with the founder's trusts; Hohn's £575M dividend in FY2022 was about 3.5 times the entire 16-partner pool of the only year it was disclosed ([eFC](https://www.efinancialcareers.ch/en/news/tci-partner-pay-2025)). The comparison with listed managers is stark: Man Group's total compensation ratio was 47–48% of revenue in 2024–25 and Och-Ziff's bonus expense ran ≈20% of revenue with salaries at ≈18% of management fees ([Man Group FY2025](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/man-group--emg/financial-results-for-the-year-ended-2025/9447616); [Och-Ziff 8-K FY2015](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001403256/000140325616000184/ozm-eprxex99x4q2015.htm)), whereas at the founder-owned single managers the staff pool is a residual of a residual, typically well below 25–35% of revenue, and the forum model in which the founder keeps all management-fee profit and 70% of incentive profit ([WSO $500M model](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/hedge-fund/500m-single-manager-hf-pl-and-comp)) is consistent with every filed data point.

Third, **the pool is skewed to a tiny partner class**, which is why "average per investment professional" misleads. TCI's $163M went to 16 people out of ≈50, half of them not even PMs; Pershing's CIO drew $28.3M from the LTIP against the President's $11.4M; Fundsmith's Robins plausibly out-earned the other eight or nine investment professionals combined; Akre's pool went to two or three partners while two junior analysts were salaried; forum posters describe Tiger Cub economics as "controlled by 1–3 people" who pay "2–4%" of attributed P&L to an analyst ([WSO Tiger cub pay 2022](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/hedge-fund/top-tiger-cub-pay-2022)). The right mental model is a step function: salary plus a capped discretionary bonus for the first three to six years, then either exit or a jump of five to twenty times on becoming a partner, point-holder or stake-owner, as Millette's move from salaried analyst to 10–25% owner in year six illustrates.

Fourth, **survivorship bias infects every multi-year average**. Forum reports that some Tiger Cub analyst programs ask "the vast majority of analysts" to leave after two to three years ([WSO](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/hedge-fund/top-hedge-funds-analyst-compensation)), the Coatue exodus, D1's team changes and Lone Pine's 2022–25 departures all point to a bimodal distribution in which most leave within two to five years and a minority stay a decade or more; the ten-year averages in the table describe only that minority, and the named long tenures (Israel 17 years, Walker 15, Washburn 15, Neff 17, Granat ≈20) are the survivors. Nothing in the public record allows the two populations to be weighted, so the honest ten-year figure is a conditional one: "for an analyst retained to year ten."

The data gaps are extensive and should be stated plainly. The researchers could not open Companies House, SEC EDGAR or IAPD, or most news sites directly, and relied on search-result excerpts of press coverage of those filings; the web-search budget ran out before every planned query could be run. Consequently: TCI's turnover, staff costs, headcount and LLP member counts for every year, and its FY2016 and FY2021 dividends, are unverified, and the entity behind each press figure is inconsistent; Pershing's PSH fee line items for 2016–2022 and its pre-2024 ownership split were not retrieved, so the 2016–2023 waterfall is low-confidence; Form ADV Item 5.B investment-headcount and Schedule A ownership were unobtainable for every firm except via dated aggregator snapshots, so every per-head figure at D1, Coatue, Lone Pine and Soroban rests on an assumed team size; no calendar-year returns exist publicly for Valley Forge or Himalaya and most years are missing for Soroban and Lone Pine; Coatue's investment-professional count and its hedge-fund and growth-fund fee rates are unknown; Akre's fund management-fee rate was taken from prior knowledge; Fundsmith's Mauritius company publishes nothing, so Smith's total take is a press estimate; the Soroban Tax Court decision, which likely contains partner-level dollar figures for 2016–17, was not read; Rich List figures are missing for Sundheim (2018, 2022–23), Laffont (2016), Hohn (2018, 2020 amounts), Ackman (2019, 2021) and Mandel (most years), and no source separates own-capital gains from fee share for any of them. Most fundamentally, **no press article, survey or filing reports analyst-level compensation at any of the nine firms for any year 2016–2025**; every analyst-level number in this report is an affordability bound, a forum anecdote, or a recruiter benchmark for a broader population, and the firm-specific rows of the table remain, at best, consistent with the economics rather than confirmed by them.

## Conclusion

The exercise changes how the original table should be read rather than overturning it. Its junior rows survive contact with every filed number and benchmark, but its ten-year rows at the four firms with real disclosure are systematically low by a factor of three to five for the people who stay, and the tenure column is wrong at the two firms with the best records (Pershing and Fundsmith) in the direction of understating how long people stay, while at the Tiger Cubs the evidence suggests most analysts never reach the years the ten-year column describes. A better table would carry three rows per firm instead of one: a cohort-dated five-year figure (2019 entrants versus 2021 entrants differ by a factor of two at D1 or Coatue), a partner-conditional ten-year figure, and an explicit attrition rate, which no public source currently supplies.

The most useful next step is not more modelling but four documents: the TCI and Fundsmith filed PDFs on Companies House (company numbers 04706248, 08898250, 07170593 and OC354233), which would replace estimated staff costs and member counts with reported ones; the Pershing Square final prospectus beneficial-ownership table, which would replace the assumed 70% investment-staff share of the partner pool; the Soroban Tax Court opinion, the only known primary document with partner-level dollars at a Tiger Cub; and the SEC's Form ADV annual filings for all nine advisers, which would pin down investment headcount year by year. With those in hand, the per-head residuals could be stated to within a factor of two rather than ten, though the analyst-level split within each pool would still rest on the founders' undisclosed discretion.
