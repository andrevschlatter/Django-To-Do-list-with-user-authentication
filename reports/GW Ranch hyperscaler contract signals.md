# Both Hyperscalers Already Signed Behind-the-Meter Gigawatts

The premise that Amazon and Microsoft are the two hyperscalers still missing big behind-the-meter (BTM) announcements is wrong on the evidence, though it is an understandable mistake given how both companies disclosed. **Microsoft crossed the line first: on June 22, 2026 Chevron's Energy Forge One announced a 20-year power agreement to supply Microsoft's ~2 GW Pecos, Texas campus from a 2.67 GW off-grid gas plant ("Project Kilby")**, on top of a March 2026 letter of intent for 1.35 GW of compute at Nscale's off-grid Monarch campus in West Virginia and a lease of 700–900 MW at Crusoe's gas-powered Abilene campus; SemiAnalysis tallies more than 5 GW of BTM nameplate signed by Microsoft in 2026 alone. **Amazon followed on August 7, 2026, when it confirmed to Cleanview that it owns GW Ranch, the 7.65 GW-permitted Pacifico Energy gas campus in Pecos County, Texas, which its developer calls the largest power-generation air permit ever granted in the United States**, and that it will buy power from the plant. GW Ranch is developed by Pacifico Energy (founder-CEO Nate Franklin) through the entity Pacifico GW LLC, holds TCEQ permit 181033/PSDTX1672/GHGPSDTX255 for 35 simple-cycle turbines, and has Amazon as both landowner and sole tenant. **Neither Liberty Energy, Atlas Energy Solutions, ProPetro, Solaris Energy Infrastructure nor VoltaGrid appears in any permit, filing, earnings call or press item connected to GW Ranch, and none of the five has named Amazon or Microsoft as a customer.** The permit sweep found exactly three Texas gas-generation records tied to a hyperscaler (GW Ranch, Kilby, and VoltaGrid's Shackelford County permit serving Oracle/OpenAI) and no air permit, PUCT, ERCOT, RRC, FERC or JETI record filed under any of the five providers' own names. The closest things to a coming hyperscaler contract are dated guidance rather than filings: Liberty's target of more than 500 MW of energy-services agreements announced before year-end 2026, Solaris's "first location for our third hyperscaler customer" ramping in Q4 2026 plus an unread late-September 8-K, Atlas's September 25 cost-reimbursement deals with an unnamed "frontier AI lab," and ProPetro's 60 MW Midwest hyperscaler site, which is the only one of the five actually delivering power to a hyperscaler today.

*Method caveat, stated once: every researcher's note behind this report carries the same limitation. The session's network proxy blocked direct page fetches on essentially every relevant domain (TCEQ, ERCOT, SEC EDGAR, DCD, Utility Dive, company IR sites, local press), and the web-search budget ran out before verification passes. Every finding therefore rests on search-engine excerpts of the cited pages rather than full-page reads. Permit numbers are reproduced only where an excerpt quoted them from an agency document or a tracker citing one. Where excerpts disagreed, both figures are given and the conflicts are collected in a table near the end. Readers should re-verify any figure they intend to trade on against the linked primary document.*

## Both hyperscalers crossed into BTM gas in 2026, Microsoft in June and Amazon in August

Microsoft's Kilby arrangement is the most fully documented hyperscaler BTM deal in the country. Chevron's release describes a 20-year agreement under which Kilby "is expected to deliver approximately 2.67 gigawatts of capacity, built through a phased, modular approach," with a final investment decision tied to year-end 2026 ([Chevron](https://www.chevron.com/newsroom/2026/q2/chevron-signs-20-year-power-agreement-with-microsoft-for-west-texas-data-center)). Two large GE Vernova turbines generate most of the power, with Caterpillar's Solar Turbines supplying the rest, and the plant is not initially designed to flow through the transmission grid ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/22/chevron-cvx-microsoft-msft-natural-gas-data-center.html)). The counterparty structure matters for anyone tracking supplier exposure: Chevron's subsidiary Energy Forge One LLC holds the Microsoft contract, Joulent (spun out of Engine No. 1, launched the same day) develops the plant through a 50-50 JV with Chevron, and National Grid Ventures agreed to invest **$1.75 billion for a 35% stake in Joulent** ([Morningstar/Business Wire](https://www.morningstar.com/news/business-wire/20260622143711/joulent-launches-to-deliver-multi-gigawatt-power-solutions-for-american-innovation)). Microsoft said "the energy infrastructure required for the site is being funded by the company itself," and Noelle Walsh framed it as "pairing new data centre infrastructure with dedicated energy supply located onsite" to "bring capacity online at the pace our customers require" ([Data Centre Magazine](https://datacentremagazine.com/news/inside-microsofts-data-centre-build-in-pecos-west-texas)). The deal was preceded by Bloomberg's March 31 report of exclusive talks over a "$7 billion" plant and an April exclusivity agreement ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-31/microsoft-in-talks-with-chevron-engine-no-1-over-7-billion-texas-power-plant)). On the regulatory side, Energy Forge One's TCEQ application (181895/PSDTX1684/GHGPSDTX260) had a public meeting on June 10, 2026 and sits at draft-permit stage ([TCEQ](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/agency/decisions/hearings/events/public-meeting-energy-forge-one-llc-181895-psdtx1684-and-ghgpsdtx260)), and its JETI application J0022 lists a **$6.5 billion** fossil-fuel generation investment with an estimated gross tax benefit of $227.8 million ([Texas Comptroller](https://comptroller.texas.gov/economy/development/prop-tax/jeti/application-details.php?id=J0022)). The one caveat is timing: Semafor reported on October 1 that FID "could slip from the end of this year to 2027 because of state politics," though Chevron's Daniel Droog said Kilby "is still on track to produce power by 2028" and Joulent's CFO said construction contracts were "days or a couple of weeks from being signed" ([Semafor](https://semafor.com/article/10/01/2026/texas-politics-could-delay-investment-decision-for-huge-chevron-data-center)).

Kilby is not Microsoft's only BTM exposure. In mid-March 2026 Microsoft signed a letter of intent for **1.35 GW of AI compute at Nscale's Monarch campus** in Mason County, West Virginia, where Phase 1 is a 2.16 GW off-grid plant built from 864 Caterpillar G3500-series reciprocating engines, with a construction permit application filed April 20, 2026 under the state's Certified Microgrid program ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/nscale-acquires-8gw-monarch-compute-campus-microsoft-signs-on-for-135gw-of-compute/); [Herald-Dispatch](https://www.herald-dispatch.com/business/point-pleasant-data-center-to-have-3-buildings-almost-1-000-generators/article_30c4bffe-b5d1-4e1d-a0f7-aff4426f2481.html)). Bloomberg reported Microsoft agreed to lease roughly **700 MW at Crusoe's Abilene campus** after Oracle and OpenAI walked away ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-24/microsoft-to-rent-texas-data-center-dropped-by-oracle-openai)), and Crusoe's own release describes a new 900 MW AI factory campus for Microsoft with onsite generation and batteries, first building energized mid-2027 ([Crusoe](https://www.crusoe.ai/resources/newsroom/crusoe-announces-new-900-mw-ai-factory-campus-in-abilene-texas-to-support-microsoft-ai-infrastructure)). Microsoft's Nebius capacity at DataOne's Vineland, New Jersey campus depends on an onsite gas plant that is now in enforcement: NJDEP fined DataOne **$1.07 million on September 22, 2026** for running 62 gas generators without air permits ([WHYY](https://whyy.org/articles/vineland-data-center-fined/)). SemiAnalysis sums it up: "In 2026 year-to-date, Microsoft has signed over 5GW of behind-the-meter nameplate capacity, of which 2.7GW with Joulent & Chevron, and well over 2GW through turnkey datacenter leases with companies like Crusoe" ([SemiAnalysis](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/what-is-so-hard-about-behind-the)). Microsoft has also been quietly permitting gas engines at its own campuses: Wyoming DEQ approved 30 natural-gas engines at Cheyenne on April 3, 2026 alongside a 3,200-acre expansion ([County 17](https://county17.com/2026/04/14/microsoft-plans-massive-3200-acre-expansion-of-cheyenne-data-centers/)), and McCoy Power Reports tracked a revision at Rio Medina, Texas to add 30 Caterpillar 3,100 ekW generators (~93 MW) ([Gas Turbine World](https://gasturbineworld.com/data-center-air-permits-point-to-massive-on-site-power-buildout/)). The Constellation Crane (ex-TMI-1) 835 MW PPA and the Helion 50 MW fusion plant are grid-matching arrangements, not BTM ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/three-mile-island-nuclear-power-plant-to-return-as-microsoft-signs-20-year-835mw-ai-data-center-ppa/)).

Amazon's BTM portfolio is smaller in count but larger in the single asset. Before August 2026, every Amazon "onsite" arrangement was either utility-owned or had moved toward the grid: the Talen Susquehanna deal was restructured on June 11, 2025 from a 960 MW co-located arrangement into a **1,920 MW front-of-the-meter PPA through 2042** after FERC rejected the amended interconnection agreement, with Talen's 10-Q placing the transition in April 2026 ([Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/talen-amazon-aws-susquehanna-nuclear-data-centert/750440/); [Talen 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001622536/000162253626000066/tln-20260630.htm)); the 73 MW Bloom fuel-cell array at Hilliard, Ohio is owned and operated by AEP Ohio with Amazon covering costs ([City of Hilliard](https://hilliardohio.gov/fuel-cells/)); NIPSCO's GenCo will build **two 1.3 GW gas plants plus a 400 MW battery** dedicated to Amazon's Indiana campuses ([Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/nisource-nipsco-amazon-data-centers-indiana/806396/)); and Duke Energy holds a separate permit for 57 temporary generators bridging Amazon's Richmond County, North Carolina campus ([WUNC](https://www.wunc.org/2026-08-29/amazons-richmond-county-data-center-gets-green-light-on-air-quality-permits)). The September 30, 2026 Constellation Calvert Cliffs deal (690 MW including a 190 MW uprate, 20 years) is explicitly front-of-meter, with power continuing to flow into PJM ([Constellation](https://www.constellationenergy.com/news/2026/09/constellation-and-amazon-announce-20-year-power-purchase-agreement-at-calvert-cliffs.html); [Maryland Matters](https://marylandmatters.org/2026/09/30/calvert-cliffs-power-purchase-amazon/)), and the Generac agreement (initial $2.4 billion across 2027–28, up to **$8 billion** over its life) is backup gensets, not prime power ([The Daily Upside](https://www.thedailyupside.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/generac-soars-as-amazons-energy-backup-plan/)). GW Ranch changed the picture: Amazon "confirmed Friday [August 7, 2026] that it is financing a massive private gas power plant in Texas that could become the single-largest source of greenhouse gas emissions in the United States" ([AFP via TechXplore](https://techxplore.com/news/2026-08-amazon-massive-private-gas-centers.html)), and Cleanview describes it as "Amazon's first major investment in an off-grid data center" ([Cleanview](https://newsletter.cleanview.co/p/scoop-amazon-is-behind-one-of-the)).

Why does the "no announcement yet" premise persist? Because neither company announced in the conventional way. Microsoft's deal was released by Chevron; Amazon's was broken by a newsletter cross-referencing building permits with satellite imagery, after which Amazon issued a statement rather than a contract press release. Amazon's management has never said "gas turbines" or "behind-the-meter" on an earnings call: on the July 30, 2026 Q2 call Jassy raised 2026 capex to roughly **$220 billion** and said Amazon "will still not have enough capacity to meet all the demand we have in 2026" ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/07/30/andy-jassy-amazon-capex-demand-aws-pga-tour/)), and that the company is "on pace to double power capacity by the end of '27 compared to '25, with most 2027 capacity already reserved" ([BigGo transcript summary](https://finance.biggo.com/quote/AMZN/earnings-call/US_AMZN_2026-07-30)), but no transcript excerpt mentions onsite generation. Microsoft's posture is similar: the January 13, 2026 Community-First plan commits to paying rates that cover all infrastructure and to "contract new power generation as needed" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/13/microsoft-data-centers-energy-ai-prices.html)), and Bloomberg's report of a **38 GW capacity target by 2032** (from ~12 GW today) implies the next wave comes largely through neocloud leases rather than Microsoft-signed generation PPAs ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/microsoft-targets-38gw-of-data-center-capacity-in-2032-report/)). The substance is there; the headlines were not.

## GW Ranch is Pacifico's 7.65 GW Pecos County campus, and Amazon owns both the land and the offtake

GW Ranch ("GW" for gigawatt; there is no pre-existing ranch of that name) is an off-grid gas-turbine power campus plus AI data center campus on roughly 8,000–8,400 acres along State Highway 18 about 17 miles north of Fort Stockton, Pecos County, in the Permian Basin. Pacifico Energy announced it on August 20, 2025 as a project "in development since early 2024" pairing gas turbines with batteries to deliver 5 GW to hyperscale data centers, targeting 1 GW in 2028 and 5 GW in 2030 ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250820331187/en/Pacifico-Energy-Announces-GW-Ranch-Project-Bringing-5-Gigawatts-of-Reliable-Off-Grid-Power-for-AI-Innovation)). The developer is Pacifico Energy, the U.S. unit of Pacifico Energy Group, founded 2012 and headquartered in San Juan Capistrano, California, whose founder, CEO and president **Nate (William Nathan) Franklin** is a former BP solar development director who built Japanese solar after Fukushima ([Crunchbase](https://www.crunchbase.com/person/nate-franklin); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/christopherhelman/2026/02/19/this-daring-developer-wants-to-power-americas-ai-future/)). Constantyn Gieskes, identified by Inside Climate News as VP of project development, is the other named principal ([Inside Climate News](https://insideclimatenews.org/news/29012026/developer-calls-gw-ranch-in-pecos-county-texas-the-largest-power-project-in-u-s/)). The permit-holding entity is **Pacifico GW LLC**, which Global Energy Monitor lists as 100% owner of both permitted units with Pacifico Energy Group LLC as parent ([GEM](https://www.gem.wiki/GW_Ranch_Energy_Center)). Forbes put Franklin's construction-cost estimate at **$12 billion** and, crucially for the timeline, reported in February 2026 that Pacifico "has no customers yet" and "lacks a commitment from the hyperscalers" ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/christopherhelman/2026/02/19/this-daring-developer-wants-to-power-americas-ai-future/)).

The air permit is the project's anchor document. TCEQ received Pacifico GW LLC's application on August 4, 2025, ran it on an expedited basis, dated its technical review January 21, 2026, and issued Permit 181033 with PSD permit PSDTX1672 and GHG PSD permit GHGPSDTX255 for the "GW Ranch Energy Center" ([TCEQ notice](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/downloads/permitting/air/bilingual/pending-permit-notices/181033-napd-english.pdf); [Oil & Gas Watch](https://oilandgaswatch.org/facility/rec_d2vfu617uo67v8p3it8g)). The technical review states that Pacifico "proposes construction of 35 natural gas-fired simple cycle turbines ... with a nominal output of 5,000 megawatts," that "21 will be Siemens SGT-800 turbines and 14 will be large frame turbines of an undecided manufacturer" chosen from Doosan DGT6-300H S2, GE 7HA.03, Mitsubishi M501JAC or Siemens SGT6-9000HL, with SCR using 19% aqueous ammonia and two Wärtsilä 18V50SG supplemental engines of 18.8 MW each ([TCEQ technical review, mirror](https://ukozvhzgrcnkcatjqfoo.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/03-00-air-construction/2026_01_21_GW_Ranch_Energy_Center_181033__PSDTX167_f6c9943f.pdf)). The **7.65 GW figure is the combined nameplate of the 35 turbines; 5 GW is the nominal delivered figure**, and the plant "will not be connected or capable of selling electricity to the local utility power grid" ([TCEQ plain-language summary](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/downloads/permitting/air/bilingual/pending-permit-notices/181033-pls-english.pdf)). Permitted potential emissions are **11,012.56 tpy of criteria pollutants and 33,204,964 tpy CO2e** ([Oil & Gas Watch](https://oilandgaswatch.org/facility/rec_d2vfu617uo67v8p3it8g)), which the Texas Tribune rendered as "more than 12,000 tons per year of regulated air pollutants" and "up to 33 million tons per year of greenhouse gases, equal to nearly 5% of the total annual greenhouse gas emissions of Canada" ([Texas Tribune](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/02/02/texas-permian-basin-power-plant-project-data-centers/)). Pacifico's own page adds 1.8 GW of battery storage, up to 750 MWac of solar, and "a 15-mile 1 BCF/d direct pipeline to Waha," with first power Q1 2027, 1 GW by 2028 and 5+ GW by 2031 "depending on customer needs" ([Pacifico Energy](https://www.pacificoenergy.com/gw-ranch)). Vinson & Elkins partners Jon Niermann and Eric Groten were permitting counsel ([Yahoo Finance/Law.com](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/vinson-elkins-advises-pacifico-energy-171104538.html)), and three Democratic senators on the EPW committee wrote to Franklin and Gieskes on March 13, 2026 asking why 7.65 GW of gas was chosen and whether carbon capture would be used ([Senate EPW letter](https://www.epw.senate.gov/public/_cache/files/e/d/ed1bcbb3-f37f-4302-9578-79946ec1a881/9801E878C570A5484426DEA549A0554A5CEB9663FB7A72AF0B72ABF161CA88CD.letter-to-pacifico.pdf)).

Amazon's involvement surfaced through a different agency entirely. On August 4, 2026 Amazon Data Services, Inc. registered three buildings (A, B, C) collectively named "Pecos County Data Center" at 6000 TX-18 with the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation, each **~189,060 square feet and ~$300 million**, construction scheduled August–December 2026, with Gensler listed as design firm ([TDLR TABS2026026954](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/TABS/Projects/TABS2026026954); [US Data Center Projects](https://usdatacenterprojects.com/texas/amazon-files-plans-for-new-data-center-buildings-in-texas-across-three-sites)). Cleanview's Michael Thomas "cross-referenced three building permits filed with Texas authorities with satellite images showing land clearing that began on July 24," and "Amazon subsequently confirmed it: the GW Ranch project, in Pecos County, is theirs" ([Distilled/Cleanview](https://www.distilled.earth/p/scoop-amazon-is-behind-one-of-the)). Amazon's statement said the campus "is powered by new on-site generation that won't raise electricity costs for Texas families and designed to transition to grid-connected service as interconnection timelines allow" ([Heatmap](https://heatmap.news/energy/amazon-gw-ranch)), and Amazon told Data Center Knowledge it is "actively exploring" a transition to grid service without disclosing timing or whether it has filed an ERCOT large-load request ([Data Center Knowledge](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/energy-power-supply/amazon-explores-grid-connection-for-8-000-acre-ai-campus)). The county had been brought in earlier: Pacifico presented to the Pecos County Commissioners Court on May 11, 2026, and the June 22, 2026 agenda carried a public hearing to designate the "GW Ranch Reinvestment Zone" under Tax Code Chapter 312 and an item to "Accept, Approve, and Execute a Tax Abatement Agreement with Pacifico GW LLC" ([Pecos County agenda, 6/22/2026](https://www.co.pecos.tx.us/commissioners-court-regular-session-agenda-6222026/)). The sequence (permit January, "no customers" February, county abatement June, Amazon filings and land clearing late July, confirmation August 7) brackets Amazon's commitment to roughly February–July 2026. Cleanview's tracker already lists "AWS GW Ranch – Building 12" at 75 MW with a 2030 operational date, implying a multi-building phased campus well beyond the first three structures ([Cleanview](https://cleanview.co/data-centers/texas/4488/aws-gw-ranch---building-12)).

What remains undisclosed is everything an investor would most want: the deal structure (PPA, tolling, or Amazon ownership of generation), the contract term, the price, the MW volume, the land purchase price, the EPC contractor, the gas supplier for the Waha lateral, and which vendor won the 14 large-frame turbines ([mlq.ai](https://mlq.ai/news/amazon-invests-in-texas-ai-campus-tied-to-gas-plant-permitted-for-33-million-tons-of-annual-emissions/)). The only confirmed equipment order is Ansaldo Energia's July 20, 2026 announcement that it will supply Pacifico with **eight AE64.3A gas turbines (~78 MW each, ~624 MW total)** for "a Texas data center project," first shipments 2027; Ansaldo "did not identify Pacifico's Texas project" ([Ansaldo Energia](https://www.ansaldoenergia.com/about-us/media-center/power-generation-news-insights/detail-news/ansaldo-energia-returns-to-the-usa-market-with-an-order-for-eight-ae643a-gas-turbines-for-a-texas-data-center-project); [POWER](https://www.powermag.com/ansaldo-us-gas-turbine-market-equipment-crunch/)). Those units match neither permitted class at GW Ranch (62.5 MW SGT-800s or ~450 MW frames), so they are either for Pacifico's other Texas project or would require a permit amendment. That other project is Cedar Creek in Bastrop County (2,842 acres, $2.2 billion, 710 MW of gas for ~490 MW of IT load, JETI application J0049, abatement requests pulled in August 2026 but not cancelled) ([Score Property Group](https://scorepropertygroup.com/insights/austin-bastrop-data-center-activity-2026/); [Texas Comptroller J0049](https://assets.comptroller.texas.gov/open-data/jeti/J0049/J0049-pacifico-bastrop-app.pdf)). Pacifico is also courting Brewster County for a second ~8,400-acre site in the Glass Mountains northwest of Alpine, meeting officials on September 15, 2026 with investment figures ranging from ~$5 billion to $60 billion and no agreements approved ([TPR](https://www.tpr.org/news/2026-09-18/developer-behind-amazons-pecos-county-data-center-approaches-brewster-county-officials)). Two disambiguations are worth stating plainly. First, a September 2025 Power Engineering article said the pre-Amazon plan "would pair GE Vernova gas turbines with BWRX-300 small modular reactors"; the issued permit names no GE Vernova contract and no nuclear component, so that reference is stale ([Power Engineering](https://www.power-eng.com/onsite-power/pacifico-energy-plans-5-gw-off-grid-facility-in-texas-for-hyperscale-data-centers/)). Second, **Microsoft has no connection to GW Ranch**; the only link is geographic, since the Chevron/Microsoft Kilby complex "sits roughly 30 miles away" in Reeves County near the town of Pecos, and sources sometimes conflate the two Permian off-grid campuses ([Chron](https://www.chron.com/news/science-environment/article/texas-data-center-power-21324591.php)).

## The next deals are visible in county filings and permit dockets, not press releases

For Amazon, the strongest unannounced signal is Homer City, Pennsylvania. In August 2026 an Amazon spokesman said "While we don't have a definitive agreement in place, we are in discussions with Homer City Redevelopment" ([Marcellus Drilling News](https://marcellusdrilling.com/2026/08/amazon-in-talks-to-land-data-centers-at-homer-city-gas-plant/)); by October 2026 Amazon Data Services had "filed a preliminary master land development plan with Indiana County to build 36 data centers on roughly 1,100 acres of the campus, powered by the 4.4–4.5 gigawatt (GW) natural gas-fired plant now under construction there" ([Marcellus Drilling News](https://marcellusdrilling.com/2026/10/amazon-files-plans-for-36-data-centers-at-homer-city-gas-plant/); [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/aws-plans-36-building-data-center-campus-in-indiana-county-pennsylvania/)). The plant uses seven GE Vernova hydrogen-enabled turbines with first deliveries in 2026 and would be, per Kiewit, "the largest gas-fired power plant in the United States," with more than 1 GW injected back to the grid ([Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/homer-city-gas-fired-power-station-data-center-firstenergy/744332/)). Moving from "in discussions" to a 36-building land plan in two months makes a definitive co-location agreement the most probable next Amazon BTM headline. Behind it sit Cleanview's count of **eight new gas plants tied to Amazon data centers with 17.5 GW combined capacity** ([Michael Thomas on X](https://x.com/curious_founder/status/2103160926786773460)), including three in Mississippi where Entergy executed a large-customer agreement for a Hinds County campus in April 2026 and Amazon pledged $12 billion more while promising to cover "all expenses for new energy infrastructure" ([Distilled/Cleanview](https://www.distilled.earth/p/eight-new-gas-plants-will-power-amazons); [Entergy 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000065984/000006598426000283/etr-20260630.htm); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/09/amazon-ceo-andy-jassy-ai-spending.html)). Pennsylvania DEP is also processing Amazon's application 09-0261A for **280 natural gas-fired generator engines** at Falls Township, framed as backup only, with comments closed August 16, 2026 ([PA DEP](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dep/newsroom/2026-07-13-dep-to-host-community-meeting-on-proposed-air-quality-permit-for-amazon-data-center-bucks-county)). The friction is rising in parallel: SELC filed two petitions in late September alleging that "by splitting the 600-plus generators into two permits, Duke and Amazon are illegally evading regulations" in North Carolina ([SELC](https://www.selc.org/press-release/amazon-and-duke-energy-attempt-to-dodge-pollution-limits-at-massive-hyperscale-data-center-in-north-carolina-selc-says/)), and a Minnesota PUC ruling held that Amazon cannot skip the permitting process for generators at the Sherco site ([Yahoo News](https://www.yahoo.com/news/amazon-cant-skip-permitting-process-155824923.html)).

For Microsoft, the pipeline runs through Chevron and Wisconsin. Chevron said in June it "eyes more deals to power US data centers" ([EnergyNow](https://energynow.com/2026/06/chevron-eyes-more-deals-to-power-us-data-centers/)), the original Engine No. 1/Chevron/GE Vernova partnership targeted up to 4 GW of "power foundries" on seven reserved 7HA slots against 2.67 GW committed at Kilby ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250128934656/en/Engine-No.-1-Chevron-and-GE-Vernova-to-Power-U.S.-Data-Centers)), Chevron has said the Pecos plant could expand to as much as 5,000 MW ([The Energy Mag](https://theenergymag.com/news/2026-04-02/microsoft-chevron-texas-ai-power)), and National Grid's initiative with Joulent aims to connect more than 10 GW of new demand within five years ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/national-grid-ventures-invests-175bn-in-joulent-for-35-stake-to-develop-large-load-power-solutions-national-grid-ventures-ngv-the-commercial-arm-of-national-grid-has-agreed-to-invest-175-billion-for-a-35-stake-in-us-power-infrastructure-develope/)). In Wisconsin, Microsoft "has already signed up to purchase electricity" from Invenergy's **~1.2 GW Red Oak Ridge gas plant** in Kenosha County, which We Energies is seeking to buy for "substantial data center load additions," and advocacy groups read the filings as showing the plant would exclusively serve the Mount Pleasant campus ([WPR](https://www.wpr.org/news/developer-proposes-natural-gas-plant-kenosha-county-invenergy-red-oak-ridge-energy-center)). Microsoft filed a FERC protest on August 21, 2026 against a >$500 million ATC–We Energies transmission charge tied to that campus, saying it was excluded from drafting the agreement while remaining "fully committed" to paying its infrastructure costs ([mgrid](https://mgrid.org/2026/08/25/microsoft-protests-the-500-million-transmission-charge-built-for-its-own-wisconsin-data-center/)). That is effectively a dedicated plant 100% subscribed by one customer under a Bespoke Resource tariff, a "front-of-the-meter BTM" structure that a We Energies purchase approval would make concrete. The remaining Microsoft items are conversions of already-signed arrangements: the Nscale Monarch LOI into a binding agreement once West Virginia permits issue, and a resolution at Vineland within NJDEP's 45-day window.

Texas regulation now gates all of this. On September 21, 2026 Governor Abbott directed TCEQ to pause issuance of all air and water permits for infrastructure directly supporting data centers until ERCOT completes its large-load audit, with TCEQ to respond by October 19 and the pause reaching "projects that will not connect to ERCOT" ([Office of the Governor](https://gov.texas.gov/news/post/governor-abbott-directs-tceq-to-halt-data-center-permits); [Bracewell](https://www.bracewell.com/resources/governor-abbott-orders-tceq-to-pause-all-permit-issuance-for-data-center-projects/)). ERCOT's verification report on provisionally qualified large loads is due **December 10, 2026** ([PUCT Interchange filing 59142](https://interchange.puc.texas.gov/Documents/59142_52_1675838.PDF)). GW Ranch's permit is already issued, so it is insulated; Kilby's draft permit and any new Pacifico, Liberty/PowerBridge or Atlas Texas filing are not. ERCOT's Batch Zero framework, approved June 2026, created the Withdrawal-Limited Private Use Network path for co-located generation, and its September 11 update lists 11 WLPUN projects, nine with generation exceeding load, but ERCOT does not publish applicant names ([ERCOT Batch Zero update](https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/09/11/14-Batch-Zero-Update.pdf)). Practically, that means no additional Texas BTM announcement from either hyperscaler is likely before mid-December, and the next scheduled management venue is Amazon's Q3 call in late October.

## The permit sweep found three hyperscaler-tied Texas gas permits and no filings by any of the five providers

The sweep's positive findings are narrow and precise. In Texas, the only records that tie a hyperscaler to a >1 GW onsite gas plant are GW Ranch (Pacifico GW LLC, TCEQ 181033/PSDTX1672/GHGPSDTX255, issued January 2026, plus Amazon Data Services' TDLR registrations and the Pecos County abatement agenda) and Kilby (Energy Forge One LLC, TCEQ 181895/PSDTX1684/GHGPSDTX260, filing from Chevron's 1500 Louisiana Street address, plus JETI J0022 and Microsoft's PPA). The TCEQ record for GW Ranch never names Amazon; the hyperscaler signal came from a different agency, TDLR, filed by Amazon Data Services under its own name, and from a county agenda that predated Amazon's confirmation by six weeks ([TDLR](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/TABS/Projects/TABS2026026954); [Pecos County](https://www.co.pecos.tx.us/commissioners-court-regular-session-agenda-6222026/)). The third hyperscaler-tied record is VoltaGrid's. VoltaGrid LLC applied on December 12, 2025 for Permit 182467/PSDTX1692/GHGPSDTX266 at 299 FM 604, Abilene (Shackelford County), for Jenbacher J624 engines with SCR serving "the nearby data center campus" ([TCEQ](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/downloads/permitting/air/bilingual/pending-permit-notices/182467-pls-english.pdf)); the draft permit issued September 18, 2026 with comments due October 19 and revised plans calling for **610 generators totaling 2,582 MW**, up from the 210 units/700 MW originally authorized under permit 180842 ([Oil & Gas Watch](https://oilandgaswatch.org/facility/rec_d3dtq3o7l7g4fps53aug)). ## Conclusion

The investable conclusion is that the two "missing" hyperscalers are not missing; they simply chose counterparties outside the oilfield-services-to-power group. Amazon's first and only true off-grid prime-power campus is a developer-owned turbine plant (Pacifico), and Microsoft's are an oil-major-owned turbine plant (Chevron/Joulent) and neocloud-owned engine fleets (Nscale, Crusoe, DataOne). Everywhere else both companies prefer structures in which a regulated utility holds the permit and the asset. That pattern, visible in every record the sweep found, is the real reason none of Liberty, Atlas, ProPetro, Solaris or VoltaGrid has an Amazon or Microsoft logo: the large-frame, PSD-permitted, 2–8 GW campus is not the modular-recip product these companies sell, and the one place a modular fleet could enter GW Ranch (bridge or supplemental power before 2027) is already specified as Wärtsilä engines in the permit. If one of the five does land a top-two hyperscaler, the evidence suggests it will arrive through an intermediary (a Vantage tenant for Liberty, a "Midwest hyperscaler" expansion for ProPetro, an "investment-grade global technology company" site for Solaris) rather than a direct Amazon or Microsoft contract, and it will show up first in a TDLR building registration, a county abatement agenda or a standard-permit registration under an SPV name, not in a TCEQ public notice.

Three things would change this picture, and all are dated. Liberty has put more than 500 MW of ESAs on the table for year-end 2026 with its Q3 call in mid-October; Solaris has an unread 8-K and a Q4 ramp it attributes to a third hyperscaler; and Texas has frozen every new data-center permit until ERCOT reports on December 10, which protects the already-permitted GW Ranch and Kilby incumbents while stalling every provider whose next site needs a TCEQ authorization. The asymmetry is worth noticing: the Abbott pause raises the scarcity value of the few islanded megawatts already permitted and operating, which today means xAI's Colossus (Solaris's Stateline), Oracle's Abilene build (VoltaGrid's 2.58 GW draft permit), and ProPetro's 60 MW, and it makes Amazon's and Microsoft's own 2027–2028 power arrive on the schedule of Pacifico's turbine deliveries and Chevron's FID rather than anything the five providers control.
