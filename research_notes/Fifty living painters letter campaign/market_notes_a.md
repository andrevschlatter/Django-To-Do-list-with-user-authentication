# Market notes A: 25 living painters (as of 16 November 2026)

## Read this first (method and confidence)

- **Every figure below is SNIPPET-grade.** Page fetching failed for all hosts in this session (en.wikipedia.org and www.theartnewspaper.com both returned "host not allowed"), so nothing was OPENED. Each figure comes from a search-result summary of the cited URL and should be checked on the source page before it is quoted to a collector.
- Searches used: 68 of the 70 allowed (max 3 per artist).
- **Aggregators are low confidence**: appraisily.com, masterworks.com, art.salon, veryimportantlot.com, invaluable.com. They are flagged "(agg.)". Higher confidence: auction-house pages, Culture Type, HENI News, Artnet News, museum and gallery sites.
- Artnet's public results pages list many 2025-2026 lots for most of these artists but show no prices in search summaries. "None found" therefore means "no priced result surfaced", not "no sales occurred".
- Primary-market (gallery) price ranges: **not found for any of the 25 artists.** One private fair sale is reported (Mark Bradford).
- Verdicts are my inference from the cited results; where they rest on one or two data points they are marked "thin evidence".

## Cross-cutting inferences

- Institutional momentum and auction prices have decoupled for several artists: Marshall (unsold $10m-15m lot during "The Histories"), Mark Bradford (three-venue museum tour, auction total down 92% in 2024), Scharf and Whitney (major surveys, weaker recent auction results found).
- The clearest upward moves are among late-recognised women painters: Wylie (record June 2026), Snyder (record November 2023, $226,800 in May 2025), Katherine Bradford (best result May 2025), and Pindell (Dia acquisition of 21 works).
- For 1980s-generation names (Schnabel, Salle, Clemente, Dine, Poons) records are 7 to 37 years old and recent results found sit in the five to low six figures.
