## Key Question 1: What onsite/BTM/co-located power deals has Microsoft publicly signed (2024–2026), and at what size?

### Takeaway
Microsoft's only true behind-the-meter (off-grid at start) deal it has itself announced is the June 22, 2026 Project Kilby arrangement in Pecos, TX (2.67 GW gas plant, 20-year PPA with Chevron's Energy Forge One / Joulent, serving a ~2 GW Microsoft campus, first power 2028, FID not yet taken). Everything else "BTM" in Microsoft's portfolio is indirect: leases/LOIs at neocloud or developer campuses that bring their own gas (Nscale Monarch WV ~1.35 GW LOI; Crusoe Abilene ~700 MW; Nebius/DataOne Vineland NJ), plus onsite gas-engine permits at Microsoft-owned campuses in Cheyenne WY and Rio Medina TX. SemiAnalysis tallies >5 GW of BTM nameplate signed by Microsoft in 2026 YTD. The Constellation Crane (TMI-1) 835 MW PPA and the Helion 50 MW fusion deal are grid-connected/matching arrangements, not BTM.

### Inferences
- Microsoft's "bring your own power" exposure is overwhelmingly gas and overwhelmingly via counterparties that own the generation (Chevron/Joulent, Nscale, Crusoe, Nebius/DataOne). Microsoft itself has only permitted modest onsite gas-engine fleets (Cheyenne 30 engines; Rio Medina 30 x 3.1 MW).
- The 2.67 GW Kilby plant vs. 2 GW campus implies ~0.67 GW of redundancy/islanding margin or future grid sales; sources do not reconcile the gap.
- Summing confirmed/reported items: Kilby 2.67 GW + Monarch ~2.16 GW phase-1 plant (1.35 GW LOI) + Crusoe Abilene 0.9 GW onsite + Vineland 0.4 GW planned ≈ 6 GW of BTM generation serving Microsoft load, consistent with SemiAnalysis's ">5 GW" figure.

## Key Question 2: Microsoft's latest (2026) stance on onsite gas and nuclear; executive statements

### Takeaway
Microsoft's 2026 posture is "pay the full cost and bring dedicated supply": the Jan 13, 2026 Community-First plan (Brad Smith) commits to paying electricity rates that cover all infrastructure and to "contract new power generation as needed"; by June 2026 Noelle Walsh was publicly defending co-located gas at Pecos as the way to "bring capacity online at the pace our customers require." Nadella frames power, not chips, as the binding constraint and says Microsoft must "earn permission" from communities. No 2026 statement from Bobby Hollis on onsite gas was found; his March 2025 line was that gas with carbon capture is acceptable "if commercially viable."
