# Liberty Energy (LBRT), Atlas Energy Solutions (AESI), ProPetro (PUMP): data-center power businesses and hyperscaler contract signals (Jan 2025 – Oct 7, 2026)

**Method note / limitations.** All findings below come from web-search result snippets and search-engine summaries of primary documents (SEC 8-K/10-Q/10-K exhibits, company press releases, earnings-call transcripts, trade press). The research environment's egress proxy blocked direct retrieval of sec.gov, the three companies' IR sites, Motley Fool, Data Center Dynamics, Hart Energy, Business Wire, Investing.com, Yahoo Finance, Substack, TCEQ records, and most other hosts, and the per-turn web-search budget was exhausted before every follow-up could be run. Items that rest on third-party transcript summaries rather than the filing text are flagged. No Q3 2026 results had been released by any of the three companies as of October 7, 2026.

## Key Question 1: What has each company actually signed for data center power, and with whom? Named or unnamed customers?

### Takeaway
None of the three companies has publicly named a hyperscaler counterparty. Liberty has a 1 GW framework/400 MW reservation with Vantage Data Centers (a named colocation developer, not a hyperscaler), a 330 MW "preliminary ESA" with an unnamed Texas data center developer that a Q1 2026 call summary says was later cancelled, and a 2 GW West Texas powered-campus JV (PowerBridge) with no tenant. Atlas has a 120 MW, 5-year PPA (Socorro, TX) with a "subsidiary of an investment-grade technology infrastructure provider" and two September 2026 cost-reimbursement agreements with "a leading frontier AI lab" backing 283 MW of new Caterpillar gensets. ProPetro has the only operating hyperscaler contract: 60 MW at an unnamed "leading hyperscaler" site in the Midwest, live since Q2 2026.
