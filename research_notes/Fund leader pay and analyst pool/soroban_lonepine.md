### Inferences
- Mandelblatt's absence from every II top-25 in 2016–2025 bounds his annual (own-capital gains + fee share) below the year's threshold: <$130M (2016), <$225M (2017), <$375M (2024), <$470M (2025), assuming II's coverage is complete. This is an upper bound on his "take," not an estimate of it.
- Mandel's Rich List figures cannot be read as his draw on firm fee revenue: with a ~$3–4B net worth largely invested in Lone Pine funds, a 20–25% fund return alone would produce $600–900M of "earnings," i.e., most of the $800M for 2025 could be own-capital gains rather than fee share.
- The missing Mandel years (2016, 2018, 2021–2023) coincide with flat/negative fund years (see KQ2), so it is likely he was below the top-25 threshold in at least 2016, 2018, 2021, 2022; 2019 and 2023 remain genuinely unknown.

## Key Question 2: What were AUM, fee terms, and net returns each year for Soroban and for Lone Pine's funds?

### Takeaway
Soroban grew from ~$8B (2015) / >$11B (2017) to ~$6B after returning $4.1B in 2018, back to ~$10B (2020) and $10.2B (end-2023), then added a ~$3.1–4.5B long-only sleeve in 2024 (13F long value $13.7B at Q4 2025); its Form ADV lists 0–1.5% management fees and 17–30% incentive allocations (tiered up to 35% over a benchmark). Lone Pine went ~$30B (2016) → $24B (end-2019) → $31B (end-2020) → $15.2B (mid-2023) → ~$17B (2024) → $19B (Oct 2025), charging 1% + 13–18/20% on Cypress and 1–2% + 10–20%-over-hurdle on Cascade, with a modified half-fee-below-high-water-mark structure after the 2022 drawdown (Cypress −36/−37.6%, Cascade −42%; 2023 +19–20%/+32%). Full-year returns are missing for many years at both firms.
