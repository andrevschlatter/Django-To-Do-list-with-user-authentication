## Key Question 2: Fee terms disclosed in ADV Part 2A

### Takeaway
Valley Forge charges a 1.00% annual management fee (monthly in advance) plus a 20% annual performance allocation with a loss-carryforward/high-water mark; Himalaya's brochure describes a Buffett-partnership-style structure with no management fee cited and a 25% incentive allocation on net appreciation above a 6% per-annum compounded preferred return after recovery of prior losses.

### Cited Findings
Valley Forge (brochure dated March 28, 2024):
- "Each Fund pays a monthly management fee, generally set at 1.00% annualized and payable monthly in advance"; pro-rated for mid-month admissions — [SEC IAPD brochure](https://files.adviserinfo.sec.gov/IAPD/Content/Common/crd_iapd_Brochure.aspx?BRCHR_VRSN_ID=900040).
- "The General Partner is generally entitled to a performance allocation equal to 20% of the annual increase, if any, in the net asset value of each investor's capital account in the VF Fund"; "An affiliate of Valley Forge is generally entitled to a performance allocation in respect of the annual increase in the net asset value of each investor's capital account in the Sub-Advised Fund"; calculated on realized and unrealized gains; "any decrease ... is carried forward in a 'loss carryforward' or 'high water mark' provision"; determined at fiscal year-end and on each withdrawal; may be reduced or waived at Valley Forge's discretion (VF Fund) or at the Institution's election with Valley Forge's consent (Sub-Advised Fund) — same brochure.
- Structure: Valley Forge Capital, LP is the main fund; Valley Forge Management, LLC (Delaware) is its general partner; the firm also sub-advises "a second private vehicle run by a large financial institution" — same brochure; HedgeFundDB (quoting an earlier brochure) says the management fee "is generally equal to 1.00% annualized" for "its sole client, Valley Forge Capital, LP" — [HedgeFundDB](https://hedgefunddb.com/Home/FundDetails/801-110956/VALLEY-FORGE-CAPITAL-MANAGEMENT--LP).
- Hedgeweek (c. 2019) independently reported "a 1 per cent management fee and a 20 per cent performance fee over a high-water mark", positions added at a 30–60% discount to intrinsic value — [Hedgeweek](https://www.hedgeweek.com/compounding-machines-cigar-butt-stocks-appeal-bond-ratings-agencies/).
- Fintrx classifies fee arrangements as percentage-of-assets plus performance-based — [Fintrx](https://fintrx.com/firms/firm/valley-forge-capital-management-162953).

Himalaya:
- Brochure excerpt: "HCI's GPs receive an incentive allocation equal to 25% of the net appreciation (whether realized or unrealized) in each applicable Limited Partner's interests after recovery of certain prior losses and a 6% per annum (compounded annually) preferred return to such Limited Partner", "subject to certain offsets, reductions, reallocations and adjustments under the applicable LPA"; the adviser "serves a single master-feeder fund complex" (Himalaya Capital Investors, L.P., formerly LL Investment Partners, L.P., and Himalaya Capital Investors (Offshore), L.P.) — [HedgeFundDB (quoting ADV Part 2A)](https://hedgefunddb.com/Home/FundDetails/801-72763/HIMALAYA-CAPITAL-MANAGEMENT-LLC).
- 2018 interview summary: "He charges no management fee and has a 6% hurdle, modeled after the original Buffett partnerships" — [Market Folly, Sept 2018](https://www.marketfolly.com/2018/09/li-lu-himalaya-capital-interview-chinas.html).
- "The fund remained closed to new investors from 2004 to 2013 and does not charge a management fee" — [Wikipedia: Li Lu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Lu). Munger's 2004 investment carried the condition that Li "not take on any additional investors" — [Benzinga, Oct 2024](https://www.benzinga.com/startups/24/10/41120804/charlie-munger-gave-his-family-fortune-to-chinese-warren-buffett-he-flipped-it-into-400-million-back). With Munger's guidance Li "reorganized the fund, removing many typical hedge-fund flaws" and adopted the early Buffett/Munger partnership structure that "did not charge an annual fixed management fee" — [Granitefirm blog, Sept 2025](https://www.granitefirm.com/blog/us/2025/09/04/li-lu-himalaya-capital/).
- Fintrx lists Himalaya as charging performance-based fees — [Fintrx: Himalaya](https://fintrx.com/firms/firm/himalaya-capital-157594). Firm website: "do not proactively provide financial advice or solicit capital ... from individual investors who are not qualified purchasers" — [himcap.com](https://www.himcap.com/). "Generally closed to new partners, but selectively holds discussions with referrals of current partners" — [Wikipedia: Himalaya Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalaya_Capital).

### Inferences
- Valley Forge's revenue therefore has a stable base (1% of AUM, ~$44M at $4.4B) plus a lumpy performance component (20% of gains above HWM), both accruing to Kantesaria-controlled entities (the LP management company and the GP).
- Himalaya's management-company revenue is essentially 100% incentive allocation: zero in years when the fund is below its high-water mark plus the 6%-compounding hurdle, and large in strong years. The 6% hurdle compounds while underwater, so after a loss year the fund must earn back the loss plus 6%/yr before any incentive accrues.
- The Himalaya structure is a "Buffett 0/6/25" arrangement (Buffett's partnerships took 25% above a 6% hurdle); the only difference from the original Buffett partnership is that Himalaya's hurdle compounds annually.

### Gaps
- The full Himalaya Part 2A (whether any share class carries a management fee, whether there is a catch-up, and the exact loss-carryforward mechanics) was not retrievable; only the excerpt above is available.
- The fee split between Valley Forge and the institution on the sub-advised (BBH) vehicle is not disclosed; the institution may retain part of the 1%/20%.

## Key Question 3: Reported annual net returns

### Takeaway
Neither firm publishes audited year-by-year returns. Valley Forge's since-inception record is reported at ~14.7–15% net annualized (2007–May 2025) vs ~10% for the S&P 500, with only a handful of individual-year data points (2017 +34%, 2018 −5.6%, 2019 +51% per a 13F-derived series; April 2020 +11.7% net, +6.2% net YTD). Himalaya's widely repeated ~30% CAGR since 1998 is unverified ("rumor"), the first year was −19%, and Munger's own statement that $88M (2004) became "four or five times that" by 2023 implies a far lower net CAGR for that capital.
