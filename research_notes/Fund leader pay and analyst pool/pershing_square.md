# Pershing Square Capital Management (Bill Ackman): firm-level economics 2016–2025 and the residual pool for non-Ackman investment staff

Research method note: direct fetches of pershingsquareholdings.com, sec.gov, institutionalinvestor.com, bloomberg.com, businesswire.com and most other hosts were blocked by the network egress proxy in this session, and the shared web-search budget ran out before every year could be filled. Figures labelled REPORTED are quoted from a source; figures labelled ESTIMATE are my arithmetic with the assumptions stated. Everything I could not source is listed under Gaps rather than guessed.

Key structural fact discovered: the management company (Pershing Square Holdco, L.P., converted to Pershing Square Inc., NYSE: PS) filed an S-1 and went public on April 29, 2026. PSH's own fee line items for 2016–2022 could not be retrieved this session (see Gaps).

## Key Question 1: PSH's management and performance fees paid to the manager each year 2016–2025 (and the fee structure)

### Takeaway
PSH pays a 1.5% management fee (0.375% quarterly in advance on fee-paying NAV) and a 16% performance fee above a high-water mark, less an "Additional Reduction" equal to 20% of fees PSCM earns on its other funds; until February 2024 that reduction was first absorbed by a $120M IPO-cost "offset" balance, so PSH effectively paid the full 16% through 2023. Exact PSH line items were only partially retrievable: manager-wide (all funds) fees are REPORTED for 2023–2025 ($170.8M/$341.9M, $206.1M/$249.4M, $230.4M/$532.1M management/performance), with half-year PSH figures for 2018–2020 and nil performance fees in 2016–2018 and 2022.

### Cited Findings

Fee structure
- Management fee: "quarterly management fee payable in advance each quarter ... equal to 0.375% (1.5% per annum) of the net assets (before any accrued performance fee) attributable to fee-paying shares" — [PSH June 2020 Interim](https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2020/08/28125504/Pershing-Square-Holdings-Ltd.-June-2020-Interim.pdf); same description in the [Pershing Square Holdco S-1/A (FY2026)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002026053/000114036126016419/ny20040230x28_s1a.htm).
- Performance fee: 16% of NAV appreciation on fee-bearing shares above a high-water mark, minus the "additional reduction"; fees crystallise each December 31 and on dividends; the additional reduction equals the lesser of the 16% fee and a "potential reduction amount" = 20% of performance allocations/fees (plus, since 2024, 20% of management fees from non-performance-fee Pershing funds) earned on certain other funds, with unused amounts carried forward — [Pershing Square Holdco Form DRS/A (FY2026)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2026053/000114036126005549/filename1.htm); [PSH 2016 Annual Report](https://pershingsquareholdings.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/PSH-Annual-Report_12.31.16.pdf) ("the potential to further reduce the 16% PSH performance fee by an amount equivalent to 20% of the performance allocation/fees generated by PSCM and its affiliated funds"); third-party summary at [AIC](https://www.theaic.co.uk/companydata/pershing-square-holdings/charges).
- IPO-cost offset: "The total offset amount, which is part of the performance fee calculation, was $120M. As of September 30, 2020, the offset amount has been reduced in the aggregate by approximately $39.6M to $80.4M" — [PSH October 2020 performance report](https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2020/11/02173108/Performance-Report-October-2020-PSH.pdf). At December 31, 2018 the offset had been "reduced by approximately $19.2M to $100.8M" — [PSH December 2018 NAV release (BusinessWire via DutchNews)](https://dutchnews.nl/businesswire/pershing-square-holdings-ltd-releases-monthly-net-asset-value-and-performance-report-for-december-2018). The offset "offsets the additional reduction until it is fully reduced to zero" — [PSH 2016 Annual Report](https://pershingsquareholdings.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/PSH-Annual-Report_12.31.16.pdf).
- February 2024 amendments: PSH's 16% performance fee "will now also be reduced by 20% of any management fees earned from any non-PSH Pershing Square funds that invest in public securities and do not have performance fees" (i.e., PSUS), and "the manager is waiving the right to receive the $36m outstanding balance of unrecovered IPO costs" — [BusinessWire, Feb 7 2024](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240207306652/en/Pershing-Square-Holdings-Ltd.-%E2%80%9CPSH%E2%80%9D-Announces-Amendments-to-its-Investment-Management-Agreement-Which-Will-Reduce-Fees-Paid-by-PSH); [QuotedData](https://quoteddata.com/2024/02/pershing-square-cuts-management-fee/); [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/pershing-square-holdings-to-see-reduced-performance-fees-93CH-4651829).
- HHH offset (2025 on): PSH's management fee is reduced by the fees PSCM earns from Howard Hughes Holdings on the HHH stock PSH holds; the HHH Services Agreement (May 5, 2025) pays PSCM a $3,750,000 quarterly base fee ($15M/yr) plus a quarterly variable fee — [S-1/A (FY2026)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002026053/000114036126016419/ny20040230x28_s1a.htm); PSH's 2024 annual report expected the HHH-related reduction to be "about an 11% reduction in management fees" — [PSH 2024 Annual Report](https://pershingsquareholdings.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Pershing-Square-Holdings-Ltd.-2024-Annual-Report-1.pdf).
- Management-fee concessions: PSCM "reduced the management fees paid by the Company and three affiliated entities ... for eight consecutive quarters beginning with the management fee payable on April 1, 2018 by a total of $32.2 million" — [PSH June 2020 Interim](https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2020/08/28125504/Pershing-Square-Holdings-Ltd.-June-2020-Interim.pdf); PSH's share was "$14.4 million over the next eight quarters in connection with the Allergan settlement" — [PSH 2017 Annual Report letter](https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/2022/10/07155606/2017-Annual-Report-Letter-Only.pdf).

### Inferences
- Because the "additional reduction" was applied first against the $120M IPO-cost offset, and that offset still had $36M unrecovered when PSCM waived it in February 2024, PSH's performance fee was effectively the full 16% (no reduction) from the 2014 IPO through 2023. The reduction only began to bite from 2024 (20% of PSUS/other-fund fees and the HHH fee offset).
- Fee-paying NAV implied by the half-year management fees (fee ÷ 0.75%): ≈ $3.6B (H1 2018), ≈ $3.6B (H1 2019), ≈ $5.3B (H1 2020). PSH total NAV is higher because shares held by PSCM affiliates (Ackman owned 21–25% of PSH in 2020–2023 per II) do not pay fees.
- ESTIMATE of PSH's 2020 performance fee: 16% × (fee-paying share ≈ 75–80%) × $3.70B gain ≈ $440–475M (consistent with $290M already accrued at June 30, 2020). ESTIMATE for 2021: 16% × ~78% × $2.44B ≈ $300M. These are not reported figures.
- From the Holdco disclosures, PSH's own performance fee was ≈ $320M in 2023 and ≈ $235M in 2024 (total minus the small PSINTL/PSLP amounts; 2024 = 2023 minus $85.5M).
- The Holdco expense line "Affiliates fee rebate" ($69.3M in 2024, $77.6M in 2025) is most plausibly the PSH additional-reduction/HHH offsets and similar rebates; not confirmed.

## Key Question 2: What II Rich List / Forbes / Bloomberg reported Ackman earned each year 2016–2025

### Takeaway
Ackman did not appear on the earnings rankings in the loss years (2016–2018, 2022); he was reported at $1.3–1.4B for 2020, ~$600–610M for 2023, $550M for 2024 and $1.1B for 2025. ## Key Question 3: Total firm AUM each year, PSH vs. private funds, and when the non-PSH funds wound down

### Takeaway
PSH grew from $7.1B (end-2019) to $18.7B (end-2025) and became ~91% of the core-fund assets; the two private funds (PSLP, PSINTL) were ~two-thirds of AUM in 2015, stopped being marketed after the 2018 redemption wave, and were down to ~$1.6B of AUM (only ~$0.7B fee-paying) by March 2026 — never formally liquidated, but kept open only for employees and long-term investors. Firm-wide AUM was ~$16.3B in April 2024 and $30.7B (incl. HHH) at end-2025.

### Cited Findings
- 2015: the two open-ended funds "comprised two-thirds of our assets under management at that time" (Valeant period) — [S-1 (FY2026)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2026053/000114036126008560/ny20040230x14_s1.htm).
