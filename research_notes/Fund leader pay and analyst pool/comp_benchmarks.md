# Analyst and PM Compensation, Comp-Pool Construction, Overhead and Tenure at Elite Single-Manager L/S Funds and Long-Only Boutiques (2016–2025)

Research conducted 2026-10-08. Note on sourcing: a large share of the primary sources (Odyssey Search Partners, Wall Street Oasis, SEC EDGAR, Institutional Investor, eFinancialCareers, Citywire, Investment Week, Hedgeweek, Heidrick, AIMA, Johnson Associates, Boyden, Glassdoor, Wikipedia) were blocked by the network egress proxy, so for those sources the figures below come from search-result excerpts of the named article rather than a full read. Two primary documents were obtained in full: the SumZero 2017 Fund Compensation Report PDF and the HFR/Glocap November 2015 press release PDF. Each finding is labeled **[survey]**, **[filing/press]**, or **[anecdote/forum]** so the report writer can weight it. The web-search budget for this session was exhausted before all follow-up queries could be run; gaps are listed at the end of each section.

## Key Question 1: What do recruiter surveys (Odyssey, Heidrick, Selby Jennings, Glocap/II, SumZero, eFinancialCareers, WSO) say year-by-year 2016–2025 about analyst comp at top single-manager L/S equity funds by experience level?

### Takeaway
No public survey isolates "top single-manager L/S equity funds" by experience level for 2016–2025; the best public series (Glocap/HFR through 2015 data, SumZero through 2016 data, Odyssey 2017–2018, eFinancialCareers 2023, Selby Jennings 2024) put the *average* entry-level analyst at a large fund at roughly $335k–$372k (2013–2015), the *median* 3–5-year professional at ~$220k and 5–7-year at ~$270k (SumZero, skewed to small funds), and the overall hedge-fund employee average at ~$450k–$520k (2017–2023). Anything above ~$1M for an analyst appears only in anonymous forum anecdotes about Tiger Cubs, not in surveys.

### Inferences
- The only recruiter surveys that explicitly segment by fund size (Glocap) put the *average* entry-level analyst at a large (>$4B) fund at $335k (2013), $372k (2014), $360k (2015). Nothing comparable is public for 2016–2025; Odyssey ($450k–$520k all-level averages, 2017–18) and eFC ($487k average, 2023) imply the all-level average moved only modestly over the decade.
- SumZero's medians are much lower than Glocap's large-fund averages because SumZero's sample is dominated by sub-$250M funds (which pay ~$100k less) and because SumZero reports medians while Glocap reports averages at a specified fund size; the SumZero 75th–99th percentile growth figures ($30k–$215k per year of experience) are the better proxy for a top-tier single-manager career path.
- "Year 1" at a Tiger Cub typically means a hire with 2+2 (banking + megafund PE) experience, i.e., 4 years of prior work, not a post-undergraduate hire (WSO route descriptions below), so SumZero's 3–5-year band ($220k median / $310k 75th pct, 2012–16 data) is the closer survey comparison, and still clearly below the forum-reported Tiger Cub figures.

### Gaps
- No Odyssey hedge fund medians for 2019–2025 were found (its public reports after 2018 are private equity/private credit); the 2025 "public equities" survey results were announced but not located.
- No Institutional Investor/Glocap Hedge Fund Compensation Report edition after the 2016 edition (2015 data) was located.
- Heidrick publishes no analyst/PM survey; a Heidrick "2025 US Global Markets Compensation Survey" is referenced by a career site but was not found.
- Selby Jennings' hedge-fund (as opposed to traditional) bands could not be read because the PDF host was blocked.
- eFC's by-experience hedge fund pay articles are paywalled/blocked.
- One outlet in the 2013 search results quoted $353,000 (vs CNBC's $335,000) for the 2013 entry-level figure; the source of the $353k figure could not be identified.

## Key Question 2: What specific reported data points exist for Tiger Cub analyst pay (Tiger Global, Coatue, Lone Pine, D1, Viking), including 2021 boom and 2022 bust effects?

### Takeaway
No Bloomberg, WSJ, FT or Business Insider article quantifying analyst bonuses at any Tiger Cub was found; all Tiger-Cub-specific analyst pay figures are anonymous forum claims (Tiger Global ~$1M/yr guarantees for 2+2 juniors in 2021–22, later "mid/high six figures"; seasoned senior analysts $1–2M average, $3–5M in exceptional years). The only hard, filed numbers for comparable single-manager firms are TCI (16 equity partners averaged $10.17M in FY to March 2025), Viking's UK LLP (2019 filings per a forum reading), Pershing Square's S-1, and Fundsmith LLP accounts.

### Inferences
- The forum-reported Tiger Global "$1M/yr guarantee" (2021–22) and subsequent retreat to "mid/high six figures" is consistent in direction with the 2022 drawdown and fee cut, but no press source confirms either number.
- TCI's filed $10.17M average for 16 equity partners (FY to Mar 2025, a record-profit year) is the strongest filed evidence that senior investment staff at an elite concentrated single-manager can average eight figures; it also shows the partner pool is tiny (16 people, ~half PMs) relative to ~50 staff.
- Pershing's structure (first 5 points of net return retained by the firm, the rest of performance fees to a member-compensation vehicle; Ackman at 24.9% of ManagementCo voting interests with others at ~15% each) indicates that at Pershing the investment team's pay is formula-linked to firm-wide performance fees and that founder economics are shared more broadly than the "founder keeps 70%+" forum model.

## Key Question 3: What is a reasonable payout ratio (investment-staff comp ÷ total fee revenue) at a single-manager fund, and what share goes to the founder vs the team? How are pools constructed (management vs incentive fees, P&L % vs discretionary, deferral, partner equity)?

### Takeaway
Public comparables show total comp ratios of ~46–48% of net revenue at a listed diversified manager (Man Group 2024–2026) and bonus expense of ~19–22% of revenue plus salaries of ~18–20% of management fees at Och-Ziff (2013–2015); at founder-owned single managers the filed evidence (TCI, Fundsmith, Pershing) shows the founder's take is set structurally (fixed profit share, preferred fee, ownership %) and routinely exceeds the entire non-founder pool in strong years. Analyst bonuses at single managers are overwhelmingly discretionary, funded from incentive fees with management fees as the backstop; explicit P&L percentages (2–5% of attributed P&L) appear only in forum reports.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- At Fundsmith in FY2018, partners took 79% of total personnel pay (£20.96m of £26.54m); by FY2025 Smith's single share (£23.3m) was 59% of LLP profit (£39.6m), so the founder's share alone is roughly 1.5x everything paid to all other partners combined. This is a long-only boutique, but it illustrates how "fixed proportion of profit" structures concentrate economics.
- At TCI the ratio is more extreme: Hohn's £575m (2022) was roughly 3.5x the entire 16-partner pool at FY2025's $10.17m average (~$163m), and in the latest year his $53m was about a third of that pool — i.e., the founder's share swings with performance-fee timing (high-water mark) far more than the partners' pool.
- A workable range for "investment-staff comp ÷ total revenue" at a profitable single manager is therefore well below the 46–48% total-comp ratio of a listed diversified manager; the Och-Ziff data (bonus ~20% of revenue, salaries ~18% of management fees) suggest ~25–35% all-in for staff excluding founder economics, with the founder's residual being the balancing item. This is an inference, not a surveyed figure.
- The forum "founder keeps all management fees + 70% of incentive fees" model is consistent with Pershing's documented "preferred" fee retention and the Fundsmith/TCI founder shares, but the exact split is firm-specific and undisclosed at the Tiger Cubs.

- No survey quantifies payout ratio for single-manager L/S funds by AUM tier; Citi's 2013–2015 reports include compensation inside "management company expenses" but the by-AUM comp split could not be read (blocked).
- Pershing's actual profit-sharing partner compensation dollar line (Q2 2026 release) could not be parsed.
- No disclosed data on partner equity at Tiger Global, Coatue, Lone Pine, Viking, D1 or Soroban; Form ADV Schedule A lists ≥5% owners but was not retrieved.
- AIMA's "In Concert" paper on alignment (deferred comp, staff co-investment, clawbacks) was blocked. — [AIMA](https://www.aima.org/static/uploaded/df23fb37-78ff-4d57-88859a7d70167d02.pdf)

## Key Question 4: What are credible estimates of overhead (non-comp) costs as a percentage of revenue or in dollars for funds of $2B, $10B, $30B, $50B?

### Takeaway
The only systematic by-AUM cost benchmarks are Citi Prime Finance's 2012–2015 surveys (break-even ~$250–330M AUM; management-company operating margins ~69 bps of AUM at $500M, ~82 bps at $900M; industry average 67 bps in 2013) and AIMA's small-manager break-even series ($86M in 2017, $64M in 2022, ~$83M in 2026); no public source gives non-comp overhead for $10–50B single managers, so those must be inferred from filed comparables (Pershing, Man, Fundsmith).

### Cited Findings
- Citi Prime Finance 2012 Hedge Fund Business Expense Survey (80+ firms, $186bn AUM): managers need ~$250m AUM to break even; expense totals covered marketing, IR, risk/compliance, operations/technology and business management but excluded compensation of investment personnel. — [Opalesque](https://www.opalesque.com/644717/Hedge_funds_must_reach_in_AUM_to471.html); [HedgeCo](http://www.hedgeco.net/news/12/2012/2012-hedge-fund-business-expense-survey.html); [WealthBriefingAsia](https://www.wealthbriefingasia.com/article.php/Hedge-Fund-Managers-Need-At-Least-$250-Million-Of-AuM-To-Break-Even-_dash_-Citi) [survey]
- Citi 2013 edition (124 firms): after paying expenses, funds with $500M AUM realize operating margins of 69 bps, rising to 82 bps at $900M; break-even ~$300M; management-company costs in this edition include third-party expenses, investment-team salaries and total compensation for support and business-management personnel (so editions are not directly comparable). — [Funds Society](https://www.fundssociety.com/en/news/alternatives/today-a-hedge-fund-needs-at-least-300-million-in-assets-just-to-break-even/); chart "Operating Margins Based on Average Management Company Fees & Expenses (by Fund Size)" — [Hedge Fund Insight](http://www.hedgefundinsight.org/chart-of-the-day-operating-margins-across-hedge-fund-aum-range/) [survey]
- Citi January 2015 release (149 firms, $581bn): industry-average operating margin from management-company fee revenue 67 bps in 2013, forecast 74 bps for 2014; ~$100M managers ran an operating deficit of 86 bps in 2013 and 69 bps in 2014; break-even $330M (2013) and $310M (2014); Citi forecast a 30% decline in industry profits for 2014; survey retitled to focus on "industry margins, profitability and valuations." — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20150129005028/en/Citi-Survey-Forecasts-30-Decline-Hedge-Fund) [survey]

- AIMA/Cowen "Emerging Stronger" (Q4 2021 questionnaire, published 2022): average break-even fell from $86m (2017) to $64m (2022), a 25% decline, per a secondary write-up; AIMA's later release says break-even has risen since 2022 but remains below pre-pandemic levels; a 2026 AIMA/Marex figure of ~$82.9m is cited by the same secondary site. — [AIMA 2022 PDF](https://www.aima.org/static/f4f172b2-e027-4be2-8776c8e4aa0352d9/emerging-stronger.pdf); [AIMA press release](https://www.aima.org/article/press-release-how-are-emerging-hedge-fund-managers-attracting-capital-and-keeping-their-edge.html); [Vibe Advisors summary](https://www.vibe-advisors.com/hedge-fund-breakeven-aum/) [survey via secondary]
- Pershing Square FY2025: revenue ~$762.5M, GAAP net income ~$249.8M, employee comp & benefits ~$20.2M (excluding the performance-fee-funded member vehicle), ~$20.7B fee-paying AUM. — [SEC S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2026053/000114036126008560/ny20040230x14_s1.htm) [filing]
- Fundsmith LLP FY to March 2024: turnover ~£280m against LLP profit £46.4m, with ~59 employees (2023) and £36bn AUM (Dec 2023). — [Citywire](https://citywire.com/new-model-adviser/news/terry-smith-takes-pay-cut-but-could-still-net-140m-after-tough-run/a2454096); [Wikipedia: Fundsmith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundsmith) [filing via press]
- Man Group: core comp $675M in 2025; comp ratio 48%; target core PBT margin 30–40%. — [Man Group](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/man-group--emg/financial-results-for-the-year-ended-2025/9447616) [filing]

### Inferences
- Break-even AUM × fee rate gives the implied fixed cost base of a small manager: $64m at 2% ≈ $1.3m/yr; $300m at ~1.5–2% ≈ $4.5–6m/yr (2013–14 Citi). These are start-up/small-manager overheads, not $5–50B overheads.
- For a $20B fee-paying activist (Pershing), revenue of $762.5M vs net income of $249.8M means ~67% of revenue is absorbed by all expenses (including the performance-fee-funded partner vehicle and taxes); non-comp overhead cannot be isolated from the excerpts.
- Fundsmith's £280m turnover vs £46m LLP profit is unexplained by a 59-person cost base; the difference likely includes charges to affiliated entities (Fundsmith Investment Services Ltd), which is consistent with Citywire's ~£140m total-take estimate; the LLP accounts should be read directly before using these as overhead ratios.
- Citi's 67–82 bps "operating margin on management fees" at $500M–$900M implies total management-company expenses (including investment-staff salaries) of roughly 60–100+ bps of AUM at that size; by $5–50B, fixed costs scale far slower than fees, so non-comp overhead as a share of revenue should be well below 20%, but no source in this research states a figure.

### Gaps
- No public survey of non-comp overhead for $2B/$10B/$30B/$50B single managers was found; Citi's by-AUM chart values (which extended to larger bands) could not be read.
- No data on pass-through expenses (research, data, technology charged to LPs) at single managers vs multi-managers; the planned search on Goldman/BI pass-through surveys could not be run.
- EY/KPMG/AIMA cost-of-compliance surveys were not retrieved.
- Man Group's non-comp cost line was not captured.

## Key Question 7: How do Institutional Investor's Rich List and Forbes compute founder earnings, and why does that overstate "money taken out of the comp pool"?

### Takeaway
II counts each manager's gains on personal capital invested in their funds plus an estimated share of management and performance fees, with no high-water-mark test applied to personal-capital gains; Forbes likewise includes the manager's personal gain/loss on fund interests but deducts firm operating costs and profit splits and excludes gains on ownership of the firm itself. In recent years personal-capital gains have dominated (e.g., Hohn 2023, Edelman 2023), so Rich List totals mostly measure investment returns on the founder's own money, not fee revenue diverted from the staff pool.

### Cited Findings
- II methodology: "we count an individual's gains on their own capital invested in their funds plus their share of the fees"; managers below their high-water mark still get credit for personal-capital gains because the list is a snapshot; the 2025 list (published March 2026) had a record $470 million minimum, the top 25 earned a combined $36.65 billion, and Chris Hohn topped it at $4.9 billion, a record; "the more personal capital managers have invested in their own funds, the easier it is to make the list." — [Institutional Investor 25th Rich List](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/hedge-funds/rich-list-institutional-investors-25th-annual-ranking-highest-earning-hedge); [Yahoo Finance release](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/institutional-investor-unveils-25th-annual-113000479.html)
- II 23rd list (2023 earnings): "Most of [Hohn's] earnings stemmed from gains generated by his own capital invested in the fund. He received a small amount of fees as TCI had to hit its high-water mark before it could charge a performance fee"; Joseph Edelman qualified "thanks solely to gains on his own capital." — [II 23rd Rich List](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2d0quhk4ghsahyyia26m8/corner-office/the-23nd-annual-ranking-of-the-highest-earning-hedge-fund-managers)
- II 22nd list (2022 earnings): Hohn "is one of ten hedge fund managers who lost money in 2022 — large sums in many cases — but still wound up qualifying for the Rich List this year." — [II 22nd Rich List](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bstpd4t08pc8xwj4we80/corner-office/the-rich-list-the-22nd-annual-ranking-of-the-highest-earning-hedge-fund-managers)
- II 2019 release: personal-capital returns "often play a major role in the ability of managers to qualify for the ranking." — [GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2019/04/30/1812364/0/en/Bridgewater-Associates-Founder-Ray-Dalio-Tops-Institutional-Investor-s-Rich-List-as-the-World-s-Highest-Earning-Hedge-Fund-Manager.html)
- Older II description: fee figures rest on "knowledgeable estimates of their share of management and performance fees," with personal investment gains added where calculable. — [II "The Rich List"](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/b1f61584d2sl0q/The-Rich-List)
- Forbes methodology (2012–2019 lists): "Forbes examined hedge fund returns and worked to understand the fee and ownership structure of a wide array of money management firms"; "Earnings figures include the personal gain or loss of each manager's interest in their funds"; firm operating costs and profit splits are deducted; figures are pretax; "Gains or losses from ownership in the investment firms themselves or from investments held outside of the managed investment pools were excluded"; managers who "now mostly or even exclusively manage their own money" are included. — [Forbes 2019](https://www.forbes.com/sites/nathanvardi/2019/03/20/the-highest-earning-hedge-fund-managers-and-traders/); [Forbes 2016](https://www.forbes.com/sites/nathanvardi/2016/02/24/the-25-highest-earning-hedge-fund-managers-and-traders-2/); [Forbes 2012](https://www.forbes.com/sites/nathanvardi/2012/03/01/the-40-highest-earning-hedge-fund-managers-3/)
- Cross-check from filings: Hohn's filed pay was $53m (£42m) in the latest Companies House year vs $346m in 2023, while II credited him with $4.9 billion for 2025 — the gap is personal-capital gains plus performance fees not yet distributed through the UK entities. — [City AM](https://www.cityam.com/chris-hohn-hedge-fund-bosss-pay-slumps-to-just-42m/); [II 25th Rich List](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/hedge-funds/rich-list-institutional-investors-25th-annual-ranking-highest-earning-hedge)

### Inferences
- Because II credits personal-capital gains even when the fund is below its high-water mark (and therefore earning no performance fee), a founder can "earn" billions in a year in which the firm's incentive-fee pool, and hence the staff bonus pool, is near zero; Rich List figures should not be read as fee revenue the founder chose not to share.
- Forbes's deduction of operating costs and profit splits makes its figures closer to "founder's net share of firm profit plus own-capital gains," but it still mixes investment returns on personal money with fee income; neither series separates the two in published tables.

### Gaps
- No standalone methodology page from II or Forbes was retrievable; Forbes has not clearly published an annual hedge fund earnings list since 2019 in the results found.
- Tiger Cub founders' Rich List figures (Coleman, Laffont, Mandel, Halvorsen, Sundheim) and their own-capital vs fee split were not captured (II pages blocked; search budget exhausted).
