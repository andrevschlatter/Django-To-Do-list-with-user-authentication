## Key question 1: Fundsmith LLP Companies House figures FY2016–FY2025 and press-reported pay for Terry Smith

### Takeaway
Fundsmith LLP's UK accounts (year to 31 March) show turnover rising from £41.6m (FY2016) to a ~£280m plateau (FY2021–FY2024), with roughly two-thirds to 72% of turnover passed each year to the Mauritius affiliate Fundsmith Investment Services Ltd (FISL, majority-owned by Smith, accounts not public), leaving LLP profit that peaked at £58.2m (FY2022) and fell to £39.6m (FY2025); the "member with the largest entitlement" (understood to be Smith) received £1.6m (FY2016) → £8.1m → £12m → £16.2m → £29.7m → £35.7m → £36.4m (FY2022 record) → £31.1–31.2m → £27.8–27.9m → £23.3m (FY2025), but press estimates of his total take including FISL run at £125m–£190m a year in FY2020–FY2024.

### Inferences
- Share of LLP turnover passed to Mauritius: FY2018 72% (88.4/122.8), FY2019 70%, FY2020 68%, FY2021 67%, FY2023 ~67% (185.7/278 if that charge belongs to FY2023). The LLP retains ~28–33% of fee revenue, out of which it pays London staff and overhead and then distributes profit to members.
- Implied LLP costs other than the FISL charge (turnover − FISL − profit): FY2018 £13.4m (incl. employee pay £5.6m); FY2019 £23.1m; FY2020 £23.9m (incl. staff bill £11.8m); FY2021 £33.5m (incl. staff bill £16.9m); FY2023 £42.4m. Non-staff overhead therefore ran roughly £8–17m a year.
- Implied FY2022 turnover (not reported in excerpts) is at least £252m + £58.2m + ~£35m of other costs ≈ £345m+, consistent with firm AUM peaking in late 2021/early 2022.
- Non-Smith member pool (LLP profit minus largest allocation): FY2017 £6.1m across 4 others (~£1.5m each); FY2018 £8.96m (partners total £20.96m − £12m); FY2019 £10.2m; FY2020 £18.8m across 7 others (~£2.7m each); FY2021 £22.0m across 8 others (~£2.75m each ≈ $3.5m); FY2022 £21.8m; FY2023 £18.7m; FY2024 £18.5m; FY2025 £16.3m.
- Employee (non-member) average pay: FY2017 ~£4.9m/14 ≈ £350k; FY2018 £5.6m/~17 ≈ £330k; FY2020 £9.99m/19 ≈ £525k (of which ~£385k variable); FY2021 £16.9m/26 ≈ £650k (staff bill incl. employer costs). These averages cover all London staff (operations, distribution, compliance), not only analysts.
- If FISL runs at an 80–90% margin (lean Mauritius operation) and Smith's economic share is 61% (press) — the SEC exhibit's 77.83% via Eighth Wonder Ltd would be higher — then Smith's FISL income ≈ FY2018 £43–49m, FY2019 £57–64m, FY2020 £76–86m, FY2021 £92–103m, FY2022 £123–138m, FY2023 £91–102m; adding the LLP allocation gives totals of roughly £55–60m (FY2018), £73–80m (FY2019), £106–115m (FY2020), £128–139m (FY2021), £159–175m (FY2022), £122–133m (FY2023) — in line with the Citywire/Telegraph estimates of £125m–£190m.

### Gaps
- FY2016 and FY2017 FISL charges (only total admin costs £38.7m/£67.5m reported); FY2022 and FY2025 turnover; FY2024 and FY2025 FISL charges; number of members in FY2016, FY2018, FY2019, FY2022–FY2025; average employee numbers and staff costs for FY2019, FY2022–FY2025 — the filed PDFs on Companies House would settle these but could not be fetched.
- The £185.7m FISL charge is attributed by one search excerpt to the FY2024 coverage and by another to late-2023 (FY2023) coverage; the ratio to turnover (67%) fits either year. Treat the year as unconfirmed.
- Outlets differ by £0.1m on FY2023 (£31.1m vs £31.2m) and FY2024 (£27.8m vs £27.9m) allocations — rounding, not substantive.
- The "£17.5bn" FUM in the FY2025 accounts coverage conflicts with Fundsmith's own "£24bn as at 31 December 2025" ([Fundsmith](https://www.fundsmith.com/)); the LLP figure may cover only the UK OEIC or a March 2025 date. Not resolved.
- FISL's profit, costs and dividends are not public; "dividends" to Smith cannot be verified. The SEC exhibit on FISL ownership (see Q2) names a foundation-owned vehicle, not Smith, so Smith's effective FISL percentage is a press estimate.

## Key question 2: Fundsmith AUM, Fundsmith Equity Fund net returns, fees, team and ownership, 2016–2025

### Takeaway
Fundsmith Equity Fund (UK) grew from £9.1bn (end-2016) to roughly £23bn (start-2021) and a peak of ~£26–29bn (late 2021/April 2022), then shrank to ~£22.5–23.3bn (end-2024) and ~£15.3bn (end-2025) as returns lagged (T Acc: +28.2%, +22.0%, +2.2%, +25.6%, +18.3%, +22.1%, −13.8%, +12.4%, +8.9%, +0.8% for 2016–2025); the firm charges a flat 0.9% (I class) / 1.0% (T class) AMC with no performance fee, is run by a core investment team of about five (Smith, Robins and three analysts), and is majority-owned by Smith with Robins holding 13.83% of the Mauritius manager.
