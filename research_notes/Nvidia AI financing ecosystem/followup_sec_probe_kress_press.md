### Inferences
- The three reports are consistent but thin. Counting sources: WSJ "people familiar" (number not given in any relay), Reuters two, NYT "people briefed" (number not given). It is possible that the same individuals briefed more than one outlet, so three reports are not three independent confirmations.
- "Accounting" in the Reuters headline is broader than "disclosures" in the WSJ report, but Reuters' own caveat means nothing ties the probe to guarantees, revenue recognition, investment marks or Megaspeed. Singapore is named only as context by all three.
- The FT's "$104 billion, roughly half" figure and the 10-Q's "22%" measure different things (legal-entity revenue versus billing location). A report writer should not present them as contradictory.

### Gaps
- Not established by any source: number and description of WSJ and NYT sources; SEC division or office; formal order versus informal inquiry; subpoenas or document requests to Nvidia or its auditor; start date; topics; whether former executives have been contacted.
- Whether the Reuters article mentions the 2022 and 2003 matters could not be confirmed.
- No Bloomberg, FT, CNBC or The Information report adding original facts about the SEC probe (as opposed to relaying WSJ/Reuters/NYT) surfaced in the searches run. Absence is weak evidence given the search limits.
- Nvidia has made no statement on the SEC reports that I could find; the first formal acknowledgement would normally be the Q3 FY27 10-Q, due about Dec 4, 2026.

## 2. Huang's exit: on-record reasons, the board investigation, his statements, Congress, Treasury and Commerce

### Takeaway
On the record, the chair has given two reasons: personal behaviour and "a lack of candor" inconsistent with the code of conduct. Reporting that the decisive issue was a year of untruthful explanations rests on unnamed people (Wired, Nov 20). The board investigation (Wachtell and Joon Kim) continues and its scope and findings are not public.
