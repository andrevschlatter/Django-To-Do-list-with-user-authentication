## A. The reported SEC probe: what is and is not known

### Takeaway
Two anonymous-source reports (WSJ, Nov 17, 2026; Reuters and NYT, Nov 20, 2026) say the SEC has a civil investigation of Nvidia that covers whether disclosures misled investors and, per Reuters/NYT, its accounting; the stage, start date, the office running it, whether there is a formal order or subpoena, and the specific accounting topics are all unreported. Nvidia has not confirmed an SEC inquiry in any filing: its Nov 17 documents mention only "the ongoing investigation by an independent committee" of its board and generic "government and regulatory inquiries", and the Q3 FY27 10-Q that would carry any formal disclosure has not been filed (it is not late: the deadline is about Dec 4, 2026).

### Inferences
*Everything in this subsection is my own reasoning, not reported fact.*

**What the record supports saying**
- The safest formulation is: "an SEC investigation has been reported by three outlets citing unnamed people; the SEC declined to comment; Nvidia has not confirmed it; the accounting topics are not known." The Nov 20 Reuters headline says accounting is "a focus", but its own text (as relayed) says Reuters could not determine which accounting issues.
- The Nov 17 forward-looking language is the closest thing to company acknowledgement. It confirms a board-committee investigation and "government and regulatory inquiries" in general terms. That phrase also covers the California subpoena and the congressional requests, so it is not confirmation of an SEC matter.
- The first place a formal acknowledgement would normally appear is the Q3 FY27 10-Q (legal proceedings note, risk factors, and the disclosure-controls conclusion that Kress must now sign as both principal executive and principal financial officer).

**Ranked hypotheses on what an SEC accounting/disclosure inquiry could examine (speculative; each with an innocent explanation)**
1. *Disclosure about Megaspeed, Singapore and the geographic-basis change (most supported by the reporting).* Why it ranks first: it is the only topic the reporting names, and the change happened one quarter after the NYT's October 2025 Megaspeed story. Innocent explanation: ASC 280 allows geographic attribution on any reasonable disclosed basis; Nvidia disclosed the change, recast prior periods, and had already told readers for three quarters that Singapore was an invoicing hub with "over 99%" of controlled compute billed there going to U.S.-based customers. Headquarters is arguably more informative than billing address.
2. *Disclosure controls around what the CEO knew (code-of-conduct and counterparty relationships).* Why: the 2022 SEC order against Nvidia was a disclosure-controls case (section G), and the board reportedly cited "lack of candor". Innocent explanation: a CEO's personal conduct is not itself a financial-reporting matter, and the board acted within days.
3. *Completeness and timing of disclosure on guarantees, backstops and "financing arrangements".* Why: the FY26 10-K said "We have not entered into any financing arrangements" (Feb 25, 2026) and the Q2 FY27 10-Q six months later describes "financing arrangements with certain investment-grade customers", a capacity-purchase backstop and a $105 billion guarantee (section C); press reports say much larger guarantees were under negotiation in July 2026. Innocent explanation: the FY26 statement may have been accurate when made; the SB Energy deal was disclosed by 8-K on the day it was signed and in the 10-Q as a subsequent event; unsigned negotiations generally need not be disclosed.
4. *Accounting classification and valuation of guarantees.* Why: Nvidia treats lease guarantees as "credit derivatives" with "not significant" fair value rather than describing an ASC 460 guarantee liability; warrants received for guarantees are recognised with "the corresponding benefit substantially deferred". These are judgment-heavy. Innocent explanation: the fair value of a guarantee that has not yet become effective, is capped, declines over time and is backed by escrow and indemnities can legitimately be small; PwC audited the FY26 treatment.
5. *Valuation of non-marketable equity and gains recognised in income.* Why: $9.9 billion of cumulative unrealised gains on private holdings and $25.5 billion of equity gains in nine months of FY27, some from funding rounds in which Nvidia itself participated; a first large impairment ($1.8 billion) arrived in Q3 FY27. Innocent explanation: the ASC 321 measurement alternative requires mark-ups on observable transactions; Nvidia excludes these gains from non-GAAP results and added non-marketable securities to its critical accounting estimates in the FY26 10-K.
6. *Revenue from investees and from customers whose purchases Nvidia supports (so-called circularity).* Why: Nvidia first said in May 2026 that some investees "may indirectly purchase or use our products in the cloud", and AI clouds "procure our data center infrastructure products" while Nvidia commits to buy their capacity. The question would be whether any consideration payable to a customer should reduce revenue (ASC 606). Innocent explanation: the arrangements are disclosed; separate transactions at fair value are accounted for separately; no restatement or control weakness has been reported.
7. *Non-GAAP presentation.* Why: the 2025 comment letter already questioned the H20 exclusion. Innocent explanation: Nvidia dropped that exclusion two quarters later and, from Q1 FY27, stopped excluding stock-based compensation, which makes non-GAAP more conservative.
8. *Insider trading/10b5-1 plans (least supported).* Why listed: insider selling was large in 2025 to 2026 (section D). Innocent explanation: sales were disclosed, most under 10b5-1 plans adopted months earlier; Huang's Form 4s show no open-market sales in 2026.

## B. Equity investments: accounting, balances, gains and commitments

### Takeaway
Nvidia's equity portfolio went from $77 million of private stakes (Jan 2020) to about $110 billion (Oct 2026) with almost all of the growth in the 15 months from Aug 2025. The accounting policy (cost less impairment, marked up on observable transactions; public stakes at fair value) has been stable in substance since FY2021, gains have always been excluded from non-GAAP results, and the filings name very few investees and have never treated any as a related party. Equity-method stakes and variable interest entities first appear in May and August 2026.

### Cited Findings

**Policy wording, first appearances and changes**
- [FILING/ON-RECORD] [PRE] FY2018 baseline: no investment policy note; the only reference is in MD&A, "sales or impairments of investments in non-affiliated companies". FY2019: "$12 million unrealized gains from non-affiliated investments". — [FY18 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581018000010/nvda-2018x10k.htm); [FY19 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581019000023/nvda-2019x10k.htm)
- [FILING/ON-RECORD] [PRE] First policy note, Q2 FY21 10-Q (filed Aug 19, 2020): "Non-marketable equity investments in privately-held companies are recorded at fair value on a non-recurring basis only if an impairment or observable price adjustment occurs in the period with changes in fair value recorded through net income... The estimated fair value is based on quantitative and qualitative factors including subsequent financing activities by the investee." Balance $110 million. — [Q2 FY21 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581020000147/nvda-20200726.htm)
- [FILING/ON-RECORD] [PRE] FY24 10-K (filed Feb 21, 2024) is the first to use the term "measurement alternative" and to give a roll-forward and cumulative gain/loss table: investments "are measured at cost minus impairment, if any, and are adjusted for changes resulting from observable price changes in orderly transactions for an identical or similar investment in the same issuer, or the measurement alternative." It also adds a risk factor: "Our investment portfolio contains industry sector concentration risks". — [FY24 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581024000029/nvda-20240128.htm)
- [FILING/ON-RECORD] [PRE] FY26 10-K (filed Feb 25, 2026): non-marketable equity securities become a critical accounting estimate for the first time ("We have critical accounting estimates in the areas of inventories, income taxes, non-marketable equity securities, and revenue recognition"; the FY25 list was "inventories, income taxes, and revenue recognition"). New valuation wording: "valued under the measurement alternative applying valuation methods based on observable transactions for similar investments of the same issuer and unobservable inputs such as volatility, expected time to liquidity, risk free rate and security-specific rights and obligations." New sentences: "We assess impairment quarterly based on qualitative and quantitative factors, including the investee's operating performance and market trends" and "The Company assesses its investments for significant influence to determine the appropriate method of accounting, including application of the equity method. Equity method investments were not material." Non-marketable equity securities were moved out of "other assets" to their own balance-sheet line. — [FY26 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000021/nvda-20260125.htm)
- [FILING/ON-RECORD] [PRE] Q1 FY27 10-Q (filed May 20, 2026): first equity-method disclosure, "We have $1.0 billion of investments in infrastructure funds accounted for using the equity method as of April 26, 2026. Our maximum loss exposure under these investments, including invested and future committed amounts, was $2.3 billion". ### Inferences
*My own reasoning.*
- Equity gains were a large share of GAAP profit in the first half of FY27: $23.7 billion of pre-tax gains against GAAP net income of about $118 billion for the half (65.3 + 59.7 for Q2 and Q3 implies Q1 of about 58.3; nine-month total 183.3). Because Nvidia strips them from non-GAAP earnings, the headline non-GAAP numbers do not depend on them.
- The measurement alternative creates gains only when there is an "observable" transaction. Where Nvidia is itself a participant in the financing round that sets the new price, the judgment about whether the transaction is orderly and for a "similar" security is the sensitive point. The filings acknowledge that adjustments "may require use of unobservable inputs". This is a standard audit focus, not an indication of error.
- The move of part of the portfolio into Level 2 (warrants, convertible preferred in public companies) and Level 3 (warrants received) means a growing slice is model-valued.
- The equity method and VIE disclosures show Nvidia concluded it has significant influence over some "infrastructure financiers" but does not control them. Consolidation conclusions for vehicles that buy Nvidia systems and lease them to Nvidia-backed tenants are an obvious area for regulator questions; the innocent reading is that these are minority fund interests with capped exposure ($4.7 billion).
- The first sizeable impairment ($1.8 billion in Q3 FY27) arriving in the same quarter as the CEO change may be read by critics as "kitchen-sinking" and by supporters as evidence the quarterly impairment process works. The filings do not say which holding was impaired.

### Gaps
- Stake-by-stake carrying values, the identity of the impaired holding, and how specific customer-investees (OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI/SpaceX, CoreWeave, Nebius, Lambda, Crusoe) are valued are not disclosed.
- Q3 FY27 figures are from unaudited earnings exhibits; the 10-Q note may add detail (equity method, VIE exposure, warrants).
- No filing quantifies revenue from investees.

## C. Guarantees, backstops and capacity purchase commitments

### Takeaway
Nvidia had no financial guarantees until the Q3 FY26 10-Q (filed Nov 19, 2025), which disclosed an $860 million lease guarantee; maximum exposure then rose to $3.5 billion (Jan 2026), $108.5 billion (including the $105 billion OpenAI/SB Energy guarantees signed Aug 17, 2026) and $118.0 billion (Oct 25, 2026). All guarantees are off balance sheet, described as credit derivatives with insignificant fair value, with "no payments" made to date. The disclosure format changed in almost every one of the last five filings, and the third-party "$530 billion" figure equals the sum of three Nvidia tables at July 26, 2026, most of which is supply purchases rather than guarantees.

*My own reasoning.*

### Inferences
*My own reasoning.*
- The Nov 19, 2025 filing is the single most changed document in the series: it introduced the first guarantee, the first investment-commitment figure, the geographic re-basing that removed Singapore, the China preliminary finding, and the "regulatory and government inquiries and investigations" wording. The innocent explanation for the last item is that it reflects matters already disclosed elsewhere in the same filing (competition requests, China's review, export-control assistance to authorities) and the Commerce request for Megaspeed sales records in May 2025, tidied into the contingency note. It does not show that an SEC inquiry existed then; nothing in the public record dates the SEC inquiry.
- Precedent from Nvidia's own history: the 2018 to 2022 SEC inquiry was not disclosed in any periodic report until it settled. So the absence of SEC language in the FY27 10-Qs is not evidence that no inquiry existed when they were filed, and equally is not evidence that one did.
- What to look for in the Q3 FY27 10-Q: (a) a named SEC matter, formal order or subpoena in the litigation note; (b) the California subpoena and congressional requests; (c) the November 2026 securities class action; (d) any change to "effective" in the disclosure-controls conclusion; (e) whether the loss-contingency paragraph still says outcomes "will not have a material adverse effect"; (f) the nature of the $107 million legal settlement.
