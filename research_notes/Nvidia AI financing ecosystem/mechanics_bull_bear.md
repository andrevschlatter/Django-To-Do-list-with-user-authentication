## What financing tools does Nvidia use, and how do they work contractually?

### Takeaway
Five tools are visible: equity stakes in customers (about $99bn at July 2026), capacity purchase/rent-back agreements ($32bn of "neocloud agreements" at October 2026, down from $36bn), revenue-floor backstops with a revenue share, lease and credit-support guarantees ($118bn maximum gross exposure, $105bn of it one OpenAI campus in Ohio), and a proposed 25% residual-value support inside the $500bn third-party financing platforms. Nvidia says it has paid nothing under any guarantee and that guarantees only become effective after the partner has financed, built and installed the capacity.

## What is the strongest bear case?

### Takeaway
The best bear argument is not that Nvidia will be bankrupted by its guarantees but that its marginal demand was manufactured by its own credit: remove the guarantor and the weakest buyers cannot borrow, which is what Rowan said on record and what the LTV and bond-yield moves show. ## What happened at Lucent, Nortel, Cisco and Motorola, and where does the analogy hold or break?

### Takeaway
Lucent's widely quoted $8.1bn appears to be $6.7bn of credit commitments plus $1.4bn of customer-debt guarantees at 30 Sep 2000, with only about $1.3bn drawn; the damage came when junk-rated carriers lost market access and the vendor became lender of last resort. The analogy holds on incentives, opacity and dependence on customers' access to credit, and breaks on vendor balance sheet, top-customer quality, and guarantees-after-completion versus direct loans.

### Cited Findings
- Lucent, 30 Sep 2000 (10-K405): commitments or agreements to extend credit of about $6.7bn, of which about $3.3bn was not yet available for drawdown and about $1.3bn was advanced and outstanding; plus about $1.4bn of guarantees of customer debt [FILING/ON-RECORD] — [Lucent 10-K405 FY2000](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001006240/000095012300011855/y43690e10-k405.txt)

### Inferences
- $6.7bn + $1.4bn = $8.1bn, which is very likely the origin of the commonly cited Lucent figure; no source found states this derivation explicitly.
- The closest 2026 parallel to Winstar is not Nvidia's loss on a guarantee but the neocloud whose other lenders retreat (LTV cut, unsecured market shut), leaving the vendor to choose between extending more support and losing the sale. Kress's "disciplined" stance is the opposite choice from Lucent's.
- Collateral life differs in the wrong direction for the bulls: telecom gear and fibre were long-lived, GPUs are depreciated over four to six years and financed on three-year amortising loans.
