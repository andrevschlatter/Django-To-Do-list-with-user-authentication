## What exactly happened with Huang's departure, and what has been reported about a Kress–Huang disagreement?

### Takeaway
The board accepted Huang's resignation as President, CEO and director on Nov 11, 2026 (announced Nov 12) with no reason given in the 8-K; later on-record statements tie it to personal conduct and "lack of candor", and an independent board investigation, an SEC inquiry and other government probes are ongoing. The Kress–Huang financing dispute rests on anonymously sourced WSJ and Reuters reporting that I could not open; nobody at Nvidia has confirmed it on the record.

### Inferences
- The only signed, primary evidence consistent with the "Kress cut the deal" story is that the Aug 17 8-K (signed by Kress as CFO) caps the guarantee at $105B on 4.25 GW and leaves the other 3.8 GW as an option "exercisable in [Nvidia's] sole discretion". The press release issued the same day headlines OpenAI as customer for 8 GW. A deal that originally covered the whole campus and was cut to roughly half would look like this, but the filing does not say so.
- The board's on-record reasons are personal conduct and candor. Any claim that financing policy caused the exit is inference by commentators.

### Gaps
- I could not open the WSJ or Reuters articles, so I cannot confirm how many sources each relied on, whether Nvidia, Kress or Huang commented, or the exact $250B and $300B wording.
- No 8-K or other filing describes Huang's severance, equity treatment, or any consulting arrangement. The Q3 10-Q or a later 8-K may.
- No on-record statement from Kress about the August meeting was found.
- Findings of the independent investigation are not public.

### Inferences
- Q4 guidance is weaker than the headline suggests. $130.0B over 14 weeks is about $9.29B a week, against $8.91B a week in Q3 ($115.8B over 13 weeks): roughly +4% sequential on a per-week basis, after +20% in Q3. The midpoint gross margin guide (71.5%) is about 3.4 points below Q3.
- Receivables absorbed most of the growth. AR rose about $18.0B in the quarter while revenue rose $19.6B; AR equals 70% of a quarter's revenue. Nvidia attributes part of this to extended terms for "investment-grade" customers, which is vendor credit to its strongest buyers, separate from the guarantees given to its weakest.

### Inferences
- The written text and the spoken quote cover different instruments. The written "take effect only if the neoclouds secure financing" language sits under "Additional Commitments" and governs the $32B neocloud take-or-pay line. A delay there postpones the exposure; nothing in the filing says it cancels it.
- The 10-Q is not late in a legal sense. Nvidia is a large accelerated filer, so a 10-Q for a quarter ended Oct 25 is due 40 days later, about Dec 4, 2026 (my calculation from the SEC rule). (3) Timing: $530B is July, $118B is October. Calling the $530B "guarantees" overstates credit exposure; quoting only $118B understates customer support.
- **A defensible "customer-support" measure** sits between them. At Q2: $108.5B guarantees + $36B neocloud + $20B third-party leases = $164.5B, plus $25B unfunded equity = $189.5B. A 25% residual-value cap implies each dollar of Nvidia exposure could support about four dollars of project cost, which is how SemiAnalysis reaches very large modelled figures.

### Inferences
- The press numbers are internally consistent with the filing-derived price. A 4.3% fall worth $182B implies about $4.23T before the announcement and $4.05T after; −2% on Nov 13 gives about $3.97T, about $165 a share on 24.1B shares. About $151 around Nov 18–19 (Form 144), then −14% on Nov 20, gives about $130, matching the reported $128 and $3.1T. The implied Oct 6 peak is about $5.8T, or roughly $242 a share.
- The market reacted more to the accounting-inquiry report (−14%) than to the CEO's exit (−4.3% on the day) or to the results (−2%). The results day reaction suggests the beat was outweighed by the Q4 margin guide, the 14-week quarter and the missing 10-Q.
- From the peak, the reported loss is about 46% in six and a half weeks.
