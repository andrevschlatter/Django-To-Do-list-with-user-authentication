### Inferences

### Inferences
All figures below are my arithmetic from the cited numbers.

- **Check of the $239.9B quarter.** Amazon $77.3B + Alphabet $51.4B + Microsoft $39.4B (cash) + Meta $34.9B + Oracle $28.5B = $231.5B from filings I read. The remaining ~$8.4B is consistent with CoreWeave's cash purchases. Adding SpaceX ($19.6B) gives about $259B a quarter, or about $86B a month, for seven builders.
- **2026 base.** Big-four guidance midpoints: Microsoft ~$170B + Alphabet $200B + Amazon $220B + Meta $140B = ~$730B. Adding Oracle (~$92B, fiscal year offset), SpaceX (~$83–88B = $48.1B nine months + $35–40B Q4) and CoreWeave ($38B) gives roughly $945B for seven names. This is close to Dell'Oro's ">$1T worldwide".
- **Capital tied up = annual capex × months of delay ÷ 12.**

| 2027 capex base | Source of base | 1 month | 2 months | 3 months | 6 months |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $920B | consensus per Goldman | $77B | $153B | $230B | $460B |
| $1.1T | Goldman base | $92B | $183B | $275B | $550B |
| $1.32T | Morgan Stanley five companies | $110B | $220B | $330B | $660B |
| $1.6T | Morgan Stanley incl. leases | $133B | $267B | $400B | $800B |

- So "delays of a few months" → "hundreds of billions" holds on any current 2027 estimate, **if** the delay applies to the whole spending base and is additional to today's lag. Both conditions are assumptions. Only part of capex is paid before energisation (chips, long-lead equipment), and part of the lag already exists and is already financed.
- **Chips only.** Nvidia's Data Center revenue is about $36B a month ($107.6B ÷ 3). A three-month slip on chips alone ties up about $108B; six months about $215B. Add other accelerator vendors and memory and the figure is higher, but chips alone reach "hundreds of billions" only with delays beyond a quarter.
- **Carrying cost, as opposed to capital tied up.** At 8% (roughly what SpaceX and Oracle now pay), $300B idle for one quarter costs about $6B in interest. The cash requirement is large; the economic cost of a short delay is an order of magnitude smaller. This distinction matters for whether "shortage" is the right word.
- Microsoft's ~$170B is most plausibly calendar 2026: quarterly run-rate of $45.2B then >$50B fits a calendar-2026 total near $170B, whereas $170B for fiscal 2027 would imply spending falls in the second half, contradicting the direction of guidance. This is my inference; I did not read the transcript.

### Inferences
- **Nvidia is itself financing part of the gap.** At a 45-day DSO, Q3 receivables would have been about $57B ($115.8B × 45 ÷ 91). Actual was $81.0B. The roughly $24B difference is credit Nvidia has extended to investment-grade buyers in two quarters. My arithmetic.
- The gap is being pushed around the chain, not eliminated: Nvidia waits longer to be paid (64 days), Amazon takes up to a year from suppliers, Oracle collects prepayments from customers, and SpaceX pays lessors in advance. Who carries a delay depends on contract terms. The investment-grade buyers have shifted some of it to suppliers; the lab and neocloud end has not.
- No source measures the industry-wide lag in months. The best hard data point for 2026 slippage is Microsoft: ~$5B of a >$50B quarter (about 10%) slipped by at least a quarter.
- Evidence cuts both ways on whether slippage is "industry-wide": Microsoft and Oracle show delays; CoreWeave shows early deliveries. The delays are concentrated in third-party shells and power, not chips.

- No measured estimate of months from GPU payment to first revenue, and no sell-side or SemiAnalysis-style estimate of time from GPU delivery to rack energisation, was found.
- Nvidia's prepaid supply balances and any customer deposits for FY27 are not in the CFO commentary; the 10-Q for the October quarter was not located.
- The Guardian investigation said to put Microsoft at ~2.2 million installed AI chips in mid-2026 was not found; no 2026 report of GPUs sitting in warehouses was confirmed.
- CoreWeave's risk-factor language on developer delays and customer termination rights was not in the portion of the 10-Q I could read.
- The source of Musk's "consensus estimate" of 15 GW is unknown.

## 5. OpenAI: revenue, burn, commitments, funding, and the Nvidia arrangements

### Takeaway
The "$105 billion Nvidia-backed project" is a set of residual value guaranties, disclosed in an Nvidia 8-K of 17 August 2026, on OpenAI's 20-year leases at SB Energy's Ohio campus; it is not a loan guarantee on OpenAI debt and not a chip lease, and nothing has been paid under it. The September 2025 "$100 billion" Nvidia investment was abandoned and replaced by a $30 billion equity stake. ## 7. Expert reactions: is "shortage" the right word?

### Takeaway
I found no named credit analyst or economist responding directly to the 21 November post. ### Inferences
- Three different things are being called a "shortage": (1) a **timing gap** — cash out before cash in, which is what Musk's sentence describes; (2) a **cost-of-capital problem** — money is available at a price, from 4.85% for Microsoft to roughly 15% for CoreWeave unsecured; (3) **rationing** — some borrowers cannot raise at any sensible price (Musk's own October claim about neoclouds, and OpenAI cutting its round).
- The November issuance record (Microsoft $25B, Meta $15B + €9B + equity, Oracle $10B + $10B equity, SpaceX ~$44B, Anthropic ~$107B, OpenAI $11–15B) shows over $200B raised by AI names in three weeks during a sell-off. That is hard to square with "shortage" in the literal sense for large issuers. It fits "available but expensive, and closing for the weakest".
- "Working capital" is imprecise. Chips and buildings awaiting energisation are capital assets in progress, not inventory or receivables. The strictly working-capital pieces are visible elsewhere: Nvidia's receivables up to 64 days, Amazon's one-year supplier terms, Oracle's customer prepayments.
- Musk has a position: SpaceX has secured its 2027 chip funding, has IPO cash, and benefits if weaker competitors cannot finance. His October post predicted their failure. This does not make the arithmetic wrong, but it should be stated alongside the claim.
- A fair one-line verdict for the fact-check: the direction and order of magnitude are supportable (a three-month slip on $0.9–1.6T of 2027 capex is $230–400B of capital waiting to earn), but "shortage" overstates the position of cash-rich hyperscalers and understates it for labs and neoclouds, and the number is his own unsourced estimate.
