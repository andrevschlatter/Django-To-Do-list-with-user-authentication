**Exchange 1 (Jan-Feb 2017): contacts with Sudan and Syria. Topic: export controls/sanctions.** [FILING/ON-RECORD]
- The staff "limited our review of your filing to your contacts with countries that have been identified as state sponsors of terrorism". Comment 1: "We are aware of websites indicating that Nvidia products are offered for sale in Sudan and Syria, countries designated by the Department of State as state sponsors of terrorism and subject to U.S. economic sanctions and export controls. Please describe to us the nature and extent of any past, current, and anticipated contacts with Sudan and Syria, whether through subsidiaries, distributors, partners, resellers, customers or other direct or indirect arrangements." Comment 2 asked for materiality "in quantitative terms, including the approximate dollar amounts of any associated revenues, assets, and liabilities for the last three fiscal years and the subsequent interim period" and in qualitative terms. Signed by Cecilia Blye, Chief, Office of Global Security Risk — [UPLOAD Jan 31, 2017](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000000000017003420/filename1.pdf)
- Nvidia answered that it "does not have any subsidiaries, affiliates, joint-venture arrangements, business partners or other operations in Sudan and Syria and does not advertise or otherwise solicit business in these countries"; that the two third-party websites the staff identified "are not and have never been NVIDIA agents, partners, or affiliates, and are not, and have never been, authorized to sell NVIDIA products in Syria or Sudan"; and listed controls: "the screening of customers and other third parties to prevent dealings with proscribed parties; an internal system that blocks any direct shipments to proscribed parties and destinations; terms and conditions in purchase orders and sales agreements requiring compliance with U.S. laws and prohibiting diversion to proscribed destinations and parties; and internal training and audit protocols." On materiality: "NVIDIA does not have any contacts with Sudan and Syria and therefore has no quantitative amounts or qualitative factors to provide." Signed by CFO Colette Kress — [CORRESP Feb 13, 2017](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581017000020/filename1.htm) (segmented quotes rejoined)
- Outcome: "We have completed our review of this subject matter." No disclosure change requested — [UPLOAD Feb 27, 2017](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000000000017006534/filename1.pdf)

**Exchange 2 (May-June 2019): cryptocurrency revenue in the segment note. Topics: crypto, segment/product revenue disclosure (ASC 280).** [FILING/ON-RECORD]
- Sole comment, on Note 16 of the FY2019 10-K: "We note your disclosures related to revenues from cryptocurrency-specific product sales in MD&A. Please tell us how you considered separate disclosure of revenues related to all your cryptocurrency related products and services when presenting the disclosures required by ASC 280-10-50-40. Tell us how such revenues are reflected currently in the table on page 72." Contacts: Tara Harkins and Lynn Dicker, Office of Electronics and Machinery — [UPLOAD dated May 7, 2019](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000000000019008189/filename1.pdf)

### Inferences
- The gap since the last public letter (Sept 10, 2025 to Nov 22, 2026) is about 14 months. Because letters appear on EDGAR only after a review closes (the brief says at least 20 business days later; I could not confirm that number on an SEC page in this session), an open Division of Corporation Finance review of the FY2026 10-K or of later 10-Qs would not be visible yet. Absence of letters is not evidence of absence of review.
- Corporation Finance reviews (comment letters) and Enforcement investigations (the reported probe) are separate processes. The public letters show what disclosure reviewers asked about, not what investigators are examining.
- Reviewers in 2023 and 2025 concentrated on the same few areas: MD&A quantification, segment presentation, product/service revenue disaggregation, and (2025) a non-GAAP adjustment. In both years they asked a revenue-recognition question (cloud services bundling in 2023; license/service revenue share in 2025) and accepted a short factual answer.
- The biggest disclosure changes in this review period (geographic basis switched to customer headquarters in Nov 2025; customer letter labels dropped; license/software/cloud revenue policy text removed in Feb 2026; extended payment terms disclosed in Aug 2026) all came **after** the last public comment letter and were not prompted by any public comment.

## 2. Revenue recognition policy: change log

### Takeaway
The policy note changed four times in substance: ASC 606 adoption (FY2019), cloud services added (FY2022), a multiple-performance-obligation paragraph added after the 2023 SEC comment (FY2024), and in the FY2026 10-K the license, software and cloud-services sub-policies were deleted and the opening sentence narrowed to "primarily from product sales". The term "bill-and-hold" does not appear in any 10-K or 10-Q text I could search (FY2018 notes through Q2 FY2027) or in the Nov 17, 2026 8-K exhibits; the two disclosures closest to revenue-timing questions are a Q2 FY2023 sentence about $287 million of orders pulled into the quarter with extended payment terms and the Q2 FY2027 disclosure of payment terms of "90 days up to one year".

### Inferences
- The accounting question raised by the neocloud arrangements is the one the SEC staff asked about in 2023 in a smaller form: when the seller of hardware also commits to buy back the capacity that hardware produces, how is the hardware sale price separated from the purchase commitment? The filings disclose the arrangement and its size but the policy note does not describe the accounting (the Q2 FY2027 10-Q has no added policy text on it that my search found). In 2023 Nvidia promised to revise Note 1 "if and when such multi-element arrangements are material". This is a disclosure observation, not a finding of error.
- Deleting the license/software/cloud sub-policies in the FY2026 10-K is consistent with those revenues being immaterial (about 1% in FY2025 by Nvidia's statement to the staff) and with the wind-down of DGX Cloud as a product (the cloud-agreement purpose text dropped "DGX Cloud offerings" between the Q3 FY2026 10-Q and the FY2026 10-K). The same deletion also removes the stated policy for any future license or revenue-share income.
- Customer advances are small at period ends but large in flow ($9.0 billion added in FY2026). Until Jul 2026 nearly all advances received in a period were recognized as revenue within the same period; the Jul 2026 balance of $2.8 billion is the first sizeable carry-over.
- The $287 million Q2 FY2023 sentence shows Nvidia has, at least once, disclosed pulling deliveries forward with extended terms and stated that this fell within its "pre-established revenue recognition policy". No similar sentence appears in later filings that I searched for "extended payment terms" until Q2 FY2027.

### Gaps
- No access to EDGAR full-text search: I could not check 8-Ks, proxy statements, earnings-call exhibits or pre-FY2018 filings for "bill-and-hold". MD&A and risk-factor text for the FY2018 10-K and the FY2019 10-Qs was not machine-searched.
- Price protection and stock rotation: my searches for "price protection" and "stock rotation" in FY2023-FY2027 filings returned nothing; older filings were not checked for these terms.
- Whether any U.S. government revenue share was accrued or paid on the roughly $60 million of licensed H20 revenue, or on the $2.4 billion of Q3 FY2027 Hopper shipments to China, is not stated in the documents read. The Q3 FY2027 10-Q is not yet filed.
- Rebates/MDF accrual balances are not separately disclosed after FY2018; "customer program accruals" appear only as a line in accrued liabilities, which I did not tabulate.

## 3. Geographic and segment reporting: change log, the switch from billing location to customer headquarters, and Singapore

### Takeaway
Nvidia reported geographic revenue by billing location from FY2018 until the Q2 FY2026 10-Q; the Q3 FY2026 10-Q (quarter ended Oct 26, 2025, filed Nov 19, 2025) switched to customer headquarters location, recast prior periods, and removed the Singapore line, which had been 18% of FY2025 revenue and 22% of Q2 FY2026 revenue on the old basis. The recast moved the Singapore amount mostly to the United States but also raised the China (including Hong Kong) figure for FY2025 from $17.1 billion to $25.0 billion.

### Inferences
- The change was announced in a table footnote in a 10-Q, not in an 8-K or a press release item, and came about ten weeks after the SEC staff closed its FY2025 10-K review without (publicly) asking about geography. An obvious innocent explanation is stated in the filing itself: billing location reflected "a customer's centralized invoicing location" and so said little about where demand sits. ASC 280 permits either basis if disclosed.
- What the reader lost: a Singapore line that had drawn attention, any statement of how much product physically ships to Singapore (last given for FY2025: "less than 2%"), and, from Q1 FY2027, the Taiwan end-customer estimate. What the reader gained: a U.S. share that better matches where the large buyers are based.
- The recast shows that on the old basis part of the revenue billed to Singapore and "Other" belonged to China-headquartered customers: the China figure rises by $7.9 billion for FY2025 and by $4.1 billion for Q1 FY2026. This does not contradict the statement that "Over 99% of controlled Data Center compute revenue billed to Singapore was for orders from U.S.-based customers", because that statement covered only export-controlled data center compute products in Q1 and Q2 FY2026, not all products or earlier periods; the uplift could be products that were not controlled at the time (for example H20 before April 2025), gaming, or networking, billed to non-China entities of China-based groups. The filings do not say which. This is the single most useful factual item for anyone examining the Singapore question.
- The FY2024 10-K did not show Singapore separately although, by the later FY2025 10-K's own numbers, it was 11.2% of FY2024 revenue; the line also appeared and disappeared between quarters in FY2024. There is no bright-line rule for individual-country disclosure (the test is materiality), so this is an inconsistency of presentation, not a rule breach on its face.
- The Q1 FY2027 statement that "Hyperscaler revenue ... remained at approximately 50% of Data Center revenue" matched the numbers as first reported (37,869 / 75,246 = 50.3%); after the Q2 recast of one company, Q1 Hyperscale is 57.2% of Data Center revenue (43,051 / 75,246). Customer-category shares are therefore sensitive to how individual large buyers are classified.

## 4. Customer concentration, quarter by quarter (FY2023 to Q3 FY2027)

### Takeaway
From no 10% customer in FY2021 to FY2023, Nvidia moved to two to four direct customers above 10% each quarter, with a peak of 23% for one customer in Q2 FY2026. ### Inferences
- Receivables are more concentrated than revenue in most recent quarters (for example Apr 26, 2026: top three customers are 64% of receivables against 54% of quarterly revenue; Jul 26, 2026: five customers are 70% of receivables while only one customer exceeded 10% of the quarter's revenue). - The Q2 FY2027 pattern is unusual: three direct customers at 21%, 17% and 16% in Q1, one at 16% in Q2, yet three at 16%, 15% and 13% for the half. Arithmetic implies the two other first-half customers fell just under 10% in Q2 or that the mix of who buys directly shifted sharply between quarters. The filing gives no explanation.
- Because the definition of "direct customer" was widened in Q3 FY2026 to include CSPs and hyperscale companies, and because the filing warns the lettered customers "may represent different customers", the direct-customer series before and after Oct 2025 is not strictly comparable.
- The only two percentages ever given for an indirect customer (22% for Q2 FY2024 and 19% for FY2024) date from the first two periods of the AI ramp. ### Inferences
- Two earlier DSO spikes had visible, benign-or-cyclical causes: Q3 FY2019 (63.5 days, crypto unwind) and Q2 to Q3 FY2023 (72 to 75 days, when revenue fell and Nvidia disclosed extended terms). The Q2 to Q3 FY2027 rise differs because it occurs while revenue is growing about 20% a quarter, and Nvidia attributes it explicitly to extended terms and to "shipment linearity" (more shipments late in the quarter).
- On Nvidia's own numbers the mechanical effect is large: had DSO stayed at the April 2026 level of 45.4 days, Oct 25, 2026 receivables would have been about $57.8 billion (115,844 × 45.4 / 91), roughly $23 billion below the reported $81.0 billion. That is a timing-of-cash observation, not a revenue-recognition finding: under ASC 606 extended terms do not by themselves prevent recognition if collection is probable, though terms beyond one year would raise a significant-financing-component question. Nvidia's disclosed upper bound is exactly "up to one year".
- The sequence of statements matters for anyone assessing disclosure timeliness: Feb 25, 2026 "We have not entered into any financing arrangements"; May 20, 2026 10-Q (my search found no extended-terms sentence in its notes or liquidity section); Aug 26, 2026 "we have and may in the future provide longer payment terms ranging from 90 days up to one year". The filings do not say when the first such terms were granted. DSO was 45 days at April 26, 2026, which suggests little effect before Q2 FY2027.
- The low DSO of FY2024 and FY2025 (41 to 46 days) was helped by customers paying before invoices were due, which Nvidia disclosed each quarter from Q2 FY2024 to Q3 FY2025 (up to $2.8 billion at Jul 28, 2024). Adding those back gives roughly 44 to 56 days. So part of the later rise is the end of early payment (plausibly as supply shortages eased and customers no longer needed to pay early), and only the move above about 55 days in mid-2026 is new territory for the AI period.
- A $4 million allowance against $38.5 billion of receivables (0.01%) reflects a customer base Nvidia describes as investment grade plus credit insurance and letters of credit; it also means any single customer problem would not be cushioned by an existing reserve.

### Gaps
- The Q3 FY2027 allowance, receivables aging, the share of receivables on extended terms, and the identities or count of customers given such terms are not disclosed. The Q3 FY2027 10-Q had not been filed by Nov 22, 2026 (the 8-K says "We plan to file our 10-Q in the coming weeks"); in the nine prior quarters, Aug 2024 through Aug 2026, the 10-Q or 10-K was filed the same day as the earnings 8-K — [EDGAR submissions index](https://data.sec.gov/submissions/CIK0001045810.json); [CFO commentary](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000088/q3fy27cfocommentary.htm). The filing deadline for a large accelerated filer is 40 days after quarter end, so the 10-Q is not late.

- Third-party forensic commentary on DSO (short sellers, accounting bloggers, 2025-2026) was not retrieved.

### Inferences
- The presentation of commitments is not comparable across the last five quarter ends: Apr 2025 (inventory obligations and cloud agreements split in text, plus a by-year table), Jul 2025 (one combined by-year table, no split), Oct 2025 to Apr 2026 (narrative only, no table; the XBRL PurchaseObligation tag stops after Jul 2025), Jul 2026 (new five-category table plus "additional commitments" and guarantees). The changes coincide with the amounts growing roughly ninefold, which makes trend analysis from tagged data impossible without reading each note. Innocent explanation: the nature of the commitments changed (leases, equity investments, guarantees) and the old table no longer fit.
- Cloud service agreements are a two-way commercial relationship with customers: Nvidia buys cloud capacity from cloud providers that buy Nvidia systems. The commitment rose from $1.59 billion (Oct 2022) to $26 to $30 billion (Oct 2025 to Apr 2026), and from Jul 2026 a further $32 to $36 billion sits in a separate category where the neocloud "procure[s] our data center infrastructure products and we commit to cloud service agreements". The filings disclose the amounts; they do not disclose how much revenue came from the counterparties to these agreements.
- Releases of previously reserved inventory lift gross margin when reserved product is later sold. They were largest in FY2026 ($1.5 billion, of which $180 million was identified as H20). On the disclosed numbers the net effect of provisions less releases was unfavorable in every period since FY2022, so releases have not been a net source of margin.
- At the Q3 FY2027 cost of revenue of $29.2 billion a quarter, supply commitments of $390 billion are more than three years of cost of revenue [COMPUTED: 390 / (29.168 × 4) = 3.3 years]. Nvidia says the commitments are "primarily memory and manufacturing facilities" for "the next several years". If demand fell short, the accounting consequence under the stated policy would be excess-purchase-obligation charges like the $4.5 billion H20 charge.

- The split of the Q3 FY2027 provisions between inventory and purchase obligations, the provision release, and the gross-margin effect await the 10-Q.
- The FY2018 quarterly provisions and the FY2019 quarterly split other than Q3 and Q4 charges were not extracted.
- The filings do not name the suppliers or the cancellability terms of the $390 billion, nor the counterparties to the cloud and neocloud agreements.

### Inferences
- Before 2023 Nvidia reported earnings and filed the 10-Q a few days apart (for example 8-K May 24, 2023 and 10-Q May 26, 2023), so a gap is not unprecedented; what is new is the interim "supplemental" exhibit and the timing one week after a CEO change and on the day of the WSJ report. The usual innocent reasons are certification logistics after a change of principal executive officer and additional audit-committee or auditor review procedures; the filings give no reason.
- The useful-life change was small ($33 million in a quarter) relative to Nvidia's income and is the same direction as changes made by large cloud companies in the same years. It matters more for Nvidia's customers' economics than for Nvidia's own accounts.
- Warranty growth tracks the move from selling chips to selling full racks and systems; the filings give no defect-specific explanation after FY2023.
