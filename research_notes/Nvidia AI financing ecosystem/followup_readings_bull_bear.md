## Chris Wood, "Why Nvidia's 'circular financing' does not concern me": thesis, the $165bn, Ohio terms, and any update after the exit

### Takeaway
The piece (Grow or Die Substack, about 10 to 14 Sep 2026, pre-exit) argues Nvidia is "not a bigger Lucent" because its balance sheet and cash flow can carry what it has promised. Its account of the Ohio guarantee is accurate: Nvidia's own 8-K confirms payment only on an OpenAI trigger event, OpenAI reimbursement and indemnity, and termination when OpenAI achieves a satisfactory credit rating. One of its supporting numbers is now out of date (cumulative private-portfolio impairments are $2.2bn, not $250m).

### Cited Findings
**How much was visible:** summary-level only, plus the same author's later posts in summary.

- Date: the X repost of the article has a post ID that decodes to 14 Sep 2026; the earlier pass found one summary dating the Substack to 10 Sep 2026 [PRE] (search summary) — [X article](https://x.com/cwood_growordie/article/2099568612277137831); [Grow or Die](https://growordieinvesting.substack.com/p/why-nvidias-circular-financing-does)
- Thesis: circular financing "is a real phenomenon and deserves scrutiny" but "doesn't concern me much"; "Nvidia is not a bigger Lucent. Lucent's balance sheet and cash flows could not justify financing customers. Nvidia's can." [OPINION] (search summary) — [Grow or Die](https://growordieinvesting.substack.com/p/why-nvidias-circular-financing-does)
- The total: "$164.5 billion—we'll call it $165 billion" is his figure for Nvidia putting its balance sheet behind customers; risks differ by type: the $108.5bn of land, power and shell guarantees cost Nvidia money only if a tenant defaults, while the take-or-pay agreements are a different kind of exposure [ANALYST ESTIMATE] (search summary) — same source. A companion piece by the same author on Advisorpedia says he added up everything in the guarantee and commitment schedules that could fairly be called putting the balance sheet behind a customer — [Advisorpedia](https://www.advisorpedia.com/active/is-nvidia-really-paying-for-its-own-growth-the-numbers-tell-a-different-story/), n/c
- Two-thirds of the $165bn is one deal that "has received little coverage". The search summary in this pass called it "a neocloud arrangement"; the first pass identified it as the $105bn Ohio/OpenAI guarantee. See Inferences for why Ohio fits the arithmetic (search summary) — same source
- Ohio terms, verified against the filing (Nvidia 8-K, 17 Aug 2026, Items 1.01 and 2.03) [FILING/ON-RECORD] (opened) — [Nvidia 8-K, 17 Aug 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000069/nvda-20260817.htm) [PRE]:
  - Nvidia gives "residual value guaranties" to SB Energy Corp. (the lessor); the tenant is an OpenAI Group PBC affiliate; leases cover about 4.25 GW of IT load at the Portsmouth site, Pike County, Ohio; Nvidia may at its sole discretion support a further 3.8 GW or so.
  - "NVIDIA's aggregate payment obligation is cumulatively capped at $105 billion for its initial commitment under the Agreements."
  - Trigger: OpenAI insolvency leading to lease default, or OpenAI failing to make lease payments. Nvidia then generally pays the shortfall between the guaranteed minimum lease value and what is recovered through a replacement lease or sale.
  - Conditions: the lessor must have met ready-for-service conditions for the premises (expected to begin in 2028); each agreement takes effect when the relevant lease commences.
  - Nvidia's options after a trigger: assume the lease, require the lessor to relet, start a sale process, let the lease terminate, or defer for up to a year while paying specified project costs.
  - Termination on the earliest of: the 20th anniversary of lease commencement; OpenAI terminating the lease under its terms; OpenAI achieving a satisfactory credit rating; other customary events.
  - OpenAI has agreed to reimburse and indemnify Nvidia for amounts Nvidia actually pays the lessor.
  - No guarantee fee is disclosed.
- Unsourced detail in a search summary: Nvidia "had considered a guarantee of about $250 billion" for the Ohio campus before the $105bn version signed on 17 Aug [UNVERIFIED; originating outlet not identified] (search summary) — surfaced alongside [Dave Manuel, 14 Nov 2026](https://www.davemanuel.com/2026/11/14/nvidia-and-circular-financing-explained/)
- Size against cash flow, from the filings rather than from Wood: Q3 FY27 operating cash flow $48.3bn and free cash flow $43.8bn; nine-month free cash flow $113.7bn; maximum guarantee exposure $118.0bn at 25 Oct 2026; "To date, we have not made any payments related to our guarantees" [FILING/ON-RECORD] (opened) — [Q3 FY27 CFO commentary](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000088/q3fy27cfocommentary.htm); [Q3 press release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000088/q3fy27pr.htm) [POST]
- His "$250m of cumulative write-downs" point is now stale: Q3 included a $1.8bn impairment on non-marketable securities; cumulative gross unrealised losses and impairments are $2.2bn against cumulative gross unrealised gains of $9.9bn [FILING/ON-RECORD] (opened) — [supplemental exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000088/supplementalfinancialinfor.htm); CFO commentary [POST]
- After the exit: no dedicated update on the guarantees was found. In a later essay on AMD he writes that "Jensen's exit matters less for the next two years than you might think, and far more for the years that will actually settle this bet"; another recent post says financing risk "has moved downstream" and the next downturn is more likely to begin in project returns than in channel inventory [OPINION] (search summary) — [Grow or Die, AMD essay](https://growordieinvesting.substack.com/p/ai-agents-just-changed-the-chip-race-355), n/c [POST likely]; the second quote's post was not identified (candidates: [What's next for Nvidia?](https://growordieinvesting.substack.com/p/whats-next-for-nvidia), [Where Nvidia goes from here](https://growordieinvesting.substack.com/p/where-nvidia-nvda-goes-from-here))

### Inferences
- The $164.5bn is almost certainly $108.5bn of guarantees + $36bn of AI cloud agreements + $20bn of third-party data center leases, the three "balance sheet behind a customer" lines in the Q2 FY27 CFO commentary (see the Hunterbrook section). $105bn / $164.5bn = 64%, which is the "two-thirds". So the "one deal" is Ohio, and the phrase "neocloud arrangement" in the search summary is loose.
- On the filing's terms, Nvidia's expected loss on Ohio depends on three things failing together: OpenAI stops paying, the campus cannot be relet or sold near the guaranteed minimum, and OpenAI cannot honour its indemnity. The third leg is weak comfort, because the trigger is OpenAI's own insolvency or non-payment. That weakness is my observation, not Wood's.
- $118bn maximum exposure equals about 2.7 times one quarter's free cash flow, or about one times nine-month free cash flow, and does not start until fiscal 2029.

### Gaps
- Full text; whether Wood compares the guarantee to cash flow in numbers; whether he has published a post-exit reassessment of the guarantees specifically.

## SemiAnalysis, "Nvidia GPU Debt Backstop Unleashes the AI Project Trinity": date, mechanism, the $530bn and $99bn, and the verdict

### Takeaway
The backstop piece (mid-2026, date still n/c, pre-exit) describes a take-or-pay revenue floor from Nvidia to a neocloud in exchange for a share of revenue above the floor, and is constructive on it. The $530bn and $99bn figures do not appear to come from this piece: they are from the later SemiAnalysis piece "Nvidia and the World Without Jensen" (about 13 Nov 2026), where $530bn is the 10-Q sum of commitments plus guarantees and $99bn is Nvidia's equity book. That later piece is bearish on Nvidia without Huang.

### Cited Findings
**How much was visible:** the free opening and worked example via several search summaries; nothing behind the paywall; the two later SemiAnalysis pieces only through secondary coverage.

*The backstop piece [PRE, date n/c]*
- Mechanism: Nvidia provides "a take-or-pay commitment to Neoclouds, a minimum revenue guarantee on the underlying GPU capacity"; in exchange Nvidia shares in revenue earned above the backstop level [ANALYST ESTIMATE/description] (search summary) — [SemiAnalysis](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/nvidia-gpu-debt-backstop-unleashes)
- Worked example (illustrative): GB300 one-year rental $6.75/hr; year-one floor $3.68/hr; of the excess Nvidia gets $1.23 and the neocloud $1.84, so the neocloud's first-year revenue is $5.52/hr. The example runs six years; the six-year average backstop price is about $2.36 per GPU-hour; Nvidia's take rate averages roughly 18% over the six years [ANALYST ESTIMATE] (search summary) — same source; [Asymmetric Curiosity](https://asymmetriccuriosity.substack.com/p/nvidia-the-central-bank-of-ai), n/c
- Disclosed deal used as a check: SharonAI's 72 MW Australian build carries a disclosed total backstop value of $4.88bn, an implied average floor of about $2.33 per GPU-hour over six years [ANALYST ESTIMATE on the per-hour figure] (search summary) — same sources
- Residual value: SemiAnalysis notes there is "no active derivatives market" to give a pricing signal for GPU residual value; one write-up derives a residual value guarantee of $9.4bn per gigawatt from SemiAnalysis numbers; SemiAnalysis says Nvidia's only support inside the $500bn capital programme is "a residual value guarantee for up to 25% of a deal". A separate write-up gives a $3.25 average for the capital-partner programme; the basis was not visible [ANALYST ESTIMATE] (search summary) — [Asymmetric Curiosity](https://asymmetriccuriosity.substack.com/p/nvidia-the-central-bank-of-ai); [SemiAnalysis, "Nvidia's Backstop Universe"](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/nvidias-backstop-universe-heads-i), n/c
- Effect on debt (from the first pass, not re-seen): lenders require an investment-grade offtake or backstop; GPU loans at 70 to 80% LTV; about 4 points of extra interest changes deal economics.
- Scale and tone: coverage headlines read "Not Bearish on Nvidia; The 'AI Central Bank' Could Move a $7 Trillion Debt Snowball"; a podcast write-up is headed "Nishball: AI Needs $7 Trillion in Debt by 2029, and Nvidia's Backstop Isn't Big Enough"; another headline says Patel put backstopped cluster deals at 6.5 GW (headline only) [ANALYST ESTIMATE] (search summary) — [Odaily](https://www.odaily.news/en/post/5211768); [BigGo podcast note](https://finance.biggo.com/news/0a2ebc8f381aac9d); [FourWeekMBA](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-nvidia-has-backstopped-6-5-gw-of-cluster-deals-patel-says/), all n/c
- Nvidia's own framing, per one summary: it "collects twice", on the chip sale and on the revenue share, and makes no loans; industry executives expect 7 to 10 neocloud survivors (search summary) — surfaced with [Asymmetric Curiosity](https://asymmetriccuriosity.substack.com/p/nvidia-the-central-bank-of-ai)
- Nvidia's filing language for the same agreements: under AI cloud (now "neocloud") agreements "we will earn revenue on the upfront sale of our infrastructure" and, if criteria are met, participate in revenue share from third-party customers. The filing does not use "take-or-pay" or "backstop" [FILING/ON-RECORD] (opened) — [Q2 FY27 CFO commentary](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000073/q2fy27cfocommentary.htm); [Q3 FY27 CFO commentary](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000088/q3fy27cfocommentary.htm)

*The $530bn and $99bn: "Nvidia and the World Without Jensen" [BETWEEN, about 13 Nov 2026 per coverage dates]*
- SemiWiki, citing the report: $530bn of "gross off-balance-sheet guarantees" at fiscal Q2, including $36bn of take-or-pay floors under neocloud capacity, plus a $99bn equity book. A search summary of SemiAnalysis's balance-sheet analysis says the $99bn equity book generated $23.7bn of gains in the first half, with $25bn more of equity investments committed [ANALYST ESTIMATE] (search summary) — [SemiAnalysis](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/nvidia-and-the-world-without-jensen); [SemiWiki](https://semiwiki.com/semiconductor-manufacturers/376352-after-jensen-a-window-for-nvidias-rivals-a-problem-for-its-suppliers/); [Dataconomy, 13 Nov 2026](https://dataconomy.com/2026/11/13/nvidias-off-balance-sheet-guarantees-surge-to-530-billion/); [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/nvidias-off-balance-sheet-guarantees-soar-to-530b-in-fiscal-q2-semianalysis/)
- Argument: Nvidia "doesn't function without Jensen"; neoclouds raised money because Huang backed them; Nvidia sells GPUs at full margin, then floors the buyer's revenue, guarantees the landlord or signs the lease itself, so non-investment-grade operators borrow at investment-grade pricing; without that, a possible "large contraction" in the ecosystem [OPINION/ANALYST ESTIMATE] (search summary) — same sources
- Verdict as quoted by SemiWiki: the departure is "really good for all Nvidia competitors, and really bad for Nvidia supply chain"; SemiAnalysis projects non-Nvidia silicon could exceed 70% of total capacity in 2028 [ANALYST ESTIMATE] (search summary) — [SemiWiki](https://semiwiki.com/semiconductor-manufacturers/376352-after-jensen-a-window-for-nvidias-rivals-a-problem-for-its-suppliers/)
- The same report speculates about a US intelligence role in the exit and says its model tracks more than 1.5 GW of Nvidia GPUs "that shouldn't be in China"; no direct evidence offered [UNVERIFIED speculation] (search summary) — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/semianalysis-suspects-us-intelligence-role-133013451.html)

*What $530bn actually contains (primary)*
- At 26 Jul 2026: future commitments $366bn (supply and capacity $279bn, cloud service agreements $29bn, data center leases not commenced $25bn, equity investments $25bn, capex $8bn); AI cloud agreements $36bn and third-party data center leases not commenced $20bn (together $56bn); guarantees $108.5bn (SB Energy $105.0bn, land/power/shell for AI clouds $3.5bn). 366 + 56 + 108.5 = $530.5bn [FILING/ON-RECORD] (opened) — [Q2 FY27 CFO commentary](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000073/q2fy27cfocommentary.htm) [PRE]

### Inferences
- Calling the $530bn "guarantees" is wrong on the filing: 53% of it ($279bn) is Nvidia's own purchase commitments to suppliers, mostly memory, and only $108.5bn is guarantees. The customer-support part is $164.5bn (guarantees + AI cloud agreements + third-party leases).
- SemiAnalysis's stance moved with events: constructive on the backstop as a financing device in mid-2026, bearish on Nvidia's position once the person underwriting it left. Use the first for mechanics and the second for the key-man argument.
- A $3.68 floor against a $6.75 market rate means the floor is about 55% of the year-one rental price, and the six-year average floor ($2.36) is about 35% of it. Lenders are being protected against a fall in rents of roughly half to two-thirds, not against any fall. This is arithmetic on the illustrative example.

- Whether $99bn is described in SemiAnalysis's own words as the equity book: seen only through SemiWiki and a search summary.

## Odd Lots with Stanley Lee (Liquid Compute) and Paul Kedrosky: date, LTV path, rates, lenders, Nvidia guarantees, residual values, distress signs

### Takeaway
One aggregator lists the episode as Bloomberg Odd Lots, "This Is The Problem With AI's Most Bullish Chart", 19 Nov 2026 (post-earnings). Lee's argument is that rising GPU rents are partly a symptom of credit rationing. LTVs fell from as high as 80% in the summer to about 70% after Labor Day to 50 to 60% now, with mid-to-high-teens rates at 50%. No summary I could see reports anything Lee or Kedrosky said about Nvidia's guarantees.

### Cited Findings
**How much was visible:** secondary summaries only (StreetSignal's own page was not visible beyond its title); no Bloomberg transcript.

## Lucent and Nortel: who made the comparison in 2026, the best rebuttals, and sourced numbers

### Takeaway
Filings give the collapse precisely: Lucent's revenue fell 57% in two years ($28.9bn in fiscal 2000 to $12.3bn in fiscal 2002, as restated for continuing operations) and Nortel's fell 62% ($27.9bn in 2000 to $10.6bn in 2002). Lucent booked about $2.6bn of customer-financing charges in fiscal 2001 and 2002 and had reserved $2.1bn against $3.0bn drawn by September 2001. The 2026 comparison is made mainly by Tunguz, Chanos (2025), Dave Manuel and several Substack writers; the best rebuttals are Nvidia's own 53-day memo, Wood's balance-sheet argument and the point that Nvidia's exposure is equity and contingent guarantees rather than loans.

### Inferences
- Revenue declines (my arithmetic on the filing figures): Lucent −26% in fiscal 2001 and −42% in fiscal 2002, −57% over two years; Nortel −37% in 2001 and −40% in 2002, −62% over two years.
- The widely quoted Lucent revenue of about $33.6bn for fiscal 2000 is the figure as originally reported; the FY2002 10-K shows $28.9bn for the same year. The gap is presumably businesses later reclassified as discontinued (spin-offs). I did not open a document that reconciles the two, so any ratio built on Lucent's revenue should say which base it uses: $8.1bn of commitments is 24% of $33.6bn but 28% of $28.9bn.
- Lucent's loss rate on drawn customer financing was high: reserves were 70% of drawn amounts at September 2001 ($2.1bn of $3.0bn) and about 86% at September 2002 ($0.95bn of $1.1bn). Total commitments were run down from $8.1bn to $1.3bn in two years, mostly by cancelling undrawn lines. That is the precedent for Kress's "could be reduced or eliminated" language.
- The size comparison misleads in both directions. In dollars and as a share of revenue Nvidia's customer support ($150bn at Q3: $118bn guarantees + $32bn neocloud agreements, about 32% of annualised Q3 revenue) is in Lucent's range or above. But Lucent's were loans that got drawn in cash before default, while Nvidia's are contingent, start in fiscal 2029, and have had no cash paid. The better parallel is what happened to revenue when customers lost access to capital: down 57% and 62%.
