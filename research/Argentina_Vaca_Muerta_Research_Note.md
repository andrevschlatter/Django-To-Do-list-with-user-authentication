## Introduction

Argentina is the one shale story outside North America that is actually delivering. National crude output hit a record 973,700 b/d in September 2026, Vaca Muerta supplied 698,500 b/d of it, Neuquén crossed 714,000 b/d, and the country should pass 1 mb/d around the turn of the year. The constraint has been pipe, not rock: Oldelval's trunk to Puerto Rosales saturated in July 2026, so the next twelve months turn on the US$3bn, 437 km VMOS oil pipeline (line-fill from January 2027, first VLCC from Punta Colorada around March, 550 kb/d by end-2027), TGS's +14 MMm3/d expansion of the Perito Moreno gas line (April 2027), Southern Energy's two Golar FLNG vessels (first LNG end-2027) and the 12 mtpa YPF/Eni/XRG Argentina LNG FID scheduled for 26 November 2026, six days after this trip. Milei's RIGI carries 25 approved projects worth US$68bn, mostly Vaca Muerta, with a 25% tax rate, export-duty exemption and an offshore export-proceeds ladder; shale crude still pays an 8% export duty, but dividends on post-2024 earnings are remittable again and companies sent a record US$4bn abroad in January-September 2026. The rock is Permian-plus (30 bbl per lateral foot in year one versus 15-23 in Texas), YPF dominates, majors are selling to locals while Chevron and TotalEnergies scale up, and the risk is macro: riesgo país above 900 bp, GDP flat, reserves near US$50bn and 2027 polls a dead heat between Milei and Axel Kicillof.

## Key Points

- Argentina produced a record 973.7 kb/d of oil in September 2026; Vaca Muerta supplied 698.5 kb/d (72%); consultants expect 1 mb/d in Q4 2026.
- VMOS (437 km, ~US$3bn, 550 kb/d design) was 85% built in late October 2026; line-fill January 2027, first VLCC cargo around March 2027.
- Oldelval's trunk has been full since July 2026 (540 kb/d firm, >570 kb/d with drag reducers); trucking bridges the gap to VMOS.
- TGS's Perito Moreno expansion lifts gas takeaway from 21 to 35 MMm3/d by 1 April 2027; producers bid 11x the residual capacity.
- Southern Energy FLNG (PAE 30/YPF 25/Pampa 20/Harbour 15/Golar 10): Hilli 2.45 mtpa end-2027, MK II 3.5 mtpa 2028, feed pipeline mid-2028.
- Argentina LNG (YPF 36/Eni 32/XRG 32; 12 mtpa; US$51bn RIGI filing) FID signing is set for 26 November 2026 in Buenos Aires.
- RIGI: 25 approved projects, US$68.4bn; 25% tax, export-duty exemption from year 3, 20/40/100% offshore export proceeds, 30-year stability, adhesion to 8 July 2027.
- Shale crude still pays an 8% export duty; conventional crude moved to a 0-8% Brent-linked scale (Decreto 59/2026); Plan Gas.Ar ends December 2028.
- Dividends on FY2025+ profits are remittable (Com. A 8226); companies sent ~US$4bn abroad in Jan-Sep 2026, a 10-year record; older earnings exit via BOPREAL.
- A 2026 well: ~3,300 m lateral, ~55 stages, 12,500 t sand, US$12.8-14m; Rystad puts year-one output at 30 bbl/ft versus 15-23 in the Permian.
- Activity: 43 rigs (September 2026), ~540 wells and 28-30,000 frac stages in 2026, 50 rigs and 39,000 stages projected for 2027.
- YPF shale oil reached 224 kb/d in Q3 2026 (+32%) with a 255-260 kb/d December exit; capex US$5.8-6.2bn; leverage 0.81x; Vista 169 kboe/d.
- M&A: ExxonMobil, Petronas and Equinor sold to Pluspetrol and Vista for US$1.7bn, US$1.5bn and US$0.7bn; Chevron filed a US$13.8bn RIGI.
- Macro: riesgo país 925 bp (5 November 2026), 2026 GDP consensus +0.7%, reserves ~US$50bn, inflation 33.4% y/y; 2027 polls tie Milei with Kicillof.

## Fiscal Regime

**RIGI gives a single-project vehicle a 30-year fiscal and FX shield.** The Régimen de Incentivo para Grandes Inversiones (Ley 27.742, Decreto 749/2024) taxes an adhered vehicle at 25% instead of 35%, allows accelerated depreciation and VAT certificates, exempts imported capital goods from duties, exempts the project's exports from export duties from the third year after adhesion (second year for strategic-export projects) and cuts dividend withholding from 7% to 3.5% after seven years. Under BCRA Com. A 8099 the vehicle may keep 20%, 40% and then 100% of export proceeds offshore on a three-step ladder (start year disputed across sources) and may pay dividends and repatriate capital without BCRA approval up to the capital actually brought in. Thresholds are US$200m generally, US$300m for oil and gas transport and, since Decreto 105/2026 of 19 February 2026, US$600m for upstream; the same decree extended adhesion to 8 July 2027 ([Microjuris, 19 Feb 2026](https://aldiaargentina.microjuris.com/2026/02/19/legislacion-se-modifica-el-rigi-y-se-prorroga-el-plazo-de-adhesion/)). Tax, customs and FX terms are locked for 30 years, the clause to read as the hedge against a 2027 change of government.

**Approved RIGI capital is dominated by Vaca Muerta oil, gas and LNG.** By 19 October 2026 the government counted 25 approved projects worth US$68.4bn (an academic monitor says US$76.2bn, using Southern Energy's updated US$15.2bn rather than the US$6.9bn declared at approval) ([La Nación, 19 Oct 2026](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/economia/vaca-muerta-suma-tres-proyectos-al-rigi-por-mas-de-us15400-millones-para-exportar-petroleo-y-gas-nid19102026/)). The energy list: VMOS (US$2.9bn, Res. 302/2025); Southern Energy FLNG (US$6.9bn, Res. 559/2025); TGS's Perito Moreno expansion (US$550m, Res. 676/2026); Pampa's Rincón de Aranda (US$4.52bn, June 2026, the first upstream approval); Tecpetrol's Los Toldos II Este (US$6.4bn, second stage August 2026); the San Matías LNG pipeline (US$1.3bn, Res. 873/2026); Pluspetrol's Bajo del Choique-La Invernada (US$12.24bn, October 2026); and two TGS NGL projects (US$3.2bn). Pending are the US$51bn Argentina LNG filing, Chevron's US$13.8bn El Trapial plan, a US$8.7bn Bajo del Toro filing and YPF's five northern-hub projects worth US$44bn. Critics (Kartal, CEPA) say RIGI "does not spill over", with 97% of approvals in oil, gas and mining.

**Outside RIGI, Milei has freed prices but kept the 8% shale export duty.** Ley Bases (July 2024) removed the Executive's power to set domestic crude prices and freed exports subject only to Energy Secretariat non-objection, ending the "barril criollo". Decreto 59/2026 put conventional crude on a sliding scale, 0% at Brent of US$65/bbl or below rising to 8% above US$80, but Vaca Muerta crude stayed at a flat 8%, and with Brent above US$100 conventional fields were lifted back to 8% in March ([Infobae, 29 Jan 2026](https://www.infobae.com/economia/2026/01/29/el-gobierno-establecio-un-nuevo-esquema-para-reducir-las-retenciones-a-la-exportacion-de-petroleo-crudo/)). Energy Secretary María Tettamanti has confirmed Plan Gas.Ar expires on 31 December 2028 without renewal. Neuquén royalties are 12% on legacy concessions, 15-18% on 2026 tenders and 7.5% for LNG-bound gas under a June 2026 bill. The labour reform was promulgated on 6 March 2026 after the Senate stripped the corporate tax cut, so the rate stays 35%; no tax reform has passed, and the latest signal is a 31 October bill taxing CEDEAR gains at 15%.

**Cash can leave Argentina again, but only for post-2024 profits.** BCRA Com. A 8226 (April 2025) let companies pay dividends through the official market on profits of fiscal years starting 1 January 2025, against audited statements. Earnings retained to December 2024 remain blocked and were offered BOPREAL Serie 4, a US$3bn BCRA bond paying 3% to October 2028, one-third usable against taxes. The 90-day cross-restriction between the official market and CCL/MEP still applies to companies, intercompany loans booked since 21 April 2025 need a 180-day minimum term, and Com. A 8481 (14 September 2026) restated the audited-statement condition. The channel works: remittances were a record US$1.89bn in Q1 2026 and close to US$4bn in January-September, the most in a decade per BCRA vice-president Vladimir Werning ([Diario Río Negro, Oct 2026](https://www.rionegro.com.ar/economia/giro-de-dividendos-las-empresas-enviaron-cerca-de-us4-000-millones-al-exterior-en-2026-segun-el-bcra-4749203/)). A RIGI vehicle is better placed because its repatriation rights key off capital contributed, not the fiscal year of the profit.

**The macro backdrop has turned from disinflation-plus-growth to disinflation-plus-stagnation.** Since January 2026 the FX band's edges move with lagged inflation; on 5 November the ceiling was ARS 1,958/US$ and the wholesale rate near ARS 1,550, 27% below it, and the BCRA has bought more than US$16bn in 2026 to take gross reserves to US$50.3bn on 3 November before an IMF payment pushed them under US$50bn ([Infobae, 5 Nov 2026](https://www.infobae.com/economia/2026/11/05/el-banco-central-ya-compro-mas-de-usd-16000-millones-en-2026-pero-las-reservas-cayeron-por-un-pago-al-fmi/)). September CPI was 1.9% m/m and 33.4% y/y and the REM sees 30.4% for 2026, but 2026 GDP consensus is +0.7% with Q3 at -2.3% and poverty back at 32.3%. The IMF completed the second EFF review on 21 May 2026 with a waiver for a roughly US$10bn net-reserve miss and reached staff-level agreement on the third in October (about US$900m, Board in early December). Milei's coalition won 40.8% in the 26 October 2025 midterm and is first minority in Deputies (95 of 257) but holds 20 of 72 senators. Poliarquía puts approval at 42% with a 31/52 positive/negative image, every October-November poll has Milei and Axel Kicillof inside the margin of error for 2027, and riesgo país jumped from a mid-July low to 925 bp on 5 November 2026.

## Midstream, Pipelines and LNG

**Oil takeaway today is roughly 650 kb/d firm, and it is full.** Oldelval's Allen-Puerto Rosales trunk moves about 75% of the basin's crude; Duplicar Plus (April 2025, US$1.2-1.4bn by source) lifted capacity from 225 to 540 kb/d, and July 2026 nominations of 86,300 m3/d exceeded the 86,000 m3/d installed ([EconoJournal, Jul 2026](https://econojournal.com.ar/oilgas/vaca-muerta-para-no-afectar-la-produccion-oldelval-activa-una-ampliacion-tecnica-del-sistema-de-evacuacion-de-petroleo/)). A US$25m pump-and-DRA "Extendido" finished around September and the line now moves more than 570 kb/d, with a 700-730 kb/d ceiling that CEO Ricardo Hösel calls interruptible. Shareholders are YPF 37%, Pluspetrol about 33% after absorbing ExxonMobil's 21%, Chevron 14%, PAE 11.9%, Tecpetrol 2.1% and Pampa 2.1%. Oiltanking Ebytem's Puerto Rosales terminal (US$600m expansion, June 2025, 780,000 m3 of storage) ships more than 380 kb/d and is adding a third berth worth 125 kb/d. OTASA to ENAP's Biobío refinery in Chile (ENAP 36.25%, YPF 36%, Chevron 27.75%) runs 110 kb/d with pumps being added for 115 kb/d. YPF's feeders (Vaca Muerta Norte 160 kb/d, Loma Campana-Lago Pellegrini 157 kb/d) move crude within the basin, and trucking at up to six times pipeline cost carried 31 kb/d in March and 15 kb/d in September.

**VMOS is the unlock, and first cargo has slipped to around March 2027.** Vaca Muerta Oil Sur runs 437 km from Allen to a new deep-water terminal at Punta Colorada with six tanks and two VLCC monobuoys; cost is about US$3bn, 70% funded by a US$2bn five-year syndicated loan at SOFR + 5.5% (July 2025, 14 lenders), and it entered RIGI on 20 March 2025. Stakes mirror firm transport contracts: YPF 29.8%, Pluspetrol 14.6%, Chevron plus CDC 12%, PAE 10%, Pampa 9.1%, Vista 9.1%, Shell 7.3%, Tecpetrol 7.3%, GyP 0.9% (industry post, September 2026; only Pampa's stake independently corroborated). The pipe was laid by November 2025 and the terminal reached 85% in late October after the Hormuz-delayed monobuoys landed at San Antonio Este ([DataPortuaria, Oct 2026](https://dataportuaria.com/es/argentina/ambiente-y-energia/la-terminal-de-punta-colorada-del-vmos-alcanza-un-85-de-avance-y-preve-su-primera-exportacion-de-petroleo-en-el-primer-trimestre-de-2027)). Guidance has slipped from a January first cargo (YPF to Congress, August) to line-fill from January and a first cargo between late February and April, with YPF saying March. Capacity ramps from 180 kb/d to 390 kb/d by mid-2027 and 550 kb/d by end-2027; pumping stations for 700-770 kb/d are agreed for 2028 or later, and 1 mb/d is under study.

**Duplicar Norte opens the northern hub but adds no export capacity.** Oldelval's 207 km, 24-inch line from Auca Mahuida to Allen (US$380-400m, Techint-built, RIGI, ship-or-pay with Pluspetrol, Chevron, Tecpetrol and GyP) was 60% complete in October 2026, targeting 220 kb/d by December 2026 and 500 kb/d in March 2027 ([Techint, 2026](https://www.techint.com/en/news/2026/duplicar-norte-the-infrastructure-that-will-expand-crude-oil-transportation-capacity-from-vaca-muerta)). It feeds El Trapial, Bajo del Choique and Los Toldos into the trunk, so it relieves gathering rather than the Atlantic bottleneck. The paired Duplicar X was shelved after the spring price drop; Oldelval will choose between a second northern pipe and repowering Medanito.

**The capacity ladder only catches up with production in 2027-28.** Firm takeaway today is about 650 kb/d (Oldelval 540 plus OTASA 110), stretched to 700-730 kb/d interruptible plus trucking, against 974 kb/d of output and a refining system capped near 550 kb/d that already runs at 530-585 kb/d, so every incremental barrel is an export; consultants expect national output above 1 mb/d in Q4 2026 ([Ámbito, Oct 2026](https://www.ambito.com/energia/vaca-muerta-llevo-la-produccion-petroleo-un-nuevo-record-y-quedo-un-paso-del-millon-barriles-diarios-n6336216)). VMOS phase one lifts export capacity to roughly 830-910 kb/d, its 550 kb/d design takes basin takeaway to 1.2-1.3 mb/d in 2027 (enough for about 2028), and the 700-770 kb/d expansion gives 1.35-1.5 mb/d from 2028. Industry targets 1.5 mb/d by 2030 with 1 mb/d exported, trade press sizes the "Vaca Muerta 2030" programme above US$5bn and Wood Mackenzie says US$22bn and 1,000 wells are needed by 2032. Still to build: a second northern line or Medanito repowering, Duplicar X, more OTASA pumping and the third berth; no new refinery is planned.

**Gas takeaway today is 21 MMm3/d on the new axis plus legacy lines.** The state-owned, TGS-operated Perito Moreno pipeline (563 km, 36-inch, Tratayén to Salliqueló) carries 21 MMm3/d (0.74 bcf/d), fed by TGS's 14.8-15 MMm3/d Tratayén conditioning plant; TGN's reversed Gasoducto Norte moves about 15 MMm3/d north and could do 19 if four stalled Esuco compressor plants are finished. Exports go to Chile via GasAndes (about 30 MMm3/d capacity against 19 MMm3/d demand) and to Brazil through Bolivia at only 2-3 MMm3/d with US$1.40-1.90/MMBtu transit fees. Neuquén set a record 118.64 MMm3/d in June 2026 and national output averaged 147.7 MMm3/d (5.2 bcf/d) in January-September, yet Argentina imported 23 LNG cargoes for about US$840m in winter 2026 because winter demand of 180-195 MMm3/d sits 50 MMm3/d above summer and there is no storage ([EconoJournal, May 2026](https://econojournal.com.ar/2026/05/si-argentina-bate-records-de-produccion-de-gas-por-que-a-la-vez-importa-gnl/)). Vaca Muerta supplied 91% of H1 2026 gas exports.

**Three gas projects are in construction or at the FID gate.** TGS is building the Perito Moreno expansion: three new La Pampa compressor plants plus an added Tratayén unit (about 90,000-95,000 HP) taking the trunk from 21 to 35 MMm3/d (1.24 bcf/d) with a 6 MMm3/d option; RIGI Res. 676/2026 cites US$550m against press totals of US$700-800m, construction began in February 2026, start-up is 1 April 2027, and the August open season drew bids above 100 MMm3/d for 9 MMm3/d available ([Shale24, Aug 2026](https://www.shale24.com/oil-gas/ampliacion-gasoducto-perito-moreno-tgs-recibio-ofertas-volumen-once-veces-mas-alto-capacidad-ociosa-transporte-resta-licitar-n1618)). The San Matías Pipeline (471 km, 36-inch, 27 MMm3/d, US$1.3bn) is Southern Energy's feed line to the Golfo San Matías: EPC awarded, a US$900m project loan closed in August 2026, ready mid-2028. TGN's 753 km Manuel Belgrano line to La Carlota (US$1.5bn pipe, US$2.2bn with system works, 13 MMm3/d from April 2029) needs firm contracts for an FID targeted at end-2026. The government plans a 30-year concession of the Perito Moreno line itself (about US$500m upfront, US$1.94bn invested in five years).

**Southern Energy is sanctioned; Argentina LNG signs on 26 November.** Southern Energy (PAE 30%, YPF 25%, Pampa 20%, Harbour 15%, Golar 10%) took FID on Golar's Hilli Episeyo (2.45 mtpa) on 2 May 2025 and on the newbuild MK II "Esperanza" (3.5 mtpa) in August 2025, both 20-year charters (US$285m and US$400m a year to Golar plus 25% of FOB above US$8/MMBtu) under a 30-year export licence. Hilli left Cameroon in mid-2026 for a US$350m Seatrium upgrade, arrives June 2027 and starts around end-2027; Esperanza was 74% built mid-2026 for 2028 operations, and SEFE has taken 2 mtpa for eight years ([Golar Q2 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/08/13/3344321/0/en/golar-lng-limited-interim-results-for-the-period-ended-june-30-2026.html)). Argentina LNG is the bigger prize: after Shell exited in December 2025, YPF signed a February 2026 JDA with Eni and ADNOC's XRG (32% each, YPF 36%) for two 6 mtpa floating units and a 48-inch pipeline; YPF raised phase-one cost to about US$24bn, filed a US$51bn RIGI application in August, agreed 7.5% royalties with Neuquén conditional on FID, and CEO Horacio Marín said on 20 October that the FID will be signed in Buenos Aires on 26 November 2026, with exports in 2030-31.

**Gas still needs about 70 MMm3/d of new demand to clear the surplus.** The sequence is 2027 (Perito Moreno +14 MMm3/d), 2027-28 (the two FLNGs and San Matías line absorbing 22-24 MMm3/d), 2029 (Belgrano +13 MMm3/d if sanctioned) and 2030-31 (Argentina LNG, 45-48 MMm3/d). Even at 35-41 MMm3/d the Perito Moreno axis cannot both cover the winter peak and clear the summer surplus, which is why LNG is structured as baseload; Brazil stays marginal until the Norte compressor plants and a northern trunk exist. TGS's separate US$3bn NGL project (Tratayén to 43 MMm3/d of full processing, a 100 km liquids line, RIGI-approved, start-up about 2030) removes a liquids ceiling that would otherwise cap wet-gas and oil growth.

## Vaca Muerta: Geology and Operations

**Vaca Muerta underlies four provinces, but Neuquén is the play.** The formation is a Late Jurassic to Early Cretaceous marine shale of the Neuquén Basin covering about 30,000 km2 (7.4 million acres), split 55% Neuquén, 24% Mendoza, 14% Río Negro and 7% La Pampa; newer mapping suggests up to 10% more productive surface ([Wikipedia ES](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yacimiento_petrol%C3%ADfero_Vaca_Muerta)). The developed core sits around Añelo north-west of Neuquén city, with crude leaving east via Allen toward Bahía Blanca, from 2027 south-east via Punta Colorada, and west to Chile via OTASA. Only about 2.7% of the oil resource has been developed, so the question is pace, not inventory.

**The resource is world-scale and Argentine re-estimates nearly doubled the oil number.** The EIA/ARI 2013 assessment put technically recoverable resources at 308 TCF of gas and 16.2 billion bbl of oil, second and fourth globally. A 2025 IAPG/Secretaría de Energía study lifted oil to 30.7 billion bbl (an August 2026 report cites 30.17 billion, +89%) and gas to 255-258 TCF, while Rystad counts about 9 billion bbl with breakevens below US$55/bbl ([Noticias NQN, 13 Aug 2026](https://www.noticiasnqn.com.ar/noticias/2026/08/13/347938-estiman-que-vaca-muerta-tiene-30170-millones-de-barriles-de-petroleo-tecnicamente-recuperables)). Argentina's proved oil rose 29.4% and proved gas 64.4% over the decade, almost all from this basin, which holds 81% of the country's proved gas.

**Maturity windows run from black oil in the east to dry gas westward.** Maturity rises toward the western depocenter, so the shallower eastern flank is black oil (Loma Campana, La Amarga Chica, Bandurria Sur, Bajada del Palo Oeste, Rincón de Aranda, Bajo del Choique), the centre is condensate and wet gas (La Calera, parts of Loma Campana, Aguada Pichana Este) and the west and south-west are dry gas (Fortín de Piedra, Aguada Pichana Oeste, El Mangrullo). Operators distinguish a southern oil core from a northern hub (El Trapial, Bajo del Choique-La Invernada, Los Toldos, Narambuena, Bajo del Toro, La Escalonada) with strong rock but 30% higher well costs. Four blocks produce about 60% of the basin's oil, and the oil boom has pulled rigs from the gas window: 39 dry-gas wells in 2025 versus 56 in 2024 ([LM Neuquén](https://mase.lmneuquen.com/gas/el-boom-del-petroleo-vaca-muerta-arrincona-al-gas-seco-n1230625)).

**Drilling began in 2010, went horizontal in 2016 and is at record pace.** Repsol-YPF tested the first shale-gas well at Loma La Lata in 2010 and the 2013 YPF-Chevron JV at Loma Campana (US$1.5bn, 132 wells) started factory development; wells have been almost exclusively horizontal since 2016. Secretaría de Energía records show 3,337-3,370 wells drilled by 31 July 2026, 2,835 producing, and the play passed 100,000 cumulative frac stages around end-2025. September 2026 saw a record 43 unconventional rigs (56 nationally), 383 wells in January-September, about 540 projected for 2026 and 50 rigs for 2027 ([LM Neuquén, Sep 2026](https://mase.lmneuquen.com/vaca-muerta/vaca-muerta-hay-43-equipos-perforacion-activos-y-proyectan-llegar-50-2027-n1259055)). Frac stages hit 2,760 in June and 24,028 in January-October, with 28-30,000 expected for 2026 and 39,000 for 2027. The 2026 bottleneck flipped to drilling and logistics: 15 frac sets were active in July but only eight ran continuously, utilisation fell to about 50%, and more than 1,000 frac-ready stages were lost in June.

**The target is a thick, overpressured, organic-rich marl at 2,500-3,500 m.** Vaca Muerta sits on the fluvial Tordillo sandstones and beneath the Quintuco carbonates, which pass up into the Mulichinco and then the Agrio shale (a secondary source rock, 2-5% TOC); the deeper Los Molles shale and Lajas tight sandstones of the Cuyo Group are separate plays our notes did not resolve. Thickness runs from 30 m at the basin edge to more than 1,000 m near the depocenter, the organic-rich section is up to 500 m and the >2% TOC interval up to 270 m; TOC averages 1-8% with peaks above 12%, highest at the basal condensed section, at 2,500-3,500 m (8,200-11,500 ft) ([SLB Oilfield Review](https://www.slb.com/-/media/files/oilfield-review/04-vaca-muerta-shale-spanish)). AAPG Memoir 121 divides the organic section into 12 units of 2%-plus TOC, the lowermost eight (the "Cocina" and "Orgánico" intervals) being the main landing zones, two to five per block; the formation is overpressured in several compartments, which drives casing design and frac intensity. Depth increases westward, consistent with Tecpetrol's 6,579 m measured-depth record at Fortín de Piedra and YPF's 8,376 m Loma Campana well with a 5,114 m lateral.

**A 2026 well is a 3,300 m lateral, 55 stages, 12,500 t of sand.** Average laterals are 3,300-3,500 m (10,800-11,500 ft) with about six wells beyond 4,500 m. The record is YPF's 5,114 m lateral at Loma Campana (May 2025) and the slim-hole record a 4,770 m lateral in a single run (October 2026); a claimed 5,751 m lateral should be verified. Completions run about 55 stages at 200-250 t of sand each, 11,000-15,000 t per well (3,300-3,900 kg/m), and drilling takes about 15 days. Vista's well cost has fallen from US$14.2m to US$12.8m, others quote US$8.5-14m with facilities, northern-fringe wells cost US$20m because of haulage, and Chevron says a well costs 30% more than in the Permian, partly offset by productivity ([Reuters, 23 Oct 2026](https://money.usnews.com/investing/news/articles/2026-10-23/exclusive-chevron-uses-permian-playbook-to-breathe-life-into-argentinas-dead-cow-oil-region)). Sand is the swing cost: US$22/t at the mine becomes US$145/t at the wellhead versus US$60/t delivered in the Permian, a US$1.0-1.1m per-well penalty.

**Productivity beats the Permian per foot, and output is at records.** Rystad puts one-year cumulative oil at about 30 bbl per lateral foot versus 15 in the Delaware and 23 in the Midland; Enverus expects EURs near 110 bbl/ft, roughly 1.2 million bbl on a 3,300 m lateral ([Rystad Energy](https://www.rystadenergy.com/insights/argentina-s-unconventional-wealth-key-to-its-hydrocarbon-prosperity)). Fifteen wells have passed 1 million bbl, Vista's BPO-2801(h) in under 12 months, and YPF/Pampa's RDM-553(h) gas well made 1.3 MMm3/d in June 2026; Vista's 1,653-well inventory breaks even below US$45/bbl at a US$4.7/boe lifting cost. National oil averaged 708 kb/d in 2024, about 800 kb/d in 2025 and 906 kb/d in January-September 2026, reaching 973.7 kb/d in September with Vaca Muerta at 698.5 kb/d and Neuquén at 714 kb/d; gas averaged 147.7 MMm3/d with shale about 86 MMm3/d (60%) but grows only 1-3% a year because transport binds ([Infobae/EFE, 20 Oct 2026](https://www.infobae.com/america/agencias/2026/10/20/argentina-logra-record-de-produccion-de-petroleo-en-septiembre-gracias-a-vaca-muerta/)). Rystad and Wood Mackenzie see Vaca Muerta shale oil near 1 mb/d by 2030, gas above 7 bcf/d after 2030 and US$100bn-plus of spend this decade; crude became Argentina's top export in H1 2026 (US$4.7bn), and the 2026 energy surplus is forecast at US$10-14.4bn.

## The Players

**YPF is half the basin and sets its tempo.** The state-controlled major operated 319.7 kb/d of Neuquén's 629 kb/d in April 2026, owns four of the five most productive blocks, produced 224 kb/d of net shale oil in Q3 2026 (+32% y/y, 86% of its oil) and guides a 255-260 kb/d December exit. It runs 18 rigs (22 by February 2027), awarded Halliburton a five-year "multibillion-dollar" completions contract with the first ZEUS electric fleet outside the US, leads VMOS (29.8%), Oldelval (37%) and Argentina LNG (36%), holds 25% of Southern Energy, and plans to file five northern-hub projects worth US$44bn ([Ámbito, Oct 2026](https://www.ambito.com/energia/ypf-gano-cerca-us1200-millones-el-tercer-trimestre-y-redujo-su-endeudamiento-al-menor-nivel-una-decada-n6339067)). Its 2026 capex of US$5.8-6.2bn is 70% shale, inside a US$29bn programme to 2031 of which US$24bn is LNG infrastructure.

**The second tier is Vista, Chevron and a cluster of 25-35 kb/d producers.** Vista (169 kboe/d, 147 kb/d of oil, 185 kboe/d targeted for 2027) is the pure-play consolidator after buying Petronas's half of La Amarga Chica and Equinor's Bandurria Sur and Bajo del Toro stakes. Chevron is about 95 kboe/d net on our arithmetic (El Trapial ~40 kboe/d plus half of Loma Campana's ~110 kboe/d gross), then Shell (33 kb/d operated), PAE (30 kb/d rising to 37-38 kb/d, 111 kb/d with non-operated barrels), Pluspetrol (26 kb/d and quadrupling), Phoenix (25-26 kb/d) and Pampa's Rincón de Aranda (21 kb/d). On gas, Tecpetrol's Fortín de Piedra (24 MMm3/d winter peaks), TotalEnergies' Aguada Pichana Este and PAE's Aguada Pichana Oeste (9 MMm3/d) dominate. Operators told the Energy Secretariat they would invest about US$13.9bn in 2026, up 13.5% ([El Economista, 2026](https://eleconomista.com.ar/energia/con-vaca-muerta-como-motor-petroleras-preven-invertir-casi-us-13900-millones-2026-n95916)).

**The Techint group touches every link of the chain.** Tecpetrol operates the largest gas block and is pivoting to oil at Los Toldos II Este (US$919m of 2026 spend, a US$6.4bn RIGI, a 70 kb/d target for 2027 that looks aggressive against about 6 kb/d by December, and 100-130 kb/d by 2030-32), holds 7.3% of VMOS and partners Pampa at Rincón de Aranda; Tenaris supplies OCTG from Campana and runs three frac sets (the third, US$110m and 70,000 HHP, started in August 2026); Techint E&C built VMOS and is building Duplicar Norte; and TGN, controlled by Rocca and Eurnekian, promotes the US$2.2bn Belgrano pipeline.

**Majors are selling, locals are buying, and two majors are doubling down.** Wood Mackenzie counts 18 major deals in two years: ExxonMobil to Pluspetrol (US$1.7bn, December 2024, with its Oldelval stake) and to YPF; Petronas's half of La Amarga Chica to Vista (US$1.5bn headline, April 2025); Equinor's Bandurria Sur and Bajo del Toro stakes to Vista (US$712m plus adjustments, May 2026); TotalEnergies' 45% of two blocks to YPF (US$500m, about US$10,000 per acre, August 2025); and Shell reported in January 2026 to be sounding buyers for a portfolio Rystad values at up to US$3bn, which CEO Wael Sawan called "fake news" ([Wood Mackenzie, Mar 2026](https://www.woodmac.com/press-releases/vaca-muerta-requires-us$22-billion-and-1000-new-wells-to-meet-export-targets/)). New money is arriving: Continental Resources agreed in August 2026 to take 50% of Phoenix from Mercuria for a platform spending more than US$4bn over five years, and GyP's Ronda 1/2026 awarded eight blocks for US$180.7m to Integra, Central Puerto, PCR and GeoPark. Against that, Chevron filed a US$13.8bn, 30-year RIGI for El Trapial and aims to triple Argentine output to 195 kboe/d by 2035, TotalEnergies pledged nearly US$10bn on 1 October 2026 (about US$5bn for Aguada Pichana and San Roque).
