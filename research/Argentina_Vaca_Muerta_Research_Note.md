# Argentina Oil & Gas: Vaca Muerta Research Note

As of 6 November 2026. Prepared ahead of the 16-20 November 2026 investor trip to Buenos Aires and Neuquén. Shared editable version: https://claude.ai/artifact/VDYJ6Qp1BV5a5iuT5fw6Fa

## Introduction

Argentina is the one shale play outside North America that is actually delivering. Oil output hit a record 974 kb/d in September 2026, 72% of it from Vaca Muerta, and should cross 1 mb/d around year-end. The constraint has been pipe, not rock, and it breaks in 2027: the US$3bn VMOS oil line (first cargo around March 2027, 550 kb/d by end-2027), TGS's +14 MMm3/d expansion of the Perito Moreno gas line (April 2027), the first of two Golar FLNG units (end-2027) and the 12 mtpa YPF/Eni/XRG Argentina LNG FID due 26 November, six days after this trip. Milei's RIGI locks in a 25% tax rate and FX freedom for 30 years and dividends on post-2024 profits can leave the country again, but shale crude still pays an 8% export duty. The rock is Permian-plus, YPF is half the basin, majors are selling to locals while Chevron and TotalEnergies scale up, and the risk is macro: riesgo país at 925 bp, flat GDP and a 2027 dead heat between Milei and Axel Kicillof.

## Key Points

- Oil: record 973.7 kb/d in September 2026 (+15% y/y), Vaca Muerta 698.5 kb/d; 43 rigs and ~540 wells in 2026.
- Oil takeaway: ~650 kb/d firm and full since July 2026. VMOS adds 180 kb/d from Q1 2027, rising to 550 kb/d by end-2027.
- Gas takeaway: 21 MMm3/d on the Perito Moreno line, 35 from April 2027; Southern Energy FLNG 5.95 mtpa in 2027-28; Argentina LNG 12 mtpa FID on 26 November, exports 2030-31.
- RIGI: 25 projects and US$68bn approved; 25% tax, duty-free exports from year 3, 30-year stability; window open to July 2027.
- Shale crude pays an 8% export duty; dividends on fiscal-2025-and-later profits are remittable, and ~US$4bn went abroad in Jan-Sep 2026.
- A 2026 well: 3,300 m lateral, ~55 stages, 12,500 t of sand, US$12.8-14m; 30 bbl per lateral foot in year one versus 15-23 in the Permian.
- YPF produces half the basin (shale oil 224 kb/d, 2026 capex US$5.8-6.2bn); Exxon, Petronas and Equinor have sold out; Chevron filed a US$13.8bn RIGI.
- Macro: riesgo país 925 bp, 2026 GDP +0.7%, reserves ~US$50bn; polls tie Milei and Kicillof for 2027.

## Fiscal Regime

**RIGI is doing the heavy lifting, almost all of it for oil and gas.** An adhered project pays 25% income tax instead of 35%, imports capital goods duty-free, exports duty-free from year three, keeps 20%, 40% and then 100% of export proceeds offshore on a three-year ladder and can pay dividends abroad up to the capital brought in, all locked for 30 years. By October 2026, 25 projects worth US$68bn were approved, including VMOS (US$2.9bn), Southern Energy FLNG (US$6.9bn), Pampa's Rincón de Aranda (US$4.5bn), Pluspetrol's Bajo del Choique (US$12.2bn), Tecpetrol's Los Toldos (US$6.4bn) and TGS's pipeline and NGL projects (US$3.8bn); the US$51bn Argentina LNG and US$13.8bn Chevron El Trapial filings are pending ([La Nación, 19 Oct 2026](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/economia/vaca-muerta-suma-tres-proyectos-al-rigi-por-mas-de-us15400-millones-para-exportar-petroleo-y-gas-nid19102026/)). The adhesion window was extended to 8 July 2027.

**Outside RIGI, Milei freed prices and exports but kept an 8% duty on shale crude.** Ley Bases (2024) ended state-set crude prices (the "barril criollo") and freed exports. Decreto 59/2026 put conventional crude on a 0-8% Brent-linked export duty, but Vaca Muerta crude stays at a flat 8%. Plan Gas.Ar subsidies end in December 2028, Neuquén royalties are 12% on legacy blocks and 15-18% on new tenders (7.5% for LNG gas), and the corporate tax rate stays at 35% after the Senate stripped the cut from the March 2026 labour reform.

**Cash can leave again, but only profits earned since 2025.** BCRA Com. A 8226 (April 2025) lets companies remit dividends on fiscal 2025 and later earnings; pre-2025 retained earnings remain trapped and were offered a 3% BCRA bond (BOPREAL) maturing 2028. Intercompany loans need a 180-day minimum term and the 90-day cross-restriction on parallel FX markets still applies to companies. Remittances ran a record US$1.9bn in Q1 2026 and about US$4bn in January-September ([Diario Río Negro, Oct 2026](https://www.rionegro.com.ar/economia/giro-de-dividendos-las-empresas-enviaron-cerca-de-us4-000-millones-al-exterior-en-2026-segun-el-bcra-4749203/)). Macro backdrop: the peso trades 27% inside its band ceiling, the BCRA has bought US$16bn in 2026, inflation is 33% y/y, 2026 GDP is tracking +0.7% and the IMF's third review clears in December.

## Midstream, Pipelines and LNG

**Oil: about 650 kb/d of firm takeaway today, and it is full.** Oldelval's Allen-Puerto Rosales trunk (YPF 37%, Pluspetrol ~33%, Chevron 14%, PAE 12%, Tecpetrol 2%, Pampa 2%) was expanded to 540 kb/d in April 2025 and saturated in July 2026; drag reducers stretch it past 570 kb/d. OTASA to Chile (ENAP 36%, YPF 36%, Chevron 28%) adds 110 kb/d, Puerto Rosales loads 380 kb/d, and trucks carry the rest at up to six times pipeline cost.

**Being built: VMOS and Duplicar Norte.** VMOS runs 437 km from Allen to a VLCC terminal at Punta Colorada: US$3bn, a US$2bn syndicated loan, RIGI-approved, 85% built, line-fill in January 2027, first cargo around March 2027, 180 kb/d rising to 550 kb/d by end-2027 and 700-770 kb/d with pumps from 2028 ([DataPortuaria, Oct 2026](https://dataportuaria.com/es/argentina/ambiente-y-energia/la-terminal-de-punta-colorada-del-vmos-alcanza-un-85-de-avance-y-preve-su-primera-exportacion-de-petroleo-en-el-primer-trimestre-de-2027)). Stakes track firm capacity: YPF 29.8%, Pluspetrol 14.6%, Chevron 12%, PAE 10%, Pampa 9.1%, Vista 9.1%, Shell 7.3%, Tecpetrol 7.3%, GyP 0.9% (only Pampa's is independently corroborated). Oldelval's Duplicar Norte (207 km, US$400m, 220 kb/d in December 2026 rising to 500 kb/d in March 2027) opens the northern hub but adds no export capacity.

**Still needed: another US$5bn-plus to reach 1.5 mb/d by 2030.** Firm capacity goes from 650 kb/d today to 1.2-1.3 mb/d once VMOS reaches 550 kb/d and 1.35-1.5 mb/d with its pump expansion; beyond that the basin needs a second northern line or Medanito repowering, a third berth at Puerto Rosales and more OTASA pumping. Refining is capped near 550 kb/d and no refinery is planned, so every new barrel is an export.

**Gas: 21 MMm3/d today, 35 from April 2027, and LNG from 2027-28.** The state-owned, TGS-operated Perito Moreno pipeline carries 21 MMm3/d (0.74 bcf/d); TGS's RIGI-backed expansion (US$550m per the resolution, US$700-800m per press) adds 14 MMm3/d on 1 April 2027 and was 11x oversubscribed ([Shale24, Aug 2026](https://www.shale24.com/oil-gas/ampliacion-gasoducto-perito-moreno-tgs-recibio-ofertas-volumen-once-veces-mas-alto-capacidad-ociosa-transporte-resta-licitar-n1618)). Argentina still imports about 23 LNG cargoes a winter because demand swings 50 MMm3/d between seasons and there is no storage. Southern Energy (PAE 30%, YPF 25%, Pampa 20%, Harbour 15%, Golar 10%) has sanctioned two Golar FLNGs, Hilli (2.45 mtpa, end-2027) and MK II Esperanza (3.5 mtpa, 2028), fed by the 471 km, US$1.3bn San Matías pipeline (mid-2028). Argentina LNG (YPF 36%, Eni 32%, XRG 32%) is two 6 mtpa floating units and a 48-inch pipeline, about US$24bn for phase one; Shell left in December 2025 and YPF says the FID signs in Buenos Aires on 26 November 2026 for exports in 2030-31. TGN's 753 km Belgrano line north (13 MMm3/d, 2029) is pre-FID. Gas needs about 70 MMm3/d of new outlets to clear the surplus.

## Vaca Muerta: Geology and Operations

**Where and how big: 30,000 km2 across four provinces, 3% developed.** Vaca Muerta is a Late Jurassic marine shale of the Neuquén Basin covering about 30,000 km2 (7.4 million acres), 55% in Neuquén and the rest in Mendoza, Río Negro and La Pampa, centred on Añelo about 100 km north-west of Neuquén city. The EIA's 2013 estimate was 308 TCF of gas and 16.2 billion bbl of oil (second and fourth largest in the world); a 2025 Argentine re-estimate lifted oil to 30.7 billion bbl, and only about 2.7% has been developed ([Noticias NQN, Aug 2026](https://www.noticiasnqn.com.ar/noticias/2026/08/13/347938-estiman-que-vaca-muerta-tiene-30170-millones-de-barriles-de-petroleo-tecnicamente-recuperables)).

**It is in sections: black oil east, condensate centre, dry gas west.** Maturity rises toward the western depocentre, so the eastern flank is black oil (Loma Campana, La Amarga Chica, Bandurria Sur, Bajada del Palo Oeste, Rincón de Aranda), the centre is condensate (La Calera, Aguada Pichana Este) and the west is dry gas (Fortín de Piedra, Aguada Pichana Oeste, El Mangrullo). Operators also split a southern oil core, where almost all drilling has been, from a northern hub (El Trapial, Bajo del Choique, Los Toldos, Narambuena) with good rock but 30% higher well costs. Four blocks produce 60% of the basin's oil.

**Formations and depth: a 2,500-3,500 m overpressured marl with up to eight landing zones.** Vaca Muerta sits on the Tordillo sandstones and under the Quintuco carbonates, Mulichinco and the Agrio shale (a secondary source rock); the deeper Los Molles shale and Lajas tight sands are separate, lightly drilled plays. It is 30 m thick at the basin edge and more than 1,000 m near the depocentre, with 1-8% TOC peaking at the base, at 2,500-3,500 m (8,200-11,500 ft) and deeper westward. The section has 12 organic-rich units; the lowest eight ("La Cocina" and "Orgánico") are the landing zones, with two to five used per block ([SLB Oilfield Review](https://www.slb.com/-/media/files/oilfield-review/04-vaca-muerta-shale-spanish)).

**Drilling so far: 3,300 wells since 2010, 43 rigs today.** YPF drilled the first shale well at Loma La Lata in 2010, the 2013 YPF-Chevron JV at Loma Campana started factory development, and wells have been almost all horizontal since 2016. About 3,340 wells had been drilled by July 2026, the play has passed 100,000 frac stages, and September 2026 set records of 43 unconventional rigs and 2,400-2,760 stages a month ([LM Neuquén, Sep 2026](https://mase.lmneuquen.com/vaca-muerta/vaca-muerta-hay-43-equipos-perforacion-activos-y-proyectan-llegar-50-2027-n1259055)).

**The typical well: 3,300 m lateral, 55 stages, 12,500 t of sand, about US$13m.** Laterals average 3,300-3,500 m (10,800-11,500 ft), with YPF's record at 5,114 m; completions run about 55 stages at 200-250 t of sand each; drilling takes about 15 days; wells are drilled from pads in stacked "cubes" across two to five landing zones. Costs run from US$12.8m (Vista) to US$14m in the core and US$20m on the northern fringe, 30% above the Permian on Chevron's numbers, largely because sand costs US$145/t delivered versus US$60/t in Texas. Year-one output is about 30 bbl per lateral foot versus 15-23 in the Permian, EURs run near 1.2 million bbl per well, and 15 wells have passed 1 million bbl ([Rystad Energy](https://www.rystadenergy.com/insights/argentina-s-unconventional-wealth-key-to-its-hydrocarbon-prosperity)).

## The Players

**YPF is half the basin; majors are selling to locals while Chevron and TotalEnergies double down.** YPF operated 320 kb/d of Neuquén's 629 kb/d in April 2026, followed by Vista (169 kboe/d), Chevron (~95 kboe/d net), Shell (33 kb/d), PAE (30 kb/d), Pluspetrol (26 kb/d), Phoenix (25 kb/d) and Pampa (21 kb/d); Tecpetrol, TotalEnergies and PAE dominate gas. In two years ExxonMobil sold to Pluspetrol (US$1.7bn), Petronas and Equinor sold to Vista (US$1.5bn and US$0.7bn), Shell is reported to be sounding buyers, and Continental Resources is buying half of Phoenix. Against that, Chevron filed a US$13.8bn 30-year RIGI and TotalEnergies pledged US$10bn. Operators plan about US$13.9bn of capex in 2026 ([Wood Mackenzie, Mar 2026](https://www.woodmac.com/press-releases/vaca-muerta-requires-us$22-billion-and-1000-new-wells-to-meet-export-targets/)).
