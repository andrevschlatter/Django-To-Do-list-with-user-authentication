# Argentina Trip Brief: Who We Are Meeting

Internal prep for the November 16-20, 2026 trip. Not for publication.

| Who | What they actually do in Argentina | Argentine production | Spending |
|---|---|---|---|
| Poliarquía (Alejandro Catterberg) | Argentina's best-known political pollster; Catterberg is its president. The meeting for a read on the 2027 election. Its September poll had Milei's approval stuck at 42%. | | |
| Harbour Energy | A non-operating gas partner, through assets bought from Wintershall Dea. Most of its production is offshore Tierra del Fuego, not Vaca Muerta. Its shale exposure is minority stakes in two TotalEnergies-operated blocks plus 15% of Southern Energy LNG. | 74 kboe/d (Jan-Sep 2026) | Not disclosed for Argentina |
| Pampa Energía | A power and gas company building a shale oil business at its wholly owned Rincón de Aranda block, which it aims to double to 45 kb/d by 2028. Holds 20% of Southern Energy LNG and 9% of VMOS, and co-controls TGS. | 21 kb/d of oil at Rincón de Aranda (Q3 2026) | US$585m in Q3 company-wide, US$158m of it at Rincón de Aranda |
| Economy Ministry and Energy Secretariat | The officials who set energy policy and approve RIGI projects. The senior energy coordinator left on November 1 and the post was abolished, so the Energy Secretary now answers to Economy Minister Luis Caputo. | | |
| Tenaris | Makes the steel pipe that lines wells, at its Campana mill, and has built the basin's third-largest fracking business. Part of the Techint group. | | US$110m on its newest frac fleet |
| Chevron | YPF's original shale partner at Loma Campana and sole owner of El Trapial. Wants to triple Argentine output to 195 kboe/d by 2035. Shareholder in VMOS, Oldelval and the Chile pipeline. | 65 kboe/d net (2025) | US$13.8bn over 30 years, pending RIGI approval |
| Vista Energy | A pure Vaca Muerta oil company founded by former YPF chief Miguel Galuccio. Grew by buying out Petronas and Equinor, and exports about three-quarters of its oil. | 169 kboe/d (Q3 2026) | US$460m in Q3 |
| YPF | The national oil company, 51% state-owned. Operates about half the basin's oil, leads the pipelines and Argentina LNG, and is shedding conventional fields to become a shale company. | 532 kboe/d, including 224 kb/d of shale oil (Q3 2026) | US$5.8-6.2bn in 2026 |
| Bill Von Gonten | Houston reservoir engineer whose firm has tested Vaca Muerta rock for more than a decade, including for Vista. An independent technical view on well quality. | | |
| TGS | Argentina's largest gas pipeline company. Operates the Perito Moreno line, is expanding it at its own cost and is building a US$3bn gas liquids project for 2030. Co-controlled by Pampa. | | More than US$500m in 2026 |
| Neuquén Province (Gustavo Medele) | The landlord. The province owns the resource, grants the licenses and collects royalties, which fund almost half its budget. | 714 kb/d of oil province-wide (Sep 2026) | |
| Halliburton | The basin's largest fracking contractor, with about 60% of stages in October, and YPF's main completions provider under a multi-year contract signed in April. Bringing the first electric frac fleet to Argentina. | | |
| Helmerich & Payne (field trip) | US drilling contractor expanding fast. Runs about a quarter of the basin's rigs today and expects 15 working by August 2027, about a third. | | |
